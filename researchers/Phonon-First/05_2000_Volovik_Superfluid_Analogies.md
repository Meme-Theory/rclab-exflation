## Abstract

In a modern viewpoint the relativistic quantum field theory is the emergent phenomenon arising in the low energy corner of the physical fermionic vacuum -- the medium, whose nature remains unknown. The same phenomenon occurs in the condensed matter systems: In the extreme limit of low energy the condensed matter system of special universality class acquires all the symmetries, which we know today in high energy physics: Lorentz invariance, gauge invariance, general covariance, etc. The chiral fermions as well as gauge bosons and gravity field arise as fermionic and bosonic collective modes of the system. The inhomogeneous states of the condensed matter ground state -- vacuum -- induce nontrivial effective metrics of the space, where the free quasiparticles move along geodesics. This conceptual similarity between condensed matter and quantum vacuum allows us to simulate many phenomena in high energy physics and cosmology, including axial anomaly, baryoproduction and magnetogenesis, event horizon and Hawking radiation, cosmological constant and rotating vacuum, etc., probing these phenomena in ultra-low-temperature superfluid helium, atomic Bose condensates and superconductors. Some of the experiments have been already conducted.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### The Anti-Grand-Unification Paradigm (Section I)

Volovik presents two contrasting views of symmetry: (1) the traditional grand unification scheme where higher energy means higher symmetry, broken at low energies; (2) the "anti-grand-unification" scheme where the higher the energy, the poorer the symmetries, and Lorentz invariance, gauge invariance, general covariance are emergent low-energy phenomena arising from a fixed point. Superfluid 3He-A provides an example of both: above $T_c$ it has the usual condensed matter symmetries; below $T_c$ it spontaneously breaks all symmetries; but in the limit $T \to 0$, it gradually acquires (analogues of) Lorentz invariance, local gauge invariance, and elements of general covariance.

The key argument: condensed matter teaches us that low-energy properties of different vacua are robust -- determined by symmetry and topology, not by microscopic details. The microscopic details only provide "fundamental constants" (speed of sound, superfluid density, etc.). Systems in the same universality class behave identically in the infrared. One cannot reconstruct atomic structure from acoustic waves, just as quantizing gravity may not reveal the vacuum's microscopic structure.

### Landau-Khalatnikov Two-Fluid Hydrodynamics as Effective Gravity (Section II)

The superfluid vacuum has collective degrees of freedom $(n, \mathbf{v}_s)$ -- particle density and superfluid velocity. The equations of motion are the continuity equation $\partial_t n + \nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} = 0$ and the London equation $m\partial_t\mathbf{v}_s + \nabla(\delta E/\delta n) = 0$. The energy functional is:
$$E = \int d^3r\left[\frac{m}{2}nv_s^2 + \epsilon(n) - \mu n + \sum_{\mathbf{p}}\tilde{E}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{r})f(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{r})\right]$$

The quadratic Lagrangian for sound waves (fluctuations $\alpha$ of the velocity potential) is:
$$\mathcal{L} = \frac{m}{2}n\left[(\nabla\alpha)^2 - \frac{1}{c^2}(\dot{\alpha} + (\mathbf{v}_s\cdot\nabla)\alpha)^2\right] = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\alpha\,\partial_\nu\alpha$$

The acoustic metric is:
$$g^{00} = -\frac{1}{mnc},\quad g^{0i} = -\frac{v_s^i}{mnc},\quad g^{ij} = \frac{c^2\delta^{ij} - v_s^i v_s^j}{mnc}$$

with $\sqrt{-g} = m^2n^2/c$. The phonon spectrum is $g^{\mu\nu}p_\mu p_\nu = 0$, i.e., $(\tilde{E} - \mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{v}_s)^2 = c^2 p^2$.

### Vacuum Energy and Cosmological Constant (Section II.G)

The vacuum energy density $\tilde{\epsilon}(n) = \epsilon(n) - \mu n$ in equilibrium equals $-P_{\mathrm{vac}}$ (vacuum pressure), analogous to the Einstein cosmological term. For a self-sustaining liquid system (no external pressure required), $P_{\mathrm{vac}} = 0$, so:
$$\tilde{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{vacuum}} \equiv 0$$

This is the key result: the vacuum energy is identically zero for any self-sustaining system, regardless of microscopic details. The effective theory estimate $\epsilon_{\mathrm{eff}} = (1/16\pi^2)\Theta^4/(\hbar^3 c^3)$ (where $\Theta \sim \hbar c/a$ is the Debye cutoff playing the role of Planck energy) gives an answer $\sim 120$ orders of magnitude too large -- confirming that effective field theory cannot predict vacuum energy.

Two proposed resolutions of the CC problem: (1) if the cosmological term is $\Lambda\sqrt{-g}$, then $\Lambda = 0$ in equilibrium at $T=0$; at nonzero $T$, vacuum energy $\sim$ energy density of matter; (2) the cosmological term has form $\Lambda(g - g_0)^2$ with a preferred background metric, so equilibrium vacuum is non-gravitating.

### Universality Classes of Fermionic Vacua (Section IV)

Three universality classes with distinct momentum-space topology:

1. **Fermi surface** (topologically stable as a vortex ring in $(p,\omega)$-space with invariant $N_1$): includes Landau Fermi liquids and non-Landau Fermi liquids.
2. **Fully gapped systems** ("Dirac particles"): includes superconductors, superfluid 3He-B. Gap is topologically trivial.
3. **Fermi point systems** (topologically stable hedgehog in $p$-space with invariant $N_3$): includes superfluid 3He-A and the Standard Model. Near the Fermi point, massless chiral fermions with linear spectrum emerge, together with Lorentz invariance and gauge symmetry.

The Standard Model belongs to class (3): all 16 fermions per generation have Fermi points characterized by topological invariant $N_3 = \pm 1$, with the sign corresponding to chirality. The topological protection explains why these fermions remain massless above the electroweak transition -- their masslessness is protected by topology, not symmetry.

### Effective RQFT from Fermi Points (Section VI)

Near a Fermi point, the quasiparticle spectrum becomes linear and the collective bosonic modes (order parameter fluctuations) acquire the structure of gauge fields and gravitational fields. The effective metric, gauge fields, and chiral fermions emerge simultaneously in the low-energy corner. The running coupling constant (zero charge effect) and SU(N) gauge fields from degeneracy of the Fermi point are discussed.

### Chiral Anomaly and Baryogenesis (Section VII)

The Adler-Bell-Jackiw anomaly equation is derived in the superfluid context. The anomalous non-conservation of baryonic charge in the Standard Model has a direct analogue in 3He-A: momentum exchange between the superfluid vacuum and quasiparticle "matter" via the chiral anomaly. This has been experimentally verified in rotating 3He-A.

### Event Horizons and Hawking Radiation (Section XIII)

Event horizons arise naturally in moving textures (vierbein domain walls). The Painleve-Gullstrand metric appears directly in effective gravity for superfluids. Volovik shows that the vacuum resists horizon formation: the superfluid condensate becomes unstable before the "speed of light" (Landau critical velocity) is reached. Stable horizons require textures (order parameter inhomogeneities) moving faster than the "speed of light" while the condensate itself remains at rest.

The Hawking temperature is $T_H = \hbar\kappa/(2\pi k_B)$ where $\kappa = dc_z/dz|_h$ is the surface gravity. For 3He-A domain walls, $T_H \sim 5$ $\mu$K -- below current experimental reach but within conceivable range.

### Vacuum Energy in 3He (Section X.C)

The cosmological term in 3He is discussed explicitly. The vacuum energy per atom ($\mu \sim -7$ K) is negative (property of a liquid), but the relevant cosmological energy $\tilde{\epsilon} = \epsilon - \mu n = 0$ in equilibrium. At nonzero temperature, $\tilde{\epsilon} \sim T^4$ (the thermal quasiparticle contribution), paralleling the observed CC being of order the matter energy density.

## Key Results

1. Relativistic QFT (Lorentz invariance, gauge invariance, general covariance) is an emergent low-energy phenomenon in condensed matter systems of the Fermi point universality class.
2. The acoustic metric for phonons in superfluids arises naturally from two-fluid hydrodynamics and has the standard Lorentzian form.
3. The vacuum energy of a self-sustaining liquid is exactly zero ($\tilde{\epsilon} = 0$), regardless of microscopic details -- resolving the cosmological constant problem in the analogue framework.
4. The effective field theory estimate of vacuum energy ($\sim E_P^4$) gives the wrong answer by $\sim 120$ orders of magnitude; only trans-Planckian (microscopic) physics determines the true vacuum energy.
5. Three universality classes of fermionic vacua are identified by momentum-space topology: Fermi surface ($N_1$), gapped ($N_2$), Fermi point ($N_3$).
6. The Standard Model fermions are in the Fermi point universality class with topological invariant $N_3 = \pm 1$.
7. The chiral anomaly (ABJ equation) is experimentally verified in rotating superfluid 3He-A.
8. Event horizons in superfluids produce Hawking radiation at $T_H \sim 5$ $\mu$K in 3He-A domain walls.
