# Spectral Dimension of the Universe

**Author(s):** J. Ambjorn, J. Jurkiewicz, R. Loll
**Year:** 2005
**Journal:** Physical Review Letters 95, 171301 (2005)
**arXiv:** hep-th/0505113
**Relevance:** HIGH

## Abstract

We measure the spectral dimension of universes emerging from nonperturbative quantum gravity, defined through state sums of causal triangulated geometries. While four-dimensional on large scales, the quantum universe appears two-dimensional at short distances. We conclude that quantum gravity may be "self-renormalizing" at the Planck scale, by virtue of a mechanism of dynamical dimensional reduction.

---

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### 1. Quantum Gravity as an Ultraviolet Regulator

The paper opens with the hypothesis that the microstructure of spacetime may provide a physical regulator for UV infinities in perturbative QFT. Two categories of approach are distinguished: (1) fundamental discreteness (loop quantum gravity, causal sets) proposing a minimal Planck-scale length, and (2) the CDT alternative, where no minimal length or fundamental discreteness is imposed -- instead, nonperturbative quantum geometry itself provides an effective UV cutoff through dynamical dimensional reduction.

The authors summarize prior CDT results: quantum universes generated by the CDT path integral have macroscopic properties of genuine isotropic, homogeneous four-dimensional worlds, with scaling behavior matching the simplest minisuperspace model of quantum cosmology. The scale factor dynamics $a(\tau)$ solves the minisuperspace equation of motion, producing a "bounce" in Euclidean signature.

### 2. The Spectral Dimension -- Definition

Diffusion on a $d$-dimensional Euclidean geometry with fixed smooth metric $g_{ab}(\xi)$ is governed by:

$$\frac{\partial}{\partial \sigma} K_g(\xi, \xi_0; \sigma) = \Delta_g K_g(\xi, \xi_0; \sigma)$$

with initial condition $K_g(\xi, \xi_0; \sigma=0) = \delta^d(\xi - \xi_0) / \sqrt{\det g(\xi)}$, where $\sigma$ is fictitious diffusion time and $\Delta_g$ is the Laplace operator.

The average return probability is:

$$P_g(\sigma) := \frac{1}{V} \int d^d\xi \sqrt{\det g(\xi)} \, K_g(\xi, \xi; \sigma)$$

For infinite flat space, $K_g = e^{-d_g^2(\xi,\xi_0)/4\sigma} / (4\pi\sigma)^{d/2}$, giving $P_g(\sigma) = 1/\sigma^{d/2}$. The dimension is extracted via:

$$-2 \frac{d \log P_g(\sigma)}{d \log \sigma} = d$$

For curved/finite-volume spaces, this still works but with corrections at large $\sigma$. For finite volume, $P_g(\sigma) \to 1$ when $\sigma \gg V^{2/d}$ (zero mode of Laplacian dominates). Large $\sigma$ probes large-scale structure; small $\sigma$ probes short distances.

### 3. Quantum Average and Fractal Generalization

The quantum average of the return probability over all equivalence classes of metrics with fixed spacetime volume $V$ is:

$$P_V(\sigma) = \frac{1}{Z_V} \int \mathcal{D}[g_{ab}] \, e^{-S_E(g_{ab})} \, \delta\!\left(\int d^4\xi \sqrt{\det g} - V\right) P_g(\sigma)$$

For fractal structures (as expected in the continuum limit of the quantum ensemble), the return probability takes the form:

$$P_N(\sigma) = \sigma^{-D_S/2} \, F\!\left(\frac{\sigma}{N^{2/D_S}}\right)$$

where $N$ is the discrete volume and $D_S$ is the spectral dimension (not necessarily integer). The spectral dimension is extracted as:

$$D_S(\sigma) = -2 \frac{d \log P_N(\sigma)}{d \log \sigma}$$

### 4. CDT Setup and Measurements

In CDT, quantum gravity is defined as the continuum limit of a regularized nonperturbative gravitational path integral. Spacetime geometries are represented by causal four-dimensional piecewise flat manifolds ("triangulations"). The path integral becomes a partition function:

$$Z = \sum_T \frac{1}{C_T} e^{-S_E(T)}$$

where $C_T$ is a symmetry factor and $S_E(T)$ the Euclidean Einstein-Regge action. All geometries share a global discrete proper time. The continuum limit is extracted via Monte Carlo simulations and finite-size scaling.

Measurements were performed in the phase generating quantum geometry extended in both space and time with four-dimensional scaling. The quantum universe is a "bounce" in Euclidean signature: three-volume starts minimal, increases to a maximum, then decreases symmetrically. Diffusion always started from a simplex at the maximal constant-time slice. Simulations used proper-time extension $t = 80$, volume up to $N \approx 181{,}000$ four-simplices, and diffusion followed for up to $\sigma_{\max} = 400$ time steps.

### 5. The Discovery: Scale-Dependent Spectral Dimension

The central result (Fig. 1 in the paper) shows $D_S(\sigma)$ monotonically increasing from $\sim 2$ at small $\sigma$ toward $\sim 4$ at large $\sigma$. This is qualitatively different from all previous systems (2D quantum gravity, spatial hypermanifolds in 4D CDT), where $D_S(\sigma)$ stabilizes as a horizontal line after lattice artifacts at small $\sigma$.

The best fit with three free parameters is:

$$D_S(\sigma) = a - \frac{b}{\sigma + c}$$

with numerical values:

$$D_S(\sigma) = 4.02 - \frac{119}{54 + \sigma}$$

This implies the return probability:

$$P(\sigma) \sim \frac{1}{\sigma^{a/2} (1 + c/\sigma)^{b/2c}}$$

with asymptotic behavior:

$$P(\sigma) \sim \begin{cases} \sigma^{-a/2} & \text{for large } \sigma \\ \sigma^{-(a-b/c)/2} & \text{for small } \sigma \end{cases}$$

The quantum geometry does NOT have a self-similar (fractal) structure at all distances, but instead has a scale-dependent spectral dimension that increases continuously from $a - b/c$ to $a$ with increasing distance.

### 6. Numerical Results

Taking into account variation across different fitting ranges $[\sigma_{\min}, \sigma_{\max}]$ and error weightings:

- **Large-scale spectral dimension**: $D_S(\sigma = \infty) = 4.02 \pm 0.1$ -- compatible with four.
- **Short-distance spectral dimension**: $D_S(\sigma = 0) = 1.80 \pm 0.25$ -- compatible with two.

### 7. Interpretation and Continuum Version

Since $\sigma$ from the diffusion equation has length dimension two, and the short-distance behavior should be governed by $G_N$, the authors propose the continuum form:

$$P_V(\sigma) \sim \frac{1}{\sigma^2} \frac{1}{1 + \text{const.} \cdot G_N / \sigma}$$

This describes a universe whose spectral dimension is four on scales large compared to the Planck scale. Below the Planck scale, quantum-gravitational excitations of geometry lead to nonperturbative dynamical dimensional reduction to two -- a dimensionality where gravity is known to be renormalizable. The paper conjectures this provides an effective short-distance cutoff by which CDT evades the UV infinities of perturbative quantum gravity, without appealing to short-scale discreteness or abandoning geometric concepts.

## Key Results

1. First dynamical derivation of scale-dependent spectral dimension in full nonperturbative quantum gravity.
2. Large-scale spectral dimension: $D_S(\sigma \to \infty) = 4.02 \pm 0.1$, compatible with four.
3. Short-distance spectral dimension: $D_S(\sigma \to 0) = 1.80 \pm 0.25$, compatible with two.
4. The flow is smooth and continuous, well-fit by $D_S(\sigma) = a - b/(\sigma + c)$.
5. The quantum geometry is NOT self-similar at all distances -- it has genuinely scale-dependent dimensionality.
6. Dimensional reduction to $d \approx 2$ provides an effective UV cutoff without fundamental discreteness or minimal length.
7. In 2 dimensions, gravity is renormalizable -- suggesting nonperturbative quantum gravity is "self-renormalizing."
8. Measurements used $\sim 181{,}000$ four-simplices with 400 diffusion steps, averaged over 400 independent configurations.
