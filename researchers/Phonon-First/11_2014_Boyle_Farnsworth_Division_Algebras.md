**Relevance:** HIGH

## Abstract

Connes' notion of non-commutative geometry (NCG) generalizes Riemannian geometry and yields a striking reinterpretation of the standard model of particle physics, coupled to Einstein gravity. We suggest a simple reformulation with two key mathematical advantages: (i) it unifies many of the traditional NCG axioms into a single one; and (ii) it immediately generalizes from non-commutative to non-associative geometry. Remarkably, it also resolves a long-standing problem plaguing the NCG construction of the standard model, by precisely eliminating from the action the collection of 7 unwanted terms that previously had to be removed by an extra, non-geometric, assumption. With this problem solved, the NCG algorithm for constructing the standard model action is tighter and more explanatory than the traditional one based on effective field theory.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### Introduction and Motivation

The paper proposes a reformulation of Connes' NCG framework that has both mathematical and physical advantages. In the traditional NCG formalism, a geometry is described by a real spectral triple $\{A, H, D, J, \gamma\}$. The core observation is that these elements naturally fuse to form a new algebra $B$, and that many traditional NCG axioms can be recovered by the single requirement that $B$ is an associative $*$-algebra.

The key physical payoff is resolving a longstanding problem in the NCG construction of the SM action functional: the presence of 7 unwanted terms in the Dirac operator that previously required an ad hoc "massless photon" assumption to remove.

The authors emphasize that in the NCG construction, one needs only two inputs: (i') the choice of algebra $A$ (determining gauge symmetry) and (ii') the representation of $A$ (determining fermions). The scalar sector (Higgs bosons and their representations) is a *predicted output*, unlike in the effective field theory (EFT) construction which requires three independent inputs.

### Step 1: Promoting $A \to \Omega A$

Let $A$ be a unital $*$-algebra over a field $F$ (possibly non-commutative or non-associative). For each element $a \in A$, introduce a formal symbol $\delta[a]$. The algebra $\Omega A$ is generated by $A$ and these differentials $\delta[a]$, modulo the relations:
- $\delta[fa] = f\delta[a]$
- $\delta[a + a'] = \delta[a] + \delta[a']$
- $\delta[aa'] = \delta[a]a' + a\delta[a']$ (Leibniz rule)

plus appropriate associativity relations. One can write $\Omega A = \Omega^0 A \oplus \Omega^1 A \oplus \Omega^2 A \oplus \ldots$ where $\Omega^m A$ consists of terms with $m$ differentials. In particular $\Omega^0 A = A$. The algebra $\Omega A$ is graded: $\omega_m \omega_n \in \Omega^{m+n} A$.

Defining $\delta[a^*] = -\delta[a]^*$ makes $\Omega A$ a $*$-algebra. The map $\delta$ can be promoted to $d: \Omega^m A \to \Omega^{m+1} A$ satisfying the graded Leibniz rule $d[\omega_m \omega_n] = d[\omega_m]\omega_n + (-1)^m \omega_m d[\omega_n]$, with $d^2[\omega] = 0$, making $(\Omega A, d)$ a differential graded $*$-algebra.

### Step 2: Promoting $\Omega^0 A \to B_0$ and $\Omega A \to B$

The central construction defines a bimodule $H$ over $A$ as a new algebra:

$$B_0 = A \oplus H$$

with the product $b_0 b'_0 = aa' + ah' + ha'$, where $aa' \in A$ is inherited from $A$, while $ah' \in H$ and $ha' \in H$ are the left- and right-actions. The left action is $ah = L_a h$ and the right action is $ha \equiv R_a h = J L_{a^*} J^*$.

Two traditional NCG axioms — (i) associativity of $A$ and (ii) the order-zero condition $[L_a, R_b] = 0$ — are condensed into the single assumption that $B_0$ is an associative algebra.

The operator $J$ is reinterpreted: the map $b_0 = a + h \to b_0^* = a^* + Jh$ is an anti-automorphism of $B_0$, with period 2 when KO dimension is 0, 1, 6, or 7 mod 8 (i.e., $J^2 = 1$) and period 4 when KO dimension is 2, 3, 4, or 5 mod 8 (i.e., $J^2 = -1$). When $J^2 = 1$, $B_0$ is a $*$-algebra with $*$-operation given by eq. (3).

Similarly, one defines:

$$B = \Omega A \oplus H$$

with product $bb' = \omega\omega' + \omega h' + h\omega'$. The Dirac operator $D$ is regarded as the representation on $H$ of the map $\delta: \Omega^0 A \to \Omega^1 A$, satisfying the Leibniz rule $D(ah) = \delta[a]h + a(Dh)$. This gives $\delta[a]h = [D, L_a]h$ and $h\delta[a] = J[D, L_a]^* J^* h$.

The order-zero condition $[L_a, R_b] = 0$ becomes the vanishing of the associator $[\omega_0, h, \omega'_0]$, while the order-one conditions $[L_a, [D, R_b]] = 0$ and $[[D, L_a], R_b] = 0$ become the vanishing of associators $[\omega_0, h, \omega_1]$ and $[\omega_1, h, \omega_0]$.

### Application to the Standard Model

The SM is described by a finite-dimensional real spectral triple of KO dimension 6, with algebra $A = \mathbb{C} \oplus \mathbb{H} \oplus M_3(\mathbb{C})$. The Hilbert space $H$ is 32-dimensional (per generation), split into $H = H_R \oplus H_L \oplus \bar{H}_R \oplus \bar{H}_L$ (right/left particles and antiparticles), each further split into lepton and quark subspaces.

The helicity operator $\gamma$ and charge conjugation $J$ act as:
- $\gamma h_R = -h_R$, $\gamma h_L = h_L$, $\gamma \bar{h}_R = \bar{h}_R$, $\gamma \bar{h}_L = -\bar{h}_L$
- $J h_R = \bar{h}_R$, $J h_L = \bar{h}_L$, $J \bar{h}_R = h_R$, $J \bar{h}_L = h_L$

The left action of $a = \{\lambda, q, m\}$ with $\lambda \in \mathbb{C}$, $q \in \mathbb{H}$, $m \in M_3(\mathbb{C})$ is specified through the quaternion representation and embedding $q_\lambda$.

The Dirac operator $D$ obeys four geometric constraints: $D^\dagger = D$, $\{D, \gamma\} = 0$, $[D, J] = 0$, and $[[D, L_a], R_b] = 0$. These constrain $D$ to the $8 \times 8$ block form (eq. 9), where $y_l$ and $y_q$ are arbitrary $2 \times 2$ matrices acting on doublet indices, $m$ is a $2 \times 2$ matrix, and $n$ is a $6 \times 2$ matrix.

**The 7-parameter puzzle:** Of the 8 complex parameters $\{a, b, \vec{c}, \vec{d}\}$ in $m$ and $n$, only $a$ (right-handed neutrino Majorana mass) belongs to the SM. The remaining 7 parameters $\{b, \vec{c}, \vec{d}\}$ are unwanted. Traditionally removed by the ad hoc "massless photon" assumption ($D$ commutes with $L_a$ for $a = \{\lambda, q_\lambda, 0\}$).

### The New Constraint and Its Solution

The associativity of $B = \Omega A \oplus H$ implies not only the usual order-zero and order-one conditions, but also a **new constraint**: $[[D, L_a], [D, R_b]] = 0$.

This "order-two" condition can be satisfied in four ways:
1. $b = \vec{c} = \vec{d} = 0$ (precisely the 7 unwanted parameters set to zero)
2. $y_{q,11} = y_{q,21} = b = 0$
3. $y_{l,11} = y_{l,21} = \vec{c} = \vec{d} = 0$
4. $y_{l,11} = y_{l,21} = y_{q,11} = y_{q,21} = \vec{c} = 0$

Solution (i) is exactly the desired result: it eliminates all 7 unwanted parameters without any ad hoc assumption.

### Embedding Direction and Final Form

The general embedding of $\mathbb{C}$ in $\mathbb{H}$ is $q_\lambda(\hat{n}) = \text{Re}(\lambda)I_{2\times 2} + \text{Im}(\lambda)\hat{n}\cdot\vec{\sigma}$. Using the more general possibility $L_a L_R = q_\lambda(\hat{n}_l) L_R$ and $L_a Q_R = q_\lambda(\hat{n}_q) Q_R$, one finds a preferred choice for $\hat{n}_l$ and $\hat{n}_q$ such that, after a change of basis, the Yukawa matrices take the form:

$$y_l = \begin{pmatrix} y_\nu \varphi_1 & -y_e \bar{\varphi}_2 \\ y_\nu \varphi_2 & +y_e \bar{\varphi}_1 \end{pmatrix}, \quad y_q = \begin{pmatrix} y_u \varphi_1 & -y_d \bar{\varphi}_2 \\ y_u \varphi_2 & +y_d \bar{\varphi}_1 \end{pmatrix}$$

with $\{y_\nu, y_e, y_u, y_d, \varphi_1, \varphi_2\} \in \mathbb{C}$. This is precisely the finite geometry that generates the standard model after fluctuation and substitution into the spectral action.

### Discussion

The authors note: (i) the formalism naturally extends to non-associative geometry; (ii) the SM algebra $A = \mathbb{C} \oplus \mathbb{H} \oplus M_3(\mathbb{C})$ can be understood as a subalgebra of $A' = M_2(\mathbb{H}) \oplus M_4(\mathbb{C})$; (iii) non-associativity already appears in the traditional NCG embedding of the SM when one tensors the finite geometry with a continuous geometry; (iv) in a follow-up paper (arXiv:1408.5367), the formalism predicts a slight extension of the SM with two new particles: a $U(1)_{B-L}$ gauge boson and a complex scalar carrying $B-L$ charge responsible for higgsing the new $U(1)_{B-L}$ symmetry.

## Key Results

1. **Axiom unification:** The order-zero condition $[L_a, R_b] = 0$, the order-one condition $[[D, L_a], R_b] = 0$, and the new order-two condition $[[D, L_a], [D, R_b]] = 0$ are all unified as associativity conditions on the algebra $B = \Omega A \oplus H$.

2. **Resolution of the 7-parameter puzzle:** The new order-two constraint $[[D, L_a], [D, R_b]] = 0$ precisely eliminates the 7 unwanted parameters $\{b, \vec{c}, \vec{d}\}$ from the Dirac operator without any ad hoc assumption, yielding exactly the SM Dirac operator.

3. **NCG is more predictive than EFT:** The NCG construction requires only 2 inputs (algebra $A$ and its representation) versus 3 for EFT (symmetries, fermions, scalars). The Higgs sector is predicted, not input.

4. **Generalization to non-associative geometry:** The reformulation naturally extends Connes' framework from non-commutative to non-associative geometry, with the familiar NCG axioms replaced by compatible associativity constraints on $B$.

5. **Reinterpretation of $J$:** The real structure $J$ is reinterpreted as the $*$-operation of $B_0$: the map $b_0 = a + h \to b_0^* = a^* + Jh$ is an anti-automorphism of period 2 (KO dim 0,1,6,7) or period 4 (KO dim 2,3,4,5).

6. **SM Yukawa structure derived:** The final form of the Yukawa matrices $y_l$ and $y_q$ (eq. 12), with Yukawa couplings $\{y_\nu, y_e, y_u, y_d\}$ and Higgs doublet $(\varphi_1, \varphi_2)$, is derived purely from the geometry.

7. **$U(1)_{B-L}$ prediction:** Follow-up work shows the formalism predicts a $U(1)_{B-L}$ gauge boson and a $B-L$ charged scalar (mentioned in Discussion, detailed in arXiv:1408.5367).

## Relevance to Phonon-Exflation

The Boyle-Farnsworth reformulation is directly relevant to the phonon-exflation framework in several ways. First, the framework uses a spectral triple with KO dimension 6 on $M^4 \times F$ where $F$ encodes the finite internal geometry; Boyle-Farnsworth's resolution of the 7-parameter puzzle strengthens the geometric inevitability of the SM Dirac operator that the framework inherits. Second, the reinterpretation of the NCG axioms as associativity conditions on $B = \Omega A \oplus H$ provides a cleaner algebraic foundation for the block-diagonal theorem ($[iK_7, D_K] = 0$) and the Schur's lemma arguments used in the framework's sector decomposition. Third, the prediction that the Higgs sector is output (not input) from the NCG construction aligns with the framework's derivation of the Higgs-sigma portal structure from the spectral action. Fourth, the natural extension to non-associative geometry may be relevant to the framework's instanton gas physics, where the finite geometry is tensored with the continuous manifold and associativity is lost beyond order-one. Finally, the $U(1)_{B-L}$ prediction from the follow-up work connects to the framework's treatment of lepton number and the BCS condensate's $K_7$ charge structure.
