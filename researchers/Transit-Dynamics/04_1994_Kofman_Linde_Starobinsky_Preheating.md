# Towards the Theory of Reheating After Inflation

**Author(s):** Lev Kofman, Andrei D. Linde, Alexei A. Starobinsky
**Year:** 1994
**Journal:** Physical Review D

---

## Abstract

Kofman, Linde, and Starobinsky introduced the term "preheating" to describe the rapid, non-perturbative stage of particle production following inflation. They showed that the oscillating inflaton field can trigger parametric resonance in coupled fields, leading to explosive particle creation far exceeding perturbative decay rates. This work revealed that reheating is not a slow thermalization process but rather an impulsive, resonant transfer of energy from coherent inflaton oscillations to relativistic particles.

---

## Historical Context

After inflation ends, the inflaton field oscillates about the minimum of its potential. Conventionally, it was assumed this decay happens perturbatively, with a rate Γ set by the inflaton's coupling to matter. However, Kofman, Linde, and Starobinsky recognized that the oscillating coherent field can amplify fluctuations of coupled fields via parametric resonance—an effect known in classical mechanics and plasma physics. This resonance can be "broad" (affecting many modes) and "violent," leading to copious particle production in a time scale much shorter than the perturbative decay time.

This discovery showed that the early universe's reheating phase is far more dynamic and violent than previously thought, with implications for baryogenesis, GUT-scale physics, and primordial gravitational waves.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### Parametric Resonance Mechanism

Consider the inflaton φ coupled to a scalar field χ via:

V = ½m_φ² φ² + ½g² φ² χ²

After inflation, φ oscillates with amplitude φ_0 >> m_φ. The equation for χ modes is:

d²χ_k/dt² + (k² + g² φ₀² cos²(m_φ t)) χ_k = 0

This is a Mathieu equation (time-periodic coefficient). For sufficiently large coupling g²φ₀²/m_φ², the resonance parameter becomes large, and certain bands of k-values undergo exponential growth.

### Instability Bands

The Mathieu equation has stability (exponential decay) and instability (exponential growth) bands. The growth exponent in the instability band is:

|χ_k| ~ exp(μ_k t)

where the Floquet exponent μ_k is non-zero for q = g²φ₀²/(4m_φ²) >> 1. For "broad resonance," a large range of modes satisfies μ_k > 0.

### Bogoliubov Approach to Parametric Resonance

At the initial moment, χ is in its vacuum state. The time-dependent frequency ω_k(t) = √{k² + g²φ₀² cos²(m_φ t)} mixes the modes via Bogoliubov transformation. After one oscillation period T = 2π/m_φ, the Bogoliubov coefficients satisfy:

|β_k|² ~ exp(-2μ_k T)

for modes in the instability band. Over N oscillations, |β_k|² grows as:

|β_k|² ~ exp(2N μ_k)

resulting in exponential particle production.

### Rescattering and Backreaction

As particle density grows, the created particles scatter off the oscillating inflaton field, transferring energy into higher modes and eventually thermalizing. Kofman et al. included these backreaction effects (particle-inflaton scattering) to estimate when preheating transitions to reheating.

### Application to λφ⁴ + g²φ²χ²

For the concrete model λφ⁴/4 + g²φ²χ²/2:
- Initial inflaton amplitude: φ₀ ~ 10¹⁶ GeV (GUT scale)
- Coupling: g ~ 10⁻¹ (weak)
- Resulting resonance parameter: q ~ 10⁴ (very broad)

The timescale for particle production is:

t_preheat ~ 1/√{gλ} × (Planck time)

For GUT-scale inflation, this gives t_preheat ~ 10⁻³⁶ s, orders of magnitude faster than perturbative reheating.

## Key Results

1. **Broad Parametric Resonance**: For many couplings, the resonance is so broad that nearly all long-wavelength modes (k < m_φ) undergo exponential growth simultaneously.

2. **Energy Transfer Efficiency**: Preheating converts a substantial fraction (order 1) of inflaton energy into relativistic particles in a dynamical timescale, not a perturbative decay time.

3. **Non-Thermal Distribution**: The particle distribution at the end of preheating is highly non-thermal, dominated by created particles with k ~ √{g} m_φ. True thermalization occurs later.

4. **Parametric Resonance Decay**: Unlike perturbative decay (Γ_pert ~ g²m_φ), the effective decay rate during preheating is:

   Γ_preheat ~ g√{gλ} m_φ

   This can exceed perturbative rates by many orders of magnitude.

5. **Backreaction Timescale**: Once particle density reaches the level where particle-inflaton scattering dominates, further growth saturates and reheating transitions to a quasi-thermalization stage.

---

## Impact and Legacy

Kofman-Linde-Starobinsky revolutionized the understanding of post-inflationary physics:

- **Reheating Temperature**: T_reheat can be lower than previously estimated because preheating is so efficient. This has implications for GUT baryogenesis, axion production, and primordial gravitational waves.

- **Preheating Gravitational Waves**: The violent parametric resonance produces copious GW radiation, testable by future detectors (LISA, BBO).

- **Heavy Particle Production**: Preheating can create particles with masses >> m_φ/2 that would be suppressed in perturbative decay, enabling GUT-scale physics.

- **Baryogenesis and Leptogenesis**: The large particle fluxes and non-thermal distributions during preheating can affect baryon/lepton asymmetry generation.

Follow-up work includes detailed simulations (Campos, Guth, Krauss), study of multi-field preheating, and gravitational wave production calculations.
