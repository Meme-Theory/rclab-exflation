# Black Hole Thermodynamics

**Author(s):** Ted Jacobson
**Year:** 1995 (foundational); 1987 earlier work
**Journal:** Physical Review D, Physical Review Letters

## Abstract

Jacobson established that black hole thermodynamics is not merely an analogy but a fundamental law of physics. The area of a black hole's event horizon plays the role of entropy; the Hawking temperature is the thermodynamic temperature. Jacobson's key insight: this correspondence is not a derived consequence of Einstein gravity but a deep principle from which Einstein's equations themselves can be derived. This inversion—deriving gravity from thermodynamics rather than vice versa—revealed profound connections between quantum mechanics, thermodynamics, and spacetime geometry.

---

## Historical Context

Hawking (1974) showed black holes emit radiation with temperature T_H = κ/(2π) (in Planck units). Bekenstein had conjectured S_BH = A/(4G) (area/entropy equivalence). By the 1990s, these were accepted as facts but seemed peculiar: why does a macroscopic object have thermodynamic properties tied to microscopic quantum fields?

Jacobson's 1995 paper "Thermodynamics of Spacetime" showed that if we assume:
1. The generalized second law (entropy increases)
2. All dynamical fields obey local energy conservation

Then Einstein's equations follow algebraically. Gravity is not a fundamental force but an emergent thermodynamic description.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### Horizon Thermodynamics and Area Entropy

For a black hole with area A, surface gravity κ, the thermodynamic quantities are:

Entropy: S = A / (4 G ℏ) (in Planck units: S ≈ A / 4)

Temperature: T = κ / (2π) (in Planck units: T = κ / (2π ℏ))

Internal energy: U = κ A / (8π)

These satisfy the first law:

dU = T dS ↔ dE = (κ / (2π)) d(A / 4)

### Causal Horizons and Entropy Flux

Jacobson's key step: consider a small patch of a causal horizon (can be any horizon—black hole, cosmological, Rindler). Observers near the horizon measure an entropy flux flowing out with velocity v. For the second law to hold, this entropy flux must satisfy:

dS/dt = (area × ρ × specific entropy flow)

Requiring this for all observer velocities and all horizon patches leads to Einstein's equations.

### Derivation from Thermodynamic Consistency

Consider a small region near a horizon where the metric can be locally approximated as Rindler (uniformly accelerating spacetime):

ds² ≈ −κ² ξ² dt² + dξ² + dx² + dy²

where ξ is the distance from the horizon and κ is the surface gravity. Observers at distance ξ from the horizon measure the Unruh temperature:

T_Unruh = κ / (2π)

For the thermodynamic entropy-area relation to be consistent with quantum mechanics (field theory in curved space), Einstein's equations must hold. Any violation leads to inconsistency.

### From Area Increase to Gravity

The second law requires dA/dt ≥ 0 (area cannot decrease). For a dynamical horizon where matter falls in:

dA/dt = (matter energy flux) × (horizon geometry factors)

Requiring consistency with local energy conservation and causality yields:

Energy-momentum tensor T_μν satisfies: ∇_μ T^μ_ν = 0 (conservation)

Gravitational field equations: T_μν ∝ G_μν (Einstein equations)

### Cosmological Horizons and Dark Energy

For a cosmological horizon (de Sitter with Λ > 0), the horizon area A_c = 4π / Λ is constant, while entropy S_c = A_c / 4 is fixed. This fixes a "temperature":

T_c = 1 / (2π √{Λ}) = (Hubble rate) / (2π)

Dark energy density Λ emerges as the entropic cost of maintaining the cosmological horizon. The universe evolves to maximize entropy subject to constraints—a principle underlying thermodynamic evolution.

## Key Results

1. **Gravity Emerges from Thermodynamics**: Einstein's equations are not fundamental but thermodynamic identities.

2. **Entropy Area Relation is Universal**: Any horizon (event, Cauchy, cosmological) exhibits S ∝ A, independent of the nature of the spacetime.

3. **Black Hole Thermodynamics is Exact**: First law, second law, and Nernst law all hold exactly, with no corrections.

4. **Dark Energy as Horizon Entropy**: Cosmological constant can be interpreted as entropy of the cosmological horizon, providing a thermodynamic explanation for dark energy density.

## Impact and Legacy

Jacobson's thermodynamic perspective:

- **Gravity as Emergence**: Spawned research on emergent gravity, holographic principle, and AdS/CFT.

- **Black Hole Entropy**: Spurred investigations into the microscopic origin of black hole entropy (string theory, loop quantum gravity).

- **Quantum Gravity**: Suggested that quantum gravity should emerge from thermodynamic principles, not derive them.

- **Cosmology**: Connected dark energy to entropy, offering new perspectives on the cosmological constant problem.
