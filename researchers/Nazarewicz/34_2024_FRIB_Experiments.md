## Abstract

This white paper is the result of a collaboration by those that attended a workshop at the Facility for Rare Isotope Beams (FRIB), organized by the FRIB Theory Alliance (FRIB-TA), on "Theoretical Justifications and Motivations for Early High-Profile FRIB Experiments". It covers a wide range of topics related to the science that will be explored at FRIB. After a brief introduction, the sections address: (II) Overview of theoretical methods, (III) Experimental capabilities, (IV) Structure, (V) Near-threshold Physics, (VI) Reaction mechanisms, (VII) Nuclear equations of state, (VIII) Nuclear astrophysics, (IX) Fundamental symmetries, and (X) Experimental design and uncertainty quantification.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### Introduction (Sec. I)
FRIB at Michigan State University began operations in May 2022, with over 200 proposals submitted to the Program Advisory Committee. Beam power will ramp from 10 kW to 400 kW over the next decade. The whitepaper was produced from a May 2023 FRIB-TA workshop bringing together experimentalists and theorists to maximize scientific impact of early experiments.

### Shell Structure Evolution (Sec. IV.A)
Textbook magic numbers (2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82, 126) apply near stability. Moving away from stability, the mean field changes and shell gaps disappear or appear in new places. Primary drivers: monopole component of the tensor force and continuum effects. Key regions: $^{28}$O (neutron clustering), $^{42}$Si (quadrupole + continuum), $^{60}$Ca (pairing in continuum), $^{78}$Ni (5th island of inversion), $^{100}$Sn (end of N=Z line). Islands of inversion occur when intruder configurations become the ground state due to competition between shell structure and correlation energy.

### Collectivity (Sec. IV.B)
Collective degrees of freedom include clustering, superfluidity, vibrations, and deformation. Octupole excitations, pygmy dipole resonance, and connections between collective and microscopic theory are active areas. FRIB uniquely positioned to study how these features are modified near the driplines.

### Pairing Effects (Sec. IV.E)
Following Bohr, Mottelson, and Pines (1958), pairing via neutron-neutron Cooper pairs modifies nuclear properties (deformation, moments of inertia, alignments). Of particular interest is a possible BCS-to-BEC crossover transition in neutron-rich nuclei. At normal density the correlation length $\zeta > d$ (Cooper pairs). At lower densities (relevant to weakly-bound nuclear surfaces), $\zeta < d$ and di-neutron localization appears. Near the drip line, the Fermi level $\lambda \to 0$ and $S_n \approx \Delta$ (pairing gap dominates binding). Two-neutron transfer reactions probe the pair density; $(t,p)$ reactions are particularly suited. Sn isotopes are the classic example; for $A > 140$, large pairing gap increases are predicted from loosely bound $2p_{1/2}$ and $2p_{3/2}$ orbitals.

### Near-Threshold Physics (Sec. V)
Near-threshold physics impacts exploration of drip lines, low-lying resonances of astrophysical interest, and light nuclei testing nuclear forces. Interplays between deformation, clustering, pairing, collective motion, and near-threshold phenomena. Key targets: $^{28-31}$F (island of inversion near drip line), $^{40}$Mg (possible two-neutron halo).

### Reaction Mechanisms (Sec. VI)
Ab initio approaches to reactions, direct reactions (transfers, knockout, breakup), statistical descriptions of compound-nuclear reactions, surrogate reactions, optical-model potentials, and fission. Unified description bridging these approaches is a key challenge.

### Nuclear Equation of State (Sec. VII)
Microscopic calculations within chiral EFT, isospin dependence, and heavy-ion collision constraints. FRIB experiments will map nuclear matter properties at extreme isospin.

### Nuclear Astrophysics (Sec. VIII)
Nucleosynthesis on neutron-rich side (r-process from Fe to actinides), proton-rich side (rp-process, p-process, $\nu$-process), and accreted neutron star crusts. FRIB will access key nuclei along the r-process path.

### Fundamental Symmetries (Sec. IX)
Nuclear beta decays and CKM unitarity, tensor/scalar currents in electroweak interaction, two-body axial vector currents, heavy-element isotopes for P and T violation tests.

### Summary (Sec. XI)
Low-energy nuclear theory has matured due to exotic beam data, novel theoretical methods with quantified predictions, and high-performance computing. FRIB goals: identify relevant degrees of freedom, connect properties to underlying nucleon-nucleon interaction. Key themes: how collective features (clustering, superfluidity, deformation) are modified near the driplines and interplay with the continuum.

## Key Results

1. FRIB provides unprecedented access to exotic nuclei near and beyond the driplines, with beam power ramping to 400 kW
2. Shell evolution driven by tensor force monopole and continuum effects generates new magic numbers and islands of inversion
3. BCS-to-BEC crossover transition predicted in neutron-rich nuclei where pairing correlation length drops below inter-pair distance
4. Near the drip line, pairing gap dominates binding ($S_n \approx \Delta$) rather than the mean field ($S_n \approx \lambda$)
5. Five key structural regions identified: $^{28}$O, $^{42}$Si, $^{60}$Ca, $^{78}$Ni, $^{100}$Sn
6. Nuclear EOS constraints from heavy-ion collisions at FRIB will probe isospin dependence at extremes
7. R-process nucleosynthesis pathways accessible at FRIB will constrain element production from Fe to actinides
8. Uncertainty quantification and Bayesian methods are becoming standard for connecting theory predictions to experimental observables
