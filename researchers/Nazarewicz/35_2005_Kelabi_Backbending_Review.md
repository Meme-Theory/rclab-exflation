# The Backbending Effect in Deformed e-e Nuclei

**Author(s):** Mohamed E. Kelabi
**Year:** 2005
**Journal:** Submitted to National Academy of Scientific Research, Journal of Basic and Applied Science, Tripoli, Libya
**arXiv:** nucl-th/0503021
**Relevance:** MEDIUM

## Abstract

An extension of the previously proposed Variable Moment of Inertia with Softness model (VMIS) has been applied to describe successfully the effect of backbending in some deformed even-even nuclei. The model shows good results in the rare earth and actinide regions.

---

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### Introduction
The backbending effect occurs when the moment of inertia increases rapidly with rotational frequency toward the rigid value. When rotational energy exceeds the energy needed to break a nucleon pair, the unpaired nucleons go into different orbits, changing the moment of inertia. Explanations include:
- Disappearance of pairing correlations due to Coriolis forces: phase transition from superfluid state to independent particle motion (Mottelson and Valatin, 1960)
- Rotational alignment (Stephens and Simon, 1972; Bengtsson and Frauendorf, 1979)
- Centrifugal stretching (Thieburger, 1973)
- Band crossing: breakup of one pair providing large angular momentum couples with collective rotation to produce a new band

The backbending is observed in $\mathcal{J}$-$\omega^2$ plots as a radical change in angular momentum behavior from linearity to stretching and back to linear.

### VMIS Model Formalism
The Variable Moment of Inertia with Softness model uses a three-parameter expression for ground state rotational band energies:

$$E(J) = A_0 J(J+1)\left[\frac{1}{1 - \sigma_1 A_0 J(J+1)}\right] + c[A_0 J(J+1)]^{3/2}$$

where $A_0 = \hbar^2/(2\mathcal{J}_0)$, the softness parameter $\sigma_1 = \frac{1}{n!}\frac{\partial^n \mathcal{J}}{\partial \mathcal{J}^n}\bigg|_{\mathcal{J}_0}$, and the constant $c$ is connected with $\beta$- and $\gamma$-vibrational energies: $c = \frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{\hbar^2\omega_\beta^4} + \frac{1}{\hbar^2\omega_\gamma^4})$.

The moment of inertia and squared rotational frequency are obtained from spin derivatives:

$$\mathcal{J} = \left[\frac{dE(J)}{dJ(J+1)}\right]^{-1}, \quad \omega^2 = \frac{dE(J)}{dJ(J+1)}$$

with the most sensitive relations being:

$$\frac{2J}{\Delta E_\gamma} = \frac{4J}{E(J) - E(J-2)} = \mathcal{J}$$
$$\omega^2 = \frac{[\Delta E_\gamma(J)]^2 - (J+1)}{2}$$

### Calculations and Results
The model is applied to 22 nuclei covering mass region A = 100 to 242, including: $^{100,102}$Zr, $^{110}$Pd, $^{120}$Ba, $^{122}$Xe, $^{130,132}$Nd, $^{140}$Ba, $^{150,152}$Nd, $^{160}$Dy, $^{162}$Er, $^{170,172,180,182,190,192}$Os, $^{230,232}$Th, $^{240,242}$Pu. Parameters $A_0$, $\sigma_1$, and $c$ are fitted by minimizing Eq. (1) for least-squares fit to experimental data at both low and high angular momenta. The $\mathcal{J}$-$\omega^2$ plots show good agreement with experimental data for ground state bands up to spin $J = 30$.

### Key Findings on Backbending Origin
The appearance of backbending is attributed to the smallness of the critical rotational frequency of the $i_{13/2}$ protons compared to $j_{15/2}$ neutrons. The behavior of the proton pair $i_{13/2}$ at high spins is decisive for the backbending effect. The absence of backbending in some nuclei may be ascribed to the presence of stable octupole deformation.

## Key Results

1. The VMIS model with 3 parameters ($A_0$, $\sigma_1$, $c$) successfully describes backbending in 22 deformed e-e nuclei across A = 100-242
2. Backbending is attributed to the phase transition from superfluid to independent-particle motion when Coriolis forces break pairs
3. The critical rotational frequency for proton $i_{13/2}$ pairs is smaller than for neutron $j_{15/2}$ pairs, making proton pair behavior decisive
4. Absence of backbending correlates with stable octupole deformation
5. Fitted parameters span wide ranges: $A_0$ = 0.008-0.54 MeV, $\sigma_1$ = 0.004-2.2, $c$ = -0.06 to 5.5 MeV$^{-2}$
6. Rare earth nuclei ($^{160}$Dy, $^{162}$Er) show clean backbending; Os isotopes show mass-dependent behavior
7. Band crossing interpretation: pair breakup produces new band with large aligned angular momentum
