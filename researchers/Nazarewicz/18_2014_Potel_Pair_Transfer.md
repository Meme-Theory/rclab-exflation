**Author(s):** Gregory Potel, Andrea Idini, Francisco Barranco, Enrico Vigezzi, Ricardo A. Broglia
**Year:** 2014
**Journal:** Conference proceedings (in "Fifty Years of Nuclear BCS", World Scientific)
**arXiv:** 1404.1317
**Relevance:** CRITICAL

## Abstract

[Not explicitly separated in proceedings format.] The paper reviews recent progress in understanding the nuclear pairing interaction through two-nucleon transfer reactions. It discusses the dual origins of nuclear pairing — bare nucleon-nucleon interaction and phonon-mediated induced interaction — and presents quantitative evidence for both via absolute differential cross sections calculated using second-order DWBA. The role of pairing vibrations as embodiments of single Cooper pairs near closed shells is discussed, along with their condensation into the superfluid phase. Applications to halo nuclei ($^{11}$Li) demonstrate the dominance of the induced pairing interaction mediated by collective vibrations.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### Pair Transfer and Pairing Correlations

The connection between BCS theory and nuclear pairing was established by Bohr, Mottelson and Pines (1958), who recognized the energy gap in deformed nuclei as analogous to the electronic gap in metallic superconductors. Nuclear pairing has two origins: (1) the bare nucleon-nucleon interaction in the $^1S_0$ channel, and (2) the exchange of collective nuclear vibrations between pairs of nucleons near the Fermi energy.

Two-nucleon transfer reactions are the specific probes of Cooper pair correlations, with absolute differential cross sections as the main model-independent observables. The coherent character of pairing correlations manifests equally in simultaneous and successive transfer processes, because Cooper pairs are weakly bound ($\ll \varepsilon_F$), highly extended ($\gg R$) objects with correlation length $\xi \approx \hbar v_F / \Delta \approx 36$ fm.

### Pairing Vibrations and Rotations

Around closed-shell nuclei, pair addition and pair subtraction modes are strongly excited in two-particle transfer. These pairing vibrations, described by RPA with constant pairing strength, smooth out the sharp occupancy change at the Fermi energy through zero-point fluctuations. Pairing vibrational bands with up to three phonon states have been observed.

For superfluid nuclei, the order parameter $\alpha = \langle\text{BCS}|P^\dagger|\text{BCS}\rangle$ measures the number of correlated pairs ($\approx 4$-$8$) and the deformation in gauge space. Adjacent ground states of isotopic chains ($N, N\pm 2$) form pairing rotational bands connected by enhanced pair transfer operators, analogous to quadrupole rotational bands connected by enhanced quadrupole operators.

### Absolute Cross Section Calculations

The minimum theory for two-nucleon transfer is second-order DWBA, where simultaneous and successive transfer processes are treated along with non-orthogonality corrections. The recent development of versatile second-order DWBA software by Potel (unpublished) allows calculation of absolute two-particle transfer cross sections that account for experimental findings within experimental errors throughout the mass table, using state-of-the-art spectroscopic amplitudes and global optical potentials.

### Medium Polarization Effects

The bare and phonon-induced interactions superpose: $V_\text{eff} = V_\text{bare} + V_\text{ind}$. The gap equation is modified by Z-factors from self-energy processes:
$$\Delta_j = -\sum_k V^\text{eff}_{jk} \frac{1}{2E_k} \frac{\Delta_k}{Z_k}$$

The Nambu-Gor'kov equations provide a consistent treatment of renormalization effects on both single-particle motion and pairing interaction. For $^{120}$Sn, the total gap $\Delta$ calculated self-consistently is considerably larger than the bare BCS value $\Delta_\text{bare}$ and overestimates experiment by $\sim 20\%$.

The induced interaction is concentrated around the Fermi energy and is strongly surface-peaked. Its effects can often be simulated by adjusting the bare interaction strength, making the pairing gap not the most specific quantity for probing the nature of the interaction.

### The Case of Halo Nuclei ($^{11}$Li)

In halo nuclei, particle-vibration coupling plays a crucial role. The $2s_{1/2}$ orbital is shifted downward by several MeV via coupling to $[p_{1/2} \otimes 2^+]_{1/2^+}$ configurations (polarization), while $1p_{1/2}$ is shifted upward due to Pauli suppression (correlation). This produces parity inversion in $N = 7$ isotones.

For $^{11}$Li, the bare $^1S_0$ neutron-neutron interaction lowers the $s_{1/2}^2(0^+)$ configuration by only $\sim 100$ keV and cannot bind the halo pair by itself. Instead, the exchange of the dipole pygmy resonance ($\sim 1$ MeV) between the halo neutrons provides the dominant glue binding the Cooper pair to the $^9$Li core — a bootstrap mechanism.

The $^1$H($^{11}$Li, $^9$Li)$^3$H reaction at TRIUMF provided quantitative evidence: the absolute differential cross section to the $^9$Li(1/2$^-$, 2.69 MeV) state measures the probability of the $|s_{1/2}, d_{5/2}^{-1}; \otimes 2^+; 0^+\rangle$ component in the $^{11}$Li ground state, confirming the role of quadrupole vibrations in binding the halo Cooper pair.

### Particle Number Fluctuations

In nuclei, particle number fluctuations are significant (unlike condensed matter). The pairing gap is modified:
$$\Delta = (\Delta_0^2 + S_0(\text{RPA})/2)^{1/2}$$
where $S_0(\text{RPA})$ contains particle-conserving matrix elements of pair addition/removal operators. Projection effects are dominant at the phase transition but lead to $\sim 10$-$20\%$ corrections for the ground state.

## Key Results

1. Nuclear pairing has dual origins: bare NN interaction and phonon-mediated induced interaction, both contributing comparably to the total gap
2. Two-nucleon transfer reactions are the specific probes of Cooper pair correlations; absolute cross sections can now be calculated throughout the mass table within experimental errors
3. Pairing vibrations (single Cooper pairs near closed shells) condense into the superfluid phase as the system moves away from closed shells
4. Successive transfer dominates over simultaneous transfer because Cooper pairs are extended objects ($\xi \approx 36$ fm $\gg R$)
5. In $^{11}$Li, the induced pairing interaction via pygmy resonance exchange dominates over the bare interaction, which cannot bind the halo pair
6. The Sn isotope chain provides the clearest evidence of pairing rotational bands, with absolute (p,t) cross sections matching theory within errors
7. Self-consistent Nambu-Gor'kov calculations for $^{120}$Sn show the total gap exceeds the bare BCS value, with the induced contribution comparable to the bare one
8. The pairing gap is not the most specific observable for probing the interaction nature; two-nucleon transfer to excited states provides more direct information
