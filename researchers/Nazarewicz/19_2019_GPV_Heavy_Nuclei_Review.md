# The Giant Pairing Vibration in Heavy Nuclei: Present Status and Future Studies

**Author(s):** M. Assie, C.H. Dasso, R.J. Liotta, A.O. Macchiavelli, A. Vitturi
**Year:** 2019
**Journal:** European Physical Journal A (special issue in memory of P.F. Bortignon)
**arXiv:** 1905.01339
**Relevance:** HIGH

---

## Abstract

The Giant Pairing Vibration, a two-nucleon collective mode originating from the second shell above the Fermi surface, has long been predicted and expected to be strongly populated in two-nucleon transfer reactions with cross sections similar to those of the normal Pairing Vibration. Recent experiments have provided evidence for this mode in $^{14,15}$C but, despite sensitive studies, it has not been definitively identified either in Sn or Pb nuclei where pairing correlations are known to play a crucial role near their ground states. In this paper we review the basic theoretical concepts of this "elusive" state and the status of experimental searches in heavy nuclei. We discuss the hindrance effects due to Q-value mismatch and the use of weakly-bound projectiles as a way to overcome the limitations of the (p,t) and (t,p) reactions. We also discuss the role of the continuum and conclude with some possible future developments.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### Theoretical Prediction (Sec. II)

The GPV was predicted by Broglia and Bes (1977) as a coherent superposition of 2p (addition) or 2h (removal) states in the next major shell $2\hbar\omega$ above (below) the Fermi surface. For a schematic Hamiltonian with independent particles interacting via constant pairing force $G$, the RPA dispersion relation is:
$$F(E) = \sum_j \frac{(2j+1)}{E - 2e_j} = \frac{2}{G}$$
The GPV is the collective state relative to the second major shell, at estimated energy $\sim 65/A^{1/3}$ MeV, carrying 20%-100% of the ground state cross section.

### Collectivity and Cross Sections

For $\Omega$ degenerate levels with amplitudes $\beta_j \approx 1/\sqrt{\Omega}$:
$$\sigma(\text{GPV}) \sim \Omega \cdot \sigma_\text{sp}$$
scaling as $A^{2/3}$ with mass number. The enhanced cross section results from coherent addition of all two-particle transfer amplitudes with the same sign, analogous to the collectivity of surface vibrations probed by inelastic scattering.

### Connection to Alpha Decay

Independent of the original prediction, the GPV was rediscovered in alpha-decay studies. The formation of the alpha particle in $^{212}$Po requires neutron-proton clustering through isovector pairing states:
$$|^{212}\text{Po(gs)}\rangle = A|^{210}\text{Pb(gs)} \cdot ^{210}\text{Po(gs)}\rangle + B|^{210}\text{Bi}(0^+_1) \cdot ^{210}\text{Bi}(0^+_1)\rangle$$
where $^{210}\text{Bi}(0^+_1)$ lies at $\sim 5$ MeV — approximately the energy of the GPV. Including this neutron-proton clustering produced perfect agreement with experimental half-lives.

### Experimental Searches (Sec. III)

Multiple (p,t) experiments at 35-60 MeV on $^{120}$Sn and $^{208}$Pb targets using magnetic spectrometers have failed to definitively identify the GPV. Upper limits for the cross section in the $\sim 12$ MeV excitation region of $^{118}$Sn are 0.13-0.19 mb. A bump-like structure with width $1.5 \pm 0.4$ MeV was observed in one experiment but no clear evidence emerged.

Recent heavy-ion experiments ($^{12}$C($^{18}$O,$^{16}$O)$^{14}$C and $^{13}$C($^{18}$O,$^{16}$O)$^{15}$C at 84 MeV) identified the GPV in light nuclei at $\sim 20$ MeV excitation, with $L = 0$ character and transfer probabilities consistent with GPV population.

### Q-value Mismatch (Sec. IV)

A key factor in the non-observation is the Q-value matching condition:
$$\sigma \sim \exp\left[-\frac{(Q - Q_\text{opt})^2}{2\hbar^2 \kappa r_0}\right]$$
Standard (p,t) and (t,p) reactions are well-matched for gs-to-gs transitions, but the large excitation energy of the GPV ($\sim 12$-15 MeV) severely quenches the cross section. Weakly bound projectiles ($^6$He, $^{11}$Li) have more favorable Q-values, with survey maps showing $^6$He and $^{11}$Li beams as the best candidates for populating the GPV in $^{208}$Pb.

### Continuum Effects (Sec. V.B)

Within the Berggren representation (complex energy plane including bound states, resonances, and continuum), the two-neutron GPV in $^{210}$Pb is found to be very wide and not a physically relevant state but rather part of the continuum background. The proton-neutron GPV in $^{210}$Bi is meaningful only if bound below $\sim 7$ MeV. As the energy approaches the continuum threshold, the collectivity gradually disappears.

The melting of the GPV peak due to width acquired from low-$\ell$ single-particle states, combined with Q-value mismatch, accounts for the elusive nature of this mode.

## Key Results

1. The GPV was predicted at $\sim 65/A^{1/3}$ MeV, carrying 20%-100% of the ground state pair transfer cross section
2. GPV has been identified in light nuclei ($^{14,15}$C) at $\sim 20$ MeV excitation but remains elusive in heavy nuclei (Sn, Pb)
3. Q-value mismatch is the primary hindrance: standard (p,t) reactions favor gs transitions, suppressing high-excitation modes exponentially
4. Weakly bound projectiles ($^6$He, $^{11}$Li) have more favorable Q-matching for populating the GPV
5. Continuum coupling causes the two-neutron GPV in heavy nuclei to be very broad, merging with the continuum background
6. The neutron-proton GPV in $^{210}$Bi may exist as a bound or narrow resonant state below $\sim 7$ MeV
7. The GPV connection to alpha decay provides independent evidence for high-shell pair clustering
8. The collective pair transfer form factor involves the deformation parameter $\beta_P \propto \Delta/G$
