# Configuration Mixing of Angular-Momentum Projected Triaxial Relativistic Mean-Field Wave Functions

**Author(s):** J. M. Yao, J. Meng, P. Ring, and D. Vretenar
**Year:** 2010
**Journal:** Physical Review C 81, 044311 (2010)
**arXiv:** 0912.2650
**Relevance:** HIGH

---

## Abstract

The framework of relativistic energy density functionals is extended to include correlations related to the restoration of broken symmetries and to fluctuations of collective variables. The generator coordinate method is used to perform configuration mixing of angular-momentum projected wave functions, generated by constrained self-consistent relativistic mean-field calculations for triaxial shapes. The effects of triaxial deformation and of K-mixing is illustrated in a study of spectroscopic properties of low-spin states in $^{24}$Mg.

---

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### 1. Single-Reference vs. Multi-Reference EDF

The paper distinguishes two levels of the EDF framework:

**Single-reference (SR) EDF:** Self-consistent mean-field, functional of one-body density matrices corresponding to a single product state. Breaks translational, rotational, particle number symmetries. Describes only bulk ground-state properties.

**Multi-reference (MR) EDF:** Includes collective correlations through symmetry restoration and configuration mixing. The energy becomes a functional of all transition density matrices from the chosen set of product states. Required for excitation spectra and transition rates.

### 2. The 3DAMP+GCM Model

The trial wave function is an angular-momentum projected GCM collective state:

$$|\Psi^{JM}_\alpha\rangle = \int dq \sum_{K\geq 0} f^{JK}_\alpha(q) \frac{1}{(1+\delta_{K0})} |JMK+,q\rangle$$

where the basis states are projected from intrinsic wave functions:

$$|JMK+,q\rangle = [\hat{P}^J_{MK} + (-1)^J \hat{P}^J_{M-K}]|\Phi(q)\rangle$$

The angular-momentum projection operator:

$$\hat{P}^J_{MK} = \frac{2J+1}{8\pi^2}\int d\Omega D^{J*}_{MK}(\Omega)\hat{R}(\Omega)$$

For a triaxially deformed even-even nucleus with $D_2$ and time-reversal symmetry, the quantum number $K$ takes only non-negative even values.

### 3. Relativistic EDF: PC-F1

The calculation uses the relativistic point-coupling functional PC-F1, with energy density:

$$E_{RMF} = \sum_k \int dr\, v^2_k \bar\psi_k(-i\gamma\nabla + m)\psi_k + \int dr\, [\frac{\alpha_S}{2}\rho^2_S + \frac{\beta_S}{3}\rho^3_S + \frac{\gamma_S}{4}\rho^4_S + \ldots]$$

where local densities (scalar, vector, isovector) are calculated in the no-sea approximation. Pairing correlations use a $\delta$-force in BCS approximation with smooth cutoff factors.

### 4. Hill-Wheeler-Griffin Equation

The weight functions $f^{JK}_\alpha(q)$ are determined from the variational condition $\delta E^J = 0$, yielding the HWG integral equation:

$$\int dq' \sum_{K'\geq 0} [H^J_{KK'}(q,q') - E^J_\alpha N^J_{KK'}(q,q')] f^{JK'}_\alpha(q') = 0$$

The kernel matrices use angular-momentum projected overlaps:

$$O^J_{KK'}(q,q') = \Delta_{KK'}[O^J_{KK'} + (-1)^{2J}O^J_{-K-K'} + (-1)^J O^J_{K-K'} + (-1)^J O^J_{-KK'}]$$

### 5. Natural States and Norm Eigenvalue Cutoff

Since the basis functions are not linearly independent, many eigenvalues of the norm overlap kernel $N^J$ are near zero. States with $n^J_k/n^J_{max} < \zeta$ are removed from the GCM basis. The collective Hamiltonian is built from the remaining "natural states":

$$H^J_{kl} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n^J_k}} \frac{1}{\sqrt{n^J_l}} \sum_{i,j} u^J_k(i) H^J(i,j) u^J_l(j)$$

The collective wave functions $g^J_\alpha(i)$ are orthonormal and $|g^J_\alpha(i)|^2$ is interpretable as probability amplitude.

### 6. Particle Number Correction

Since BCS states are not eigenstates of particle number, the Hamiltonian kernel is modified:

$$H'(r;q,q';\Omega) = H(r;q,q';\Omega) - \lambda_p[Z(r;q,q';\Omega) - Z_0] - \lambda_n[N(r;q,q';\Omega) - N_0]$$

The Lagrange multipliers $\lambda_\tau$ are taken as deformation-dependent Fermi energies from the mean-field calculation.

### 7. Results for $^{24}$Mg

**Convergence:** The inclusion of oblate shapes lowers the ground state by ~300 keV. Including triaxial shapes ($\gamma \neq 0$) lowers energies by an additional ~300 keV and enhances B(E2) values by 4-8%.

**Excitation spectrum:** The level scheme agrees well with experiment, though spectra are systematically stretched (angular-momentum projection is performed after variation, neglecting time-odd components and alignment effects).

**B(E2) values:** Calculated parameter-free with bare proton charges. Ground-state band: $B(E2: 2^+_1 \to 0^+_1) = 82.3$ $e^2$fm$^4$ (exp: 99), $B(E2: 4^+_1 \to 2^+_1) = 147.1$ $e^2$fm$^4$ (exp: 123). Good agreement considering no effective charges are used.

**Band structure:** Ground-state band (dominant $K=0$), quasi-$\gamma$ band ($K=2$ dominant, starting at $2^+_2$), and quasi-$\beta$ band ($K=0$, starting at $0^+_2$) are identified. The $K=0$ component of $2^+_1$ exhausts 92% of the norm.

**Probability distributions:** The ground state and $K=0$ component of $2^+_1$ are concentrated along the prolate axis ($\beta \approx 0.5$). Despite the projected energy surface showing a minimum at $\beta = 0.50$, $\gamma = 20°$, GCM configuration mixing drives the structure back toward prolate symmetry.

**E0 transitions:** The E0 transition rate $\rho^2_{21}(E0) = |\langle 0^+_2|\hat{T}(E0)|0^+_1\rangle|^2/e^2R^4_0$ is computed from GCM wave functions.

## Key Results

1. Triaxial shapes lower energies by ~300 keV and enhance B(E2) by 4-8% compared to axially symmetric calculations
2. The 3DAMP+GCM with relativistic EDF (PC-F1) reproduces low-spin spectroscopy of $^{24}$Mg well
3. K-mixing is essential for the $\gamma$-band; the $2^+_2$ state has 87% K=2 content
4. Angular momentum projection shifts the PES minimum to triaxial ($\gamma \approx 20°$), but GCM mixing restores near-prolate structure
5. Norm eigenvalue cutoff $\zeta = 5 \times 10^{-3}$ is optimal; excitation energies are insensitive for $\zeta < 10^{-2}$
6. Particle number dispersion is ~0.3 for ground state -- particle-number projection is an important future improvement
7. Spectra are systematically stretched vs. experiment (projection after variation misses alignment effects)

## Relevance to Phonon-Exflation

The GCM framework for configuration mixing beyond the mean field is the nuclear analog of what the phonon-exflation framework requires but has not yet implemented. The framework's BCS mechanism on SU(3) produces a mean-field ground state at each tau, but the transit through the fold involves dynamical configuration mixing -- the GCM collective wave function $|\Psi^J_\alpha\rangle$ mixing different deformations is the nuclear version of summing over instanton configurations in the framework's partition function. Specific parallels: (1) The norm eigenvalue cutoff problem -- removing near-zero eigenvalues of the overlap kernel -- is structurally identical to the framework's regularization of the spectral action at high cutoff. (2) The finding that triaxial shapes lower energies by ~300 keV via K-mixing maps onto the framework's U(1)$_7$ symmetry breaking, where Jensen-breaking mixes K-sectors and lowers the BCS condensation energy. (3) The systematic stretching of spectra (projection after variation) parallels the framework's inverted Born-Oppenheimer problem, where geometry (fast) and pairing (slow) evolve on different timescales.
