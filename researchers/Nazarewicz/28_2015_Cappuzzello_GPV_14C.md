The Giant Pairing Vibration (GPV), a collective two-nucleon mode originating from the second major shell above the Fermi surface, has been a long-standing theoretical prediction since Broglia and Bes (1977). This work reports the first experimental observation of GPV signatures in light nuclei $^{14}$C and $^{15}$C, using heavy-ion-induced two-neutron transfer reactions $^{12}$C($^{18}$O,$^{16}$O)$^{14}$C and $^{13}$C($^{18}$O,$^{16}$O)$^{15}$C at 84 MeV incident energy with the MAGNEX spectrometer at INFN-LNS.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### The Giant Pairing Vibration Concept

The GPV is the pairing analogue of giant resonances in the particle-hole channel. While ordinary pairing vibrations (PV) involve spin-zero-coupled pair excitations across a single major shell gap, the GPV involves coherent superposition of 2p (addition mode) or 2h (removal mode) states in the next major shell, $2\hbar\omega$ above (or below) the Fermi surface.

The GPV was predicted by Broglia and Bes (1977) to appear at an energy of approximately $65/A^{1/3}$ MeV, carrying a cross section that is 20-100% of the ground state two-nucleon transfer cross section. Despite extensive searches in heavy nuclei (Sn and Pb isotopes), it had never been definitively identified prior to this work.

### Experimental Design

- **Reactions:** $^{12}$C($^{18}$O,$^{16}$O)$^{14}$C and $^{13}$C($^{18}$O,$^{16}$O)$^{15}$C
- **Beam energy:** 84 MeV incident laboratory energy
- **Spectrometer:** MAGNEX at INFN-LNS (Laboratori Nazionali del Sud), Catania
- **Technique:** Two-neutron transfer reactions with heavy-ion projectiles

### Key Observations

**$^{14}$C resonance:**
- Excitation energy: $E_x = 19.9 \pm 0.1$ MeV
- Width: $1.2 \pm 0.3$ MeV (FWHM)

**$^{15}$C resonance:**
- Excitation energy: $E_x = 20.4 \pm 0.1$ MeV
- Width: $1.9 \pm 0.3$ MeV (FWHM)

### Evidence for GPV Identification

1. **$L = 0$ monopole character:** Confirmed by angular distribution oscillations and coupled reaction channels (CRC) calculations. The angular distributions show the characteristic forward-peaked pattern expected for $L = 0$ transfer.

2. **Excitation energy:** Consistent with the theoretical prediction of $\sim 65/A^{1/3}$ MeV for the GPV energy.

3. **Transfer probability analysis:** The extracted transfer probabilities are consistent with GPV population, confirming the coherent enhancement expected from the collective nature of the mode.

4. **Width:** The observed widths of 1.2 and 1.9 MeV are consistent with theoretical predictions of 1-2 MeV for the GPV damping width.

5. **Supplementary measurement:** An independent experiment at 270 MeV beam energy confirmed the results, ruling out experimental artifacts.

### Validation of Particle-Hole Symmetry

The observation of the GPV validates the formal equivalence between particle-hole (shape) and particle-particle (pairing) excitations established by Broglia and co-workers. Just as giant dipole resonances concentrate electromagnetic strength through coherent particle-hole superpositions, the GPV concentrates pair-transfer strength through coherent two-particle superpositions.

### Why Light Nuclei Succeeded Where Heavy Nuclei Failed

The serendipitous observation in light nuclei, when the GPV had eluded detection in more "propitious" heavy nuclei like Pb, provides insight into the damping mechanisms of giant resonances. In heavy nuclei, the continuum effects are more severe: the two particles in the pairing mode at high energy escape easily into the continuum, while in light nuclei the mode is better confined. Additionally, Q-value mismatch effects suppress cross sections in heavy systems using conventional (p,t) and (t,p) reactions.

## Key Results

1. First experimental observation of the Giant Pairing Vibration, at $E_x = 19.9 \pm 0.1$ MeV in $^{14}$C and $E_x = 20.4 \pm 0.1$ MeV in $^{15}$C.
2. $L = 0$ monopole character confirmed by angular distributions and CRC calculations.
3. Widths of $1.2 \pm 0.3$ MeV ($^{14}$C) and $1.9 \pm 0.3$ MeV ($^{15}$C) consistent with theoretical predictions.
4. Transfer probabilities confirm collective GPV strength.
5. Results independently confirmed at 270 MeV beam energy.
6. Validates the particle-hole/particle-particle analogy in nuclear collective modes predicted by Broglia and Bes (1977).
