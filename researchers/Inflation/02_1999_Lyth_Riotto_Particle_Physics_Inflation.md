# Particle Physics Models of Inflation and the Cosmological Density Perturbation

**Author(s):** David H. Lyth, Antonio Riotto
**Year:** 1999
**Journal:** Physics Reports 314, 1-146 (1999)
**arXiv:** hep-ph/9807278
**Relevance:** HIGH -- comprehensive review of inflation model-building from the particle physics perspective; classifies models by potential shape and supersymmetric origin; derives spectral index predictions for dozens of models; essential reference for understanding the eta problem and the SUSY constraints that any fundamental inflationary model must satisfy

---

## Abstract

This is a review of particle-theory models of inflation, and of their predictions for the primordial density perturbation that is thought to be the origin of structure in the Universe. It contains mini-reviews of the relevant observational cosmology, of elementary field theory and of supersymmetry, that may be of interest in their own right. The spectral index n(k), specifying the scale-dependence of the spectrum of the curvature perturbation, will be a powerful discriminator between models, when it is measured by Planck with accuracy $\Delta n \sim 0.01$. The usual formula for n is derived, as well as its less familiar extension to the case of a multi-component inflaton; in both cases the key ingredient is the separate evolution of causally disconnected regions of the Universe. Primordial gravitational waves will be an even more powerful discriminator if they are observed, since most models of inflation predict that they are completely negligible. We treat in detail the new wave of models, which are firmly rooted in modern particle theory and have supersymmetry as a crucial ingredient.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### Section 1: Introduction (pp. 2-8)

Historical overview of inflation from Guth's "old inflation" (1981, tunneling from false vacuum) through "new inflation" (Linde 1982, Albrecht-Steinhardt 1982, rolling near potential maximum) to Linde's "chaotic inflation" (1983, monomial potentials with $\phi \gg M_P$). The crucial 1991-1994 development of **hybrid inflation** (Linde 1991): a second field $\psi$ ends inflation via a second-order phase transition while the inflaton $\phi$ remains sub-Planckian. The 1994 paper by Copeland, Liddle, Lyth, Stewart, and Wands identified that hybrid inflation typically has $\phi \ll M_P$, enabling contact with particle physics, but also emphasized that generic supergravity gives inflaton masses too large for inflation (the eta problem).

### Section 2: Observing the Density Perturbation (pp. 8-14)

The curvature perturbation $\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{k})$ is defined via $\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{k}) = \frac{1}{4}(a/k)^2 R^{(3)}(\mathbf{k})$ where $R^{(3)}$ is the spatial curvature scalar seen by comoving observers. It is time-independent during the primordial era (between horizon exit and re-entry). The spectrum is defined by $\langle g^*(\mathbf{k})g(\mathbf{k}')\rangle = \delta^3(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}')(2\pi^2/k^3)\mathcal{P}_g(k)$. The COBE normalization gives $\delta_H \equiv (2/5)\mathcal{P}_\mathcal{R}^{1/2} = 1.91 \times 10^{-5}$, and the spectral index is constrained to $n = 1.0 \pm 0.2$.

### Section 3: The Slow-Roll Paradigm (pp. 14-25)

The slow-roll parameters are $\epsilon \equiv \frac{1}{2}M_P^2(V'/V)^2$ and $\eta \equiv M_P^2 V''/V$, with flatness conditions $\epsilon \ll 1$, $|\eta| \ll 1$. The slow-roll approximation $\dot\phi = -V'/3H$ yields the scalar spectrum $\frac{4}{25}\mathcal{P}_\mathcal{R}(k) = \delta_H^2(k) = \frac{1}{75\pi^2 M_P^6}\frac{V^3}{V'^2} = \frac{1}{150\pi^2 M_P^4}\frac{V}{\epsilon}$. The spectral index to first order in slow-roll is $n - 1 = -6\epsilon + 2\eta$, with running $dn/d\ln k = 16\epsilon\eta - 24\epsilon^2 - 2\xi^2$ where $\xi^2 = M_P^4 V'V'''/V^2$. The gravitational wave spectrum is $\mathcal{P}_{\rm grav}(k) = \frac{2}{75\pi^2}\frac{V}{M_P^4}$ with tensor-to-scalar ratio $r = 12.4\epsilon$ (in their convention) and tensor spectral index $n_{\rm grav} = -2\epsilon$.

### Section 4: Calculating the Curvature Perturbation (pp. 25-36)

Derives the perturbation calculation from first principles using the separate universe approach: causally disconnected regions evolve independently after horizon exit. Extends to multi-component inflaton case where the trajectory in field space can curve, potentially generating isocurvature perturbations. The key result: $\mathcal{R}$ is constant on superhorizon scales for adiabatic perturbations.

### Section 5: Field Theory and the Potential (pp. 36-56)

Mini-review of quantum field theory relevant to inflation: renormalizable vs. non-renormalizable theories, internal symmetries, spontaneous symmetry breaking, the one-loop Coleman-Weinberg correction, non-perturbative effects. Crucially, the flatness requirements on the potential are $\epsilon \ll 1$ and $|\eta| \ll 1$, which translate to $V'^2/V \ll M_P^{-2}$ and $|V''| \ll V/M_P^2$ -- extremely flat potentials are needed. In supersymmetric theories, the F-term supergravity potential generically gives $m_\phi^2 \sim H^2$ (the eta problem), violating the flatness condition.

### Section 6: Forms for the Potential (pp. 56-80)

Systematic classification of potential forms with COBE normalizations and $n$ predictions. Key classes:
- **Monomial potentials** $V = \lambda_p \phi^p$: predict $n = 1 - (p+2)/(2N)$ and $r = 4p/N$
- **Inverted quadratic** $V = V_0(1 - \frac{1}{2}\eta_0\phi^2/M_P^2)$: predicts $n = 1 - \eta_0/N$ (for $\eta_0 \sim 1$)
- **Hybrid inflation** $V \approx V_0[1 + (\text{small correction})]$: allows $n$ close to 1 from either side
- **D-term inflation**: predicts $n = 0.96$--$0.98$
- **Running mass models**: can give strongly scale-dependent $n(k)$

### Section 7: Supersymmetry (pp. 80-106)

Comprehensive SUSY review for inflation: the SUSY algebra, global SUSY Lagrangian, F-term and D-term potentials, soft SUSY breaking, supergravity scalar potential $V = e^{K/M_P^2}[|D_i W|^2 K^{i\bar{j}} - 3|W|^2/M_P^2] + \frac{1}{2}D^a D^a$, and string-derived supergravity (moduli, dilaton, Horava-Witten M-theory).

### Sections 8-9: F-term and D-term Inflation (pp. 107-136)

F-term inflation: The generic supergravity F-term gives an inflaton mass $m_\phi^2 \sim V/M_P^2 \sim H^2$, making $|\eta| \sim 1$. Solutions include: (a) non-generic Kahler potential with cancellation, (b) shift symmetry protection, (c) running mass from quantum corrections. D-term inflation: Uses a Fayet-Iliopoulos D-term, which avoids the F-term mass problem. Predicts $n = 0.96$--$0.98$ but requires $\phi \gtrsim M_P$, creating problems with higher-order terms.

## Key Results

1. The COBE normalization constrains the inflationary potential: $V^{1/4}/\epsilon^{1/4} = 6.7 \times 10^{16}$ GeV.
2. The spectral index for single-field slow-roll: $n - 1 = -6\epsilon + 2\eta$.
3. The eta problem: generic supergravity gives $\Delta\eta \sim 1$, spoiling slow-roll regardless of energy scale.
4. Hybrid inflation allows $\phi \ll M_P$ during inflation, enabling particle physics model-building.
5. The spectral index is the most powerful observational discriminator between models at $\Delta n \sim 0.01$.
6. Most particle physics models predict negligible gravitational waves ($r \ll 0.01$).
7. D-term inflation predicts $n = 0.96$--$0.98$ but faces string theory embedding problems.
8. The running mass model can produce strongly scale-dependent $n(k)$.

## Relevance to Phonon-Exflation

This review is relevant to the exflation program in several ways. (1) The eta problem -- the generic supergravity inflaton mass $m_\phi^2 \sim H^2$ -- is the particle physics analog of the spectral action's CC overshoot: both arise from the sensitivity of scalar potentials to UV physics at the Planck scale. The exflation framework avoids this entirely because it has no fundamental scalar inflaton; the Jensen deformation parameter $\tau$ driving the transit is a geometric parameter of the spectral triple, not a quantum field subject to radiative corrections. (2) The review's systematic classification of $n_s$ predictions from different potential shapes provides a target list: the exflation prediction $n_s = 0.9561$ from gauge-invariant spectral geometry must be checked against all these model families. (3) The emphasis on hybrid inflation's sub-Planckian field values resonates with the exflation picture where all dynamics is internal (fiber-level), not super-Planckian. (4) The F-term vs. D-term distinction maps loosely onto the spectral action's division between $a_0$ (vacuum energy) and $a_2$ (gravity) Seeley-DeWitt coefficients, though the spectral action framework unifies them as different moments of the same Dirac spectrum.
