## Abstract

We report on the implications for cosmic inflation of the 2018 release of the Planck cosmic microwave background (CMB) anisotropy measurements. The results are fully consistent with those reported using the data from the two previous Planck cosmological releases, but have smaller uncertainties thanks to improvements in the characterization of polarization at low and high multipoles. Planck temperature, polarization, and lensing data determine the spectral index of scalar perturbations to be $n_s = 0.9649 \pm 0.0042$ at 68% CL. We find no evidence for a scale dependence of $n_s$, either as a running or as a running of the running. The Universe is found to be consistent with spatial flatness with a precision of 0.4% at 95% CL by combining Planck with a compilation of BAO data. The Planck 95% CL upper limit on the tensor-to-scalar ratio, $r_{0.002} < 0.10$, is further tightened by combining with the BICEP2/Keck Array BK15 data to obtain $r_{0.002} < 0.056$. In the framework of standard single-field inflationary models with Einstein gravity, these results imply that: (a) the predictions of slow-roll models with a concave potential, $V''(\phi) < 0$, are increasingly favoured by the data; and (b) based on two different methods for reconstructing the inflaton potential, we find no evidence for dynamics beyond slow roll. Three different methods for the non-parametric reconstruction of the primordial power spectrum consistently confirm a pure power law in the range of comoving scales $0.005\,\text{Mpc}^{-1} \lesssim k \lesssim 0.2\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$. A complementary analysis also finds no evidence for theoretically motivated parameterized features in the Planck power spectra. The new Planck polarization data provide a stringent test of the adiabaticity of the initial conditions for the cosmological fluctuations. In correlated, mixed adiabatic and isocurvature models, the non-adiabatic contribution to the observed CMB temperature variance is constrained to 1.3%, 1.7%, and 1.7% at 95% CL for cold dark matter, neutrino density, and neutrino velocity, respectively. All these findings support the key predictions of the standard single-field inflationary models, which will be further tested by future cosmological observations.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### Section 1: Introduction

The paper is the definitive Planck inflation analysis, updating PCI13 (2013 release) and PCI15 (2015 release). Planck measured 1,430,000 temperature modes (TT up to $\ell = 2500$) and 109,000 polarization modes (EE up to $\ell = 2000$), representing a factor of 7 (570) improvement over WMAP 9-year in temperature (polarization). The primordial power spectrum is parameterized as:
$$\ln \mathcal{P}_\mathcal{R}(k) = \ln A_s + (n_s - 1)\ln(k/k_*) + \frac{1}{2}\frac{dn_s}{d\ln k}\ln(k/k_*)^2 + \ldots$$
with default pivot scale $k_* = 0.05\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$.

### Section 3: Planck 2018 Results for Main Inflationary Observables

**3.1 Scalar spectral index $n_s$**:

The key results for the scalar spectral index in the base-$\Lambda$CDM model:

| Data combination | $n_s$ (68% CL) |
|:---|:---|
| Planck TT+lowE | $0.9626 \pm 0.0057$ |
| Planck TT,TE,EE+lowE | $0.9649 \pm 0.0044$ |
| Planck TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing | $0.9649 \pm 0.0042$ |
| Planck TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing+BAO | $0.9665 \pm 0.0038$ |

The optical depth is now $\tau = 0.0544 \pm 0.0073$ (TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing), significantly lower and more precise than the Planck 2015 value $\tau = 0.078 \pm 0.022$.

**3.2 Ruling out $n_s = 1$**: The Harrison-Zeldovich spectrum is ruled out at $8.4\sigma$ (TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing), with $\Delta\chi^2 = 72.4$. Simple one-parameter extensions (varying $N_{\text{eff}}$ or $\Omega_K$) are insufficient to reconcile $n_s = 1$ with the data.

**3.3 Running of $n_s$**: No evidence for scale dependence. The running is $dn_s/d\ln k = -0.0045 \pm 0.0067$ (68% CL, TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing). Running of the running is also consistent with zero.

**3.4 Spatial curvature**: Combining Planck with BAO: $\Omega_K = 0.0007 \pm 0.0019$ (68% CL), consistent with flat spatial sections at 0.4% precision (95% CL).

**3.5 Tensor-to-scalar ratio**: Planck alone gives $r_{0.002} < 0.10$ (95% CL, TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing). Combined with BK15: $r_{0.002} < 0.056$ (95% CL). This rules out $V(\phi) \propto \phi^2$ at high significance and increasingly favors concave potentials with $V''(\phi) < 0$.

### Section 4: Implications for Slow-Roll Models

Slow-roll parameters are constrained as:
- $\epsilon_V < 0.0044$ (95% CL, Planck TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing+BK15+BAO)
- $\eta_V = -0.0141^{+0.0074}_{-0.0075}$ (68% CL)
- $\xi_V^2 = 0.0044^{+0.0035}_{-0.0038}$ (68% CL)

Models ruled out or disfavored include: $V \propto \phi^2$ (ruled out), $V \propto \phi^{4/3}$, $V \propto \phi$ (marginally allowed), natural inflation with $f < 5M_{\text{pl}}$. Models favored include: $R^2$ (Starobinsky) inflation, Higgs inflation, $\alpha$-attractors.

### Sections 5-6: Potential Reconstruction and Power Spectrum Reconstruction

No evidence for dynamics beyond slow roll from two methods of potential reconstruction. Three independent methods for non-parametric power spectrum reconstruction confirm a pure power law over $0.005 \lesssim k \lesssim 0.2\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$.

### Sections 7-8: Features Search

No statistically significant primordial features found in the power spectrum. Combined power spectrum and bispectrum analysis finds no evidence for oscillatory features (logarithmic or linear in $k$).

### Section 9: Isocurvature Constraints

Non-adiabatic contribution to observed CMB temperature variance constrained to $<1.3\%$ (CDM), $<1.7\%$ (neutrino density), $<1.7\%$ (neutrino velocity) at 95% CL.

### Section 11: Conclusions

All findings support the key predictions of standard single-field slow-roll inflation: adiabatic, Gaussian, nearly scale-invariant scalar perturbations with a red tilt. The data favor concave potentials.

## Key Results

1. $n_s = 0.9649 \pm 0.0042$ (68% CL, Planck TT,TE,EE+lowE+lensing) -- the most precise determination of the scalar spectral index.
2. $n_s = 1$ (Harrison-Zeldovich) ruled out at $8.4\sigma$.
3. $r_{0.002} < 0.056$ (95% CL, Planck+BK15) -- strong upper limit on tensor-to-scalar ratio.
4. No evidence for running: $dn_s/d\ln k = -0.0045 \pm 0.0067$ (68% CL).
5. Spatial flatness confirmed: $\Omega_K = 0.0007 \pm 0.0019$ (68% CL, Planck+BAO).
6. $\tau = 0.0544 \pm 0.0073$ -- improved optical depth determination.
7. Concave potentials ($V'' < 0$) increasingly favored; $V \propto \phi^2$ ruled out.
8. Pure power-law primordial spectrum confirmed over $0.005 \lesssim k \lesssim 0.2\,\text{Mpc}^{-1}$.
9. Non-adiabatic contributions to CMB variance $< 1.7\%$ at 95% CL.
10. No evidence for primordial features or dynamics beyond slow roll.
