## Key Results

1. Announced the discovery that black holes emit thermal radiation at a temperature inversely proportional to their mass: $T = \hbar c^3 / (8\pi G M k_B)$.
2. This brief Nature letter preceded the full derivation in the 1975 CMP paper (Paper 05). It presented the result and its implications without the complete Bogoliubov coefficient calculation.
3. Argued that small black holes ($M \lesssim 10^{15}$ g, formed in the early universe) would have temperatures exceeding $10^{11}$ K and would emit strongly, producing a "white hole"-like explosion at end of life.
4. Promoted the Bardeen-Carter-Hawking thermodynamic analogy to a physical identity: $\kappa/(2\pi)$ IS the temperature, not merely analogous to it.
5. Raised the information problem for the first time: if a black hole evaporates completely, what happens to the information that fell in?
