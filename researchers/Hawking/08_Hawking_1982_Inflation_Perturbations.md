## Key Results

1. Computed the spectrum of density perturbations generated during inflation from quantum fluctuations of the scalar field. This was one of the first calculations of inflationary perturbations (simultaneously with Starobinsky 1982, Guth & Pi 1982, and Bardeen, Steinhardt & Turner 1983).
2. Showed that the perturbation spectrum is approximately scale-invariant (Harrison-Zeldovich), with amplitude $\delta\rho/\rho \sim H^2/\dot{\phi}$ at horizon crossing.
3. Applied the calculation to the "new inflation" scenario (single bubble, slow-roll), where the scalar field rolls slowly from a false vacuum.
4. The perturbation amplitude is determined by the inflationary potential: $\frac{\delta\rho}{\rho} \sim \frac{H^2}{\dot{\phi}} \sim \frac{V^{3/2}}{M_P^3 V'}$ evaluated at horizon exit.

## Relevance to Phonon-Exflation

The phonon-exflation framework does not use a classical inflaton field, so the standard Hawking 1982 perturbation calculation does not directly apply. However, the framework must reproduce an approximately scale-invariant perturbation spectrum to match CMB observations. The framework's analog is the Kibble-Zurek mechanism during the tau-transit: the instanton gas produces density perturbations through pair creation rather than through inflaton quantum fluctuations. The amplitude formula $\delta\rho/\rho \sim H^2/\dot{\phi}$ sets the quantitative target that any alternative perturbation mechanism must match.
