# Observation of Thermal Hawking Radiation and its Temperature in an Analogue Black Hole

**Author(s):** Juan Ramon Munoz de Nova, Katrine Golubkov, Victor I. Kolobov, and Jeff Steinhauer
**Year:** 2019
**Journal:** Nature Physics (preprint: arXiv:1809.00913)
**arXiv:** 1809.00913
**Relevance:** HIGH

---

## Abstract

We measure the correlation spectrum of the Hawking radiation emitted by an analogue black hole and find it to be thermal at the Hawking temperature implied by the analogue surface gravity. The Hawking radiation is in the regime of linear dispersion, in analogy with a real black hole. Furthermore, the radiation inside of the black hole is seen to be composed of negative-energy partners only. This work confirms the prediction of Hawking's theory regarding the value of the Hawking temperature, as well as the thermality of the spectrum. The thermality of Hawking radiation is the root of the information paradox. The correlations between the Hawking and partner particles imply that the analogue black hole has no analogue firewall.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### Experimental Setup

The analogue black hole consists of a flowing Bose-Einstein condensate of 8000 $^{87}$Rb atoms confined in a focused laser beam ($\lambda = 812$ nm, waist = 3.9 $\mu$m). A blue-detuned laser ($\lambda = 442$ nm) creates a downward potential step (waterfall potential) that accelerates the condensate to supersonic speeds. In the supersonic region ($x > 0$), a phonon traveling toward the step in the comoving frame travels away in the lab frame -- unable to reach the horizon, analogous to the interior of a black hole.

### Hawking Temperature Measurement

For a stationary 1D flow with $nv$ = constant, the Hawking temperature is:
$$k_B T_H = -\frac{\hbar}{2\pi}\left(\frac{c}{n}\frac{dn}{dx} + \frac{dc}{dx}\right)\bigg|_{x=0}$$
evaluated at the sonic horizon. The measured average Hawking temperature is 0.35 nK, or $0.12\,mc_{\text{out}}^2$.

### Correlation Measurement

Hawking radiation is observed via the density-density correlation function $G^{(2)}(x,x') = \sqrt{\xi_{\text{out}}\xi_{\text{in}}} / (n_{\text{out}} n_{\text{in}}) \langle \delta n(x) \delta n(x') \rangle$. The correlations between Hawking and partner modes $\langle \hat{b}_H \hat{b}_P \rangle$ are extracted by Fourier transform. This observable is largely free of background because it represents correlations between inside and outside the black hole.

### Thermal Spectrum Verification

Using the Bogoliubov transformation $\hat{b}_H = \alpha \hat{b}_+ + \beta \hat{b}_-^\dagger$, the Planck distribution $|\beta|^2 = 1/(e^{\hbar\omega/k_BT_H} - 1)$ gives the predicted correlation spectrum $S_0^2(|\beta|^2 + 1)|\beta|^2$. The measured correlation spectrum agrees with this thermal prediction with no free parameters.

### Dispersion Relations

Measured via an oscillating horizon technique. The Hawking radiation lies in the linear-dispersion regime, confirming the analogy with massless particles near a real black hole. The partner mode has negative energy inside the black hole, as predicted.

### No Firewall

The correlations between Hawking and partner particles are at the predicted magnitude, implying no analogue firewall at the horizon that would reduce these correlations.

## Key Results

1. First quantitative confirmation of the Hawking temperature from surface gravity: $T_H = 0.35$ nK measured in BEC analog
2. Correlation spectrum of Hawking radiation is thermal (Planckian) with no free parameters
3. Radiation is in the linear-dispersion regime, analogous to massless particles near a real black hole
4. Partner modes inside the analogue black hole carry negative energy only
5. No analogue firewall observed -- Hawking/partner correlations are at predicted magnitude
6. Experiment repeated 7400 times to build statistical ensemble
