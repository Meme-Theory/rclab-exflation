## Abstract

Superconductivity with transition temperature $T_c = 1.7$ K has been observed in bilayer graphene. The main factors which may shed light on the mechanism of the formation of this superconductivity are the following. Superconductivity is observed in bilayer graphene, when the two layers are twisted, and the maximum of $T_c$ takes place at the "magic angle" of twist, at which the electronic band structure becomes nearly flat. Actually the same factors have been suggested to explain experiments in graphite which reported high-T superconductivity in highly oriented pyrolytic graphite (HOPG). The dispersionless energy spectrum has a singular density of states, which provides the transition temperature being proportional to the coupling constant instead of the exponential suppression, as was suggested by Khodel and Shaginyan.

---

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### Flat Band Mechanism
In conventional BCS superconductivity, $T_c \propto \exp(-1/\lambda)$ with exponential suppression. For flat band systems with dispersionless spectrum and singular DOS, Khodel and Shaginyan showed that:

$$T_c \propto \lambda \quad \text{(linear in coupling constant)}$$

This dramatic enhancement was first found for nuclear systems by Belyaev (1961).

### Sources of Flat Band Formation
Three mechanisms are identified:
1. **Topology**: Band-touching lines (Dirac lines) in graphite spectrum lead to approximate flat bands at surfaces/interfaces
2. **Symmetry**: Moire bands in twisted bilayer graphene (Bistritzer-MacDonald)
3. **Interactions**: Khodel-Shaginyan fermion condensate — merging of levels due to electron-electron interaction

### Graphite Connection
The twist of graphene layers or HOPG domains provides the topological mechanism. Misfit dislocation arrays at interfaces create pseudo-magnetic fields that flatten the spectrum. Hints of room-temperature superconductivity appear only when samples contain quasi-2D interfaces between twisted HOPG domains.

## Key Results

1. Flat band superconductivity has $T_c \propto \lambda$ (linear) vs conventional $T_c \propto e^{-1/\lambda}$ (exponential)
2. Magic-angle twisted bilayer graphene confirms the flat band mechanism
3. Three routes to flat bands: topological, symmetry-protected, interaction-driven
4. Graphite interfaces between twisted domains are natural flat band systems
5. Room-temperature superconductivity in graphite materials remains a viable target
