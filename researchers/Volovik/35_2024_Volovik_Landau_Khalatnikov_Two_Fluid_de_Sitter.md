## Abstract

Condensed matter analogs are useful when considering phenomena associated with the quantum vacuum. This is because in condensed matter, we know physics both in the infrared and in the ultraviolet limits, whereas in particle physics and gravity, physics on the trans-Planckian scale is unknown. One of the cornerstones of the connection between non-relativistic condensed matter and modern relativistic theories is the two-fluid hydrodynamics of superfluid helium, developed by Landau and Khalatnikov. The dynamics and thermodynamics of the de Sitter state of the expansion of the Universe bear some features of a multi-fluid system. There are actually three components: the quantum vacuum, the gravitational component and relativistic matter. The expanding de Sitter vacuum serves as a thermal bath with local temperature, which is twice the Gibbons-Hawking temperature associated with the temperature of the cosmological horizon. This local temperature leads to the heating of the matter component and the gravitational component, which behaves as Zel'dovich stiff matter and represents dark matter. In equilibrium and in the absence of ordinary matter, the positive partial pressure of the dark matter compensates the negative partial pressure of the quantum vacuum. That is why, in full equilibrium, the total pressure is zero. This is rather similar to the superfluid and normal components of superfluid liquid, which together produce a zero pressure of the liquid in the absence of environment. We propose the phenomenological theory, which describes the dynamics of dark energy and dark matter. If one assumes that, in the dynamics, gravitational dark matter behaves like real Zel'dovich stiff matter, it is shown that both components undergo power law decay due to the energy exchange between these components. It then follows that their values at the present time are of the correct order of magnitude. We also consider other problems through the prism of condensed matter physics, including black holes and the Planck constant.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### II. Landau-Khalatnikov Two-Fluid Hydrodynamics and Analogue Gravity

#### A. River model
The superfluid velocity $v_s$ provides a "flowing quantum vacuum" with acoustic metric $-g_{00}(r) = 1 - v_s^2(r)/c^2$. The Tolman law $T(r) = T\sqrt{-g_{00}(r)}$ appears already in Khalatnikov's book. The Painleve-Gullstrand metric for black holes corresponds to vacuum flow $v_s^2 = 2GM/r$.

#### B-D. Emergent gravity
Three routes: (B) Weyl point topology in fermionic materials, (C) elasticity tetrads from superplastic vacuum with $[E^a_\mu] = [L]^{-1}$, (D) bilinear composite tetrads from $^3$He-B.

### III. de Sitter State as Heat Bath

#### B. Hydrogen atom ionization
An electron at the $n$-th level in a hydrogen atom in de Sitter background can escape via WKB tunneling with rate $w \sim \exp(-\pi E_n/\hbar H) = \exp(-E_n/T)$, giving $T = \hbar H/\pi = 2T_{\text{GH}}$.

#### C. Proton decay
The proton inverse $\beta$-decay rate in de Sitter: $\Gamma \sim \exp(-\pi\Delta M/H) = \exp(-\Delta M/T)$. A single proton triggers instability of de Sitter vacuum through multiple matter creation.

#### D-E. Local thermodynamics and holography
Vacuum energy $\epsilon_{\text{vac}} = (3\pi/8G)T^2$, entropy density $s_{\text{dS}} = (3\pi/4G)T = (3/4G)H$. Total entropy in Hubble volume equals $A/(4G)$ (bulk-boundary correspondence).

### IV. Two-Component Thermodynamics

#### A. Modified Gibbs-Duhem relation
The gravitational degrees of freedom (conjugate pair $K = 1/(16\pi G)$ and $\mathcal{R}$) modify the Gibbs-Duhem relation: $Ts_{\text{dS}} = \epsilon_{\text{vac}} + P_{\text{vac}} - K\mathcal{R}$. This defines a dark matter pressure $P_{\text{DM}} = P_{\text{vac}} - K\mathcal{R}$. The dark matter energy density equals the vacuum energy density: $\epsilon_{\text{DM}} = \epsilon_{\text{DE}}$.

#### B-C. Zel'dovich stiff matter and Fermi liquid
The dark matter component satisfies $P = w\epsilon$ with $w = 1$ (stiff matter). This is the same equation of state as Landau Fermi liquid. The two-component de Sitter state mirrors the superfluid/normal component decomposition in superfluid helium.

### V. Two-Component Dynamics

#### A-C. Phenomenological theory
Without energy exchange: standard $\rho \propto a^{-3(1+w)}$ decay. With energy exchange between dark energy and dark matter (gravitational stiff matter), both undergo power-law decay. Both components reach correct present-day magnitudes.

### VI-VII. Black Holes and Unruh Effect
Hawking radiation as quantum tunneling across the horizon. Non-extensive Tsallis-Cirto entropy for black holes. Double Unruh temperature from combined Schwinger + Unruh processes.

### VIII. Cosmological Constant Problems
The main lesson from condensed matter: fully equilibrium quantum vacuum has exactly zero vacuum energy. The de Sitter state is unstable because it serves as a heat bath creating matter, which promotes relaxation to Minkowski vacuum with $\Lambda = 0$.

### IX. Planck Constants and Dimensionless Physics
Two Planck constants: $\hbar$ (dimension of time) and $\hslash$ (dimension of length). In the acoustic metric of $^4$He, $\hslash_{\text{ac}}$ has dimension of length and is on the order of the interatomic distance. This supports $\hslash \sim l_P \sim G$.

## Key Results

1. The de Sitter vacuum is a thermal bath at $T = H/\pi = 2T_{\text{GH}}$, demonstrated by hydrogen ionization and proton decay rates
2. The de Sitter state has three components: dark energy (vacuum), gravitational dark matter (stiff matter, $w = 1$), and ordinary matter
3. In equilibrium, positive pressure of dark matter compensates negative vacuum pressure (zero total pressure), analogous to superfluid + normal components
4. With energy exchange, both dark components undergo power-law decay reaching correct present-day values
5. A single proton triggers de Sitter instability through cascading matter creation
6. Bulk entropy in Hubble volume = Gibbons-Hawking horizon entropy (holographic correspondence)
7. Two Planck constants $(\hbar, \hslash)$ emerge as elements of Minkowski metric tetrads
