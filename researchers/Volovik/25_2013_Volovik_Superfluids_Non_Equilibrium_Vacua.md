## Abstract

We discuss phenomenology of quantum vacuum. Phenomenology of macroscopic systems has three sources: thermodynamics, topology and symmetry. Thermodynamics of the self-sustained vacuum allows us to treat the problems related to the vacuum energy: the cosmological constant problems. The natural value of the energy density of the equilibrium self-sustained vacuum is zero. Cosmology is discussed as the process of relaxation of vacuum towards the equilibrium state. The present value of the cosmological constant is very small compared to the Planck scale, because the present Universe is very old and thus is close to equilibrium.

Momentum space topology determines the universality classes of fermionic vacua. The Standard Model vacuum both in its massless and massive states is topological medium. The vacuum in its massless state shares the properties of superfluid 3He-A, which is topological superfluid. It belongs to the Fermi-point universality class, which has topologically protected fermionic quasiparticles. At low energy they behave as relativistic massless Weyl fermions. Gauge fields and gravity emerge together with Weyl fermions at low energy. This allows us to treat the hierarchy problem in Standard Model: the masses of elementary particles are very small compared to the Planck scale because the natural value of the quark and lepton masses is zero. The small nonzero masses appear in the infrared region, where the quantum vacuum acquires the properties of another topological superfluid, 3He-B, and 3+1 topological insulators. The other topological media in dimensions 2+1 and 3+1 are also discussed. In most cases, topology is supported by discrete symmetry of the underlying microscopic system, which indicates the important role of discrete symmetry in Standard Model.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### I. Introduction: Phenomenology of quantum vacuum

The quantum vacuum is treated as a macroscopic many-body system with the characteristic energy scale being the Planck energy E_P ~ 10^{19} GeV. Three sources govern low-energy phenomenology: thermodynamics, symmetry, and topology. The vacuum has two components: the ground state itself and matter (fermionic/bosonic quasiparticles above the ground state).

Topology provides the anti-GUT approach: "fundamental" symmetry and fields gradually emerge when the Planck scale is approached from above. Fermions are primary objects; gauge fields and gravity are secondary, emerging together with Weyl fermions at low energy.

The Standard Model vacuum is topologically nontrivial in both its massless state (analogous to 3He-A, Fermi-point universality class) and massive state (analogous to 3He-B and 3+1 topological insulators).

### II. Quantum vacuum as self-sustained medium

The cosmological constant problem: naive zero-point energy estimation gives rho_vac ~ E_P^4 ~ 10^{120} times the observed value. Resolution comes from treating the vacuum as a self-sustained medium (like a quantum liquid droplet at T=0 in empty space).

The vacuum is characterized by a conserved charge q (analogous to particle density n in quantum liquids). Several Lorentz-invariant realizations are presented:

1. **Yang-Mills chiral condensate**: q = <F-tilde_mu-nu F^{mu-nu}>. The cosmological constant is NOT the vacuum energy epsilon(q), but the thermodynamic potential: Lambda = rho_vac = epsilon(q) - q d(epsilon)/dq. In the Gibbs-Duhem relation at T=0, this equals minus pressure. For a self-sustained system in the absence of environment, P=0 and Lambda=0.

2. **4-form field**: F_{alpha-beta-gamma-delta} = q e_{alpha-beta-gamma-delta}/sqrt(-det g). The Maxwell equation reduces to d(epsilon)/dq = mu (integration constant = chemical potential). The cosmological constant is again Lambda = epsilon(q) - mu q.

3. **Aether field**: u^mu(x) with observable derivatives u^mu_nu = nabla_nu u^mu. The Lorentz-invariant vacuum solution is u^mu_nu = q g^{mu-nu}.

All three realizations give the same universal thermodynamic description: the equilibrium condition is epsilon(q_0) - q_0 d(epsilon)/dq_0 = 0, with positive vacuum compressibility chi(q_0) > 0 for stability.

### III. Thermodynamics of quantum vacuum

The vacuum energy epsilon(q_0) has Planck-scale magnitude (dictated by microscopic physics), but the gravitating energy rho_vac = epsilon - mu q = 0 in equilibrium. This is not fine-tuning but thermodynamic necessity for any self-sustained system.

Vacuum compressibility: chi^{-1}_vac = q^2 d^2(epsilon)/dq^2 > 0 (stability condition).

Thermal fluctuations of Lambda: <(Delta Lambda)^2> = T/(V chi_vac). Requiring fluctuations below the observed Lambda implies V > 10^{28} V_H (volume far exceeds Hubble volume).

### IV. Dynamics of quantum vacuum

The full action combines the q-field, gravity, and matter:

S = -integral d^4x sqrt(|g|) [R/(16 pi G(q)) + epsilon(q) + L_M(q, psi)]

The generalized Einstein equations contain the cosmological "constant" Lambda = epsilon(q) - mu q, which is now dynamical. The Weinberg fine-tuning problem is bypassed because the vacuum variable q has a conservation law yielding d(epsilon)/dq = mu as an integration constant (parameter of the solution, not the action).

Multiple Minkowski vacua exist for any bare cosmological constant Lambda_bare: infinitely many equilibrium values q_0 compensate Lambda_bare via the q-field. Each vacuum has different G(q_0) and Standard Model parameters but all have zero cosmological constant.

### V. Cosmology as approach to equilibrium

After a kick displacing q from equilibrium, the vacuum relaxes back. At late times:

q(t) - q_0 ~ q_0 sin(omega t)/(omega t), where omega ~ E_P

The vacuum energy relaxes as rho_vac(t) ~ omega^2/t^2 sin^2(omega t). At present time: rho_vac(t_present) ~ E_P^2/t_present^2 ~ E_P^2 H^2, approximately matching the observed cosmological constant.

The Minkowski vacuum is an attractor: in the aether-field realization, the chemical potential mu is NOT an integration constant, so de Sitter solutions naturally relax to Minkowski without fine-tuning. The only condition is positive vacuum compressibility.

A realistic scenario involves step-wise relaxation: vacuum energy moves from plateau to plateau responding to Standard Model phase transitions, following the decreasing matter energy density. The present small remnant Lambda may originate from neutrino sector anomalies, QCD anomaly, torsion, or electroweak crossover relaxation effects.

### VI-VII. Vacuum as topological medium / Semi-metal state

Four universality classes of gapless fermionic vacua:
1. **Fermi points** (co-dimension 3): 3He-A, Weyl semimetals, SM above electroweak transition. Emergent Weyl fermions, gauge fields, and gravity at low energy.
2. **Fermi lines/points** (co-dimension 2): d-wave superconductors, graphene. Topology protected by discrete symmetry.
3. **Fermi surfaces** (co-dimension 1): normal metals, normal 3He. Emergent Landau Fermi liquid theory.
4. **Fermi bands** (co-dimension 0): flat bands on surfaces of materials with bulk nodal lines.

Near a Fermi point with topological charge N_3 = +/-1, the Green's function expands as:

G^{-1}(p_mu) = e^beta_alpha Gamma^alpha (p_beta - p^{(0)}_beta) + ...

The expansion parameters e^beta_alpha (vierbein = emergent gravity) and p^{(0)}_beta (gauge field) emerge as dynamical collective bosonic fields. For N_3 > 1, the shift p^{(0)}_beta becomes a matrix field giving SU(N_3) non-Abelian gauge fields.

### VIII. Vacuum in state of topological insulator

3He-B and the massive Standard Model vacuum are both 3+1 fully gapped topological media characterized by the invariant:

N_K = (e_{ijk}/24 pi^2) tr[integral d^3p K G (d_{pi} G^{-1}) G (d_{pj} G^{-1}) G (d_{pk} G^{-1})]

where K = tau_2 (combination of time-reversal and particle-hole symmetries; for SM, K = gamma_5 gamma_0).

Phase diagram in the (mu, 1/m) plane: topological quantum phase transition between weak-coupling 3He-B (N_K = 2) and strong-coupling 3He-B (N_K = 0) when mu crosses zero, equivalent to the Dirac mass sign change.

### IX. Discussion

Topology is primary in the anti-GUT scheme: it protects massless fermions, produces emergent symmetries (Lorentz invariance, gauge symmetry), and determines universality classes. Discrete symmetry plays a crucial supporting role -- it prohibits cancellation of Fermi points with opposite topological charges. The underlying Z_2 symmetry produces effective SU(2) gauge fields.

The gravitating energy of the vacuum is strictly zero in ANY self-sustained equilibrium vacuum (gapless or gapped, topological or trivial) -- this is a thermodynamic consequence independent of vacuum topology. Cosmology is the process of relaxation toward equilibrium.

## Key Results

1. The cosmological constant problem is resolved by treating the vacuum as a self-sustained medium: the gravitating energy is NOT epsilon(q) but the thermodynamic potential epsilon(q) - mu q, which is zero in equilibrium by thermodynamic necessity
2. The vacuum variable q satisfies a conservation law, making mu an integration constant (parameter of the solution, not the action), bypassing Weinberg's fine-tuning problem
3. Infinitely many Minkowski vacua exist for any bare Lambda_bare, each with different G and SM parameters but all with Lambda = 0
4. Cosmology is the process of relaxation to equilibrium; rho_vac(t) ~ E_P^2 H^2 at late times, matching observations
5. The Minkowski vacuum is an attractor (proven in the Dolgov/aether-field realization) -- no fine-tuning of integration constants needed
6. The Standard Model vacuum is topologically nontrivial in both massless (Fermi-point class, like 3He-A) and massive (topological insulator class, like 3He-B) states
7. Gauge fields and gravity emerge as collective bosonic modes near Fermi points, with the vierbein e^beta_alpha and gauge potential p^{(0)}_beta as expansion parameters
8. Discrete symmetry of the microscopic vacuum is essential: it supports topological invariants, prohibits Fermi point cancellation, and generates effective non-Abelian gauge fields (Z_2 -> SU(2))
9. Thermal fluctuations of Lambda require the real volume of the Universe to exceed the Hubble volume by a factor > 10^{28}
