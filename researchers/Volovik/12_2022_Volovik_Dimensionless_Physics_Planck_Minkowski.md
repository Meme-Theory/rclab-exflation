**Author(s):** G.E. Volovik
**Year:** 2022
**Journal:** arXiv preprint (ERC Grant Agreement No. 694248)
**arXiv:** 2209.15426
**Relevance:** HIGH

## Abstract

Diakonov theory of quantum gravity, in which tetrads emerge as the bilinear combinations of the fermionic fields, suggests that in general relativity the metric may have dimension 2, i.e. [g_mu_nu] = 1/[L]^2. Several other approaches to quantum gravity, including the model of superplastic vacuum and BF-theories of gravity support this suggestion. The important consequence of such metric dimension is that all the diffeomorphism invariant quantities are dimensionless for any dimension of spacetime. These include the action S, interval s, cosmological constant Lambda, scalar curvature R, scalar field Phi, etc. Here we are trying to further exploit the Diakonov idea, and consider the dimension of the Planck constant. The application of the Diakonov theory suggests that the Planck constant hbar is the parameter of the Minkowski metric. The Minkowski parameter hbar is invariant only under Lorentz transformations, and is not diffeomorphism invariant. As a result the Planck constant hbar has nonzero dimension -- the dimension of length [L]. Whether this Planck constant length is related to the Planck length scale, is an open question. In principle there can be different Minkowski vacua with their own values of the parameter hbar. Then in the thermal contact between the two vacua their temperatures obey the analog of the Tolman law: hbar_1/T_1 = hbar_2/T_2.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### Dimensional tetrads (Diakonov theory)

Tetrads emerge as bilinear combinations of fermionic fields (Diakonov): E^a_mu = (1/2)(Psi^dagger gamma^a d_mu Psi - Psi^dagger d_mu gamma^a Psi). Since the fermionic fields Psi are dimensionless, tetrads have dimension [E^a_mu] = 1/[L], the same as gauge fields. The metric g_mu_nu = eta_ab E^a_mu E^b_nu then has dimension 1/[L]^2.

### All diffeomorphism invariants are dimensionless

The interval ds^2 = g_mu_nu dx^mu dx^nu is dimensionless: [1/L^2][L^2] = [1]. This propagates universally: action S, mass M, cosmological constant Lambda, scalar curvature R, scalar field Phi -- all dimensionless for any spacetime dimension N.

### Planck constant as Minkowski metric element

In flat Minkowski spacetime, the metric becomes ds^2_Mink = (1/hbar^2)(-dt^2 + dr^2), identifying hbar = 1/sqrt(-g^Mink_00). The Planck constant has dimension of length [L], is Lorentz-invariant but NOT diffeomorphism-invariant. It is a property of a specific Minkowski vacuum, not a fundamental constant.

### Schrodinger equation from classical fields

The Klein-Gordon equation in Minkowski spacetime, expanded in 1/M, yields the Schrodinger equation i*hbar*d_t*psi = -(hbar^2/2M)*nabla^2*psi directly, where hbar enters only through the Minkowski metric elements. Quantum mechanics emerges from classical relativistic field theory in dimensional metric.

### Multiple Minkowski vacua and Tolman law

Different Minkowski vacua can have different values of hbar. In thermal contact, the Tolman law gives hbar_1/T_1 = hbar_2/T_2. Cosmological domain walls between vacua with different signs of hbar are possible.

### Newton constant and Planck length

Both G and hbar have dimension [L]. The Planck mass M_P = sqrt(hbar/G) is dimensionless. Neither G nor hbar enters the gravitational action (eq. 36) -- both are non-fundamental. The Planck length l_P = sqrt(hbar*G) has dimension [L], same as hbar itself.

## Key Results

1. All diffeomorphism-invariant quantities (action, mass, Lambda, R, Phi) are dimensionless in any spacetime dimension
2. Planck constant hbar is an element of the Minkowski metric with dimension [L], not a fundamental constant
3. Schrodinger equation emerges directly from classical scalar field action with dimensional metric
4. Newton constant G also has dimension [L]; Planck mass M_P = sqrt(hbar/G) is dimensionless
5. Multiple Minkowski vacua with different hbar values can coexist, obeying Tolman law hbar_1/T_1 = hbar_2/T_2
6. De Sitter Hawking temperature T = hbar*H/(2*pi) follows from metric structure
7. Commutation relations [k_i, x_j] = i*delta^j_i contain no hbar -- phase space volume is dimensionless

## Relevance to Phonon-Exflation

This paper provides a key conceptual bridge: the Planck constant is not fundamental but is an element of the Minkowski vacuum metric. In the phonon-exflation framework where spacetime emerges from a superfluid substrate, the effective hbar would be determined by the substrate's ground-state metric -- connecting directly to the elasticity tetrad formalism (papers 20-21). The possibility of multiple Minkowski vacua with different hbar values is directly relevant to the framework's transit scenario: the pre-transit and post-transit vacua could have different effective Planck constants, with the Tolman law governing their thermal contact. The dimensionless physics program also simplifies the framework's vacuum energy analysis -- Lambda is dimensionless and enters the action without hbar or G prefactors.
