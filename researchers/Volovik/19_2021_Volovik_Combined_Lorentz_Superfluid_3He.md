## Abstract

We consider the possibility of the scenario in which the P, T and Lorentz symmetry of the relativistic quantum vacuum are all the combined symmetries. These symmetries emerge as a result of the symmetry breaking of the more fundamental P, T and Lorentz symmetries of the original vacuum, which is invariant under separate groups of the coordinate transformations and spin rotations. The condensed matter vacua suggest two possible scenarios: the $^3$He-A scenario (emergent combined symmetry from topology) and the $^3$He-B scenario (combined symmetry from broken symmetry).

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### Two Scenarios for Combined Lorentz Symmetry

**$^3$He-A scenario (topological):** Weyl points in the quasiparticle spectrum are topologically protected. Combined Lorentz symmetry emerges together with tetrads and Weyl fermions in the low-energy sector. No separate Lorentz symmetries exist microscopically (physics is Galilean at the atomic scale).

**$^3$He-B scenario (symmetry breaking):** The original vacuum has separate coordinate and spin rotation symmetries: $G = SO(3)_c \times SO(3)_s$. In the B-phase, this breaks to the diagonal: $SO(3)_c \times SO(3)_s \to SO(3)_J$. The order parameter $e^i_a$ (triad) breaks both rotational symmetries but preserves combined rotations.

### Extension to Relativistic Physics
The $^3$He-B pattern extends to 3+1 Minkowski vacuum:

$$L_c \times L_s \to L_J$$

with the full symmetry breaking scheme:

$$G = L_c \times P_c \times T_c \times L_s \times P_s \times T_s \to H = L \times P \times T$$

where $L = L_c L_s$, $P = P_c P_s$, $T = T_c T_s$ are all combined symmetries.

### Degenerate Minkowski Vacua
The homotopy groups of the degenerate vacuum manifold $G/H = O(3,1)$:
- $\pi_0(G/H) = \mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$: three types of cosmic domain walls
- $\pi_1(G/H) = \mathbb{Z}_2$: torsion strings (analog of spin-orbital vortices in $^3$He-B)

### Torsion Strings
With axisymmetric tetrads $e^0 = dt, e^3 = dz, e^1 = dr, e^2 = r\,d\phi$, the metric is flat ($g_{ik} = -\delta_{ik}$) but the curvature 2-form has a delta-function singularity on the string axis: $R^{12}_{ik} = e_{ikl} \hat{z}_l \delta^2(r)$.

### Multiple Tetrads for Weyl Fermions
Since Weyl fermions are primary (Dirac are composite), four different tetrads are needed for the four types of Weyl fermions: $e^\mu_{aR}$, $e^\mu_{aL} = P_s e^\mu_a$, $e^\mu_{a\bar{R}} = P_s T_s e^\mu_a$, $e^\mu_{a\bar{L}} = T_s e^\mu_a$.

### Cosmological Constant from Tetrads
Using the tetrad determinant $e = \det e^a_\mu$ as the vacuum variable in q-theory:

$$\rho_{\text{vac}}(e) = \epsilon(e) + e \frac{d\epsilon}{de}$$

The equilibrium condition $\rho_{\text{vac}}(e_{\text{eq}}) = 0$ nullifies the CC. The extension to imaginary $e = i\beta$ connects to thermodynamics: $\beta$ becomes inverse temperature, and $\rho_{\text{vac}} = d(S)/d\beta = 0$ in equilibrium.

## Key Results

1. P, T, and Lorentz symmetry are all combined symmetries, broken from separate coordinate and spin symmetries
2. Minkowski vacuum is degenerate: $\pi_0 = \mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ (domain walls), $\pi_1 = \mathbb{Z}_2$ (torsion strings)
3. Torsion strings have curvature singularity but flat metric — the curvature is in the connection, not the metric
4. Four tetrad fields for four Weyl fermion types; parity violation could break equivalence principle
5. Tetrad determinant serves as q-variable; CC nullified in equilibrium
6. Connection between gravity and thermodynamics: Minkowski vacuum from finite-temperature 4+0 system
