## Abstract

The type-II Weyl and type-II Dirac points emerge in semimetals and also in relativistic systems. In particular, the type-II Weyl fermions may emerge behind the event horizon of black holes. In this case the horizon with Painleve-Gullstrand metric serves as the surface of the Lifshitz transition. This relativistic analogy allows us to simulate the black hole horizon and Hawking radiation using the fermionic superfluid with supercritical velocity, and the Dirac and Weyl semimetals with the interface separating the type-I and type-II states.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### Emergent Weyl Fermions and Tetrads
The general Hamiltonian for emergent Weyl fermions near a topologically protected Weyl point $\mathbf{p}^{(0)}$ with Chern number $N_3 = \pm 1$:

$$H = e^j_k (p_j - p^{(0)}_j) \hat{\sigma}_k + e^j_0 (p_j - p^{(0)}_j)$$

The position $\mathbf{p}^{(0)}(\mathbf{r}, t)$ acts as an emergent U(1) gauge field. The parameters $e^j_k$ and $e^j_0$ are emergent tetrad fields describing gravity experienced by Weyl fermions.

### Type-I vs Type-II Weyl Points
- **Type-I** ($|e^j_0 [e^{-1}]^k_j| < 1$): conventional Weyl cone (possibly tilted)
- **Type-II** ($|e^j_0 [e^{-1}]^k_j| > 1$): overtilted cone; two Fermi surfaces touch at Weyl point
- **Lifshitz transition** at $|e^j_0 [e^{-1}]^k_j| = 1$: Dirac lines emerge (co-dimension 2 nodes)

### Event Horizon as Lifshitz Transition
Using the Painleve-Gullstrand metric with shift velocity $v(r)$:
- Outside horizon ($|v| < c$): type-I Weyl fermions
- At horizon ($|v| = c$): Lifshitz transition; Dirac line appears
- Inside horizon ($|v| > c$): type-II Weyl fermions with Fermi surfaces

The black hole horizon is the surface of the topological Lifshitz transition between type-I and type-II Weyl points.

### Berry Flux Transfer
At topological Lifshitz transitions, Berry flux (Chern number $N_3$) is transferred between Fermi surfaces through type-II Weyl points. Different configurations:
1. Type-II Weyl connects Fermi pockets — Berry flux transferred between pockets
2. Type-II Weyl connects outer and inner Fermi surfaces — Weyl point released, inner surface disappears

### Flat Band at Lifshitz Transition
In the vicinity of the Lifshitz transition, electron-electron interaction can lead to formation of a dispersionless (flat) band with zero energy and singular DOS. This opens a route to room-temperature superconductivity, with $T_c \propto \lambda$ (linear in coupling).

## Key Results

1. Type-II Weyl fermions emerge behind black hole event horizons
2. The horizon is a surface of Lifshitz transition between type-I and type-II
3. Dirac lines at the transition are supported by combined topology and symmetry
4. Berry flux transfer between Fermi surfaces through type-II Weyl points
5. Flat band formation near Lifshitz transitions enables enhanced $T_c$
6. Weyl semimetals can simulate black hole horizons and Hawking radiation
7. Inner Fermi surfaces can disappear at secondary Lifshitz transitions
