## Abstract

Recently Horava proposed a model for gravity which is described by the Einstein action in the infrared, but lacks the Lorentz invariance in the high-energy region where it experiences the anisotropic scaling. We test this proposal using two condensed matter examples of emergent gravity: acoustic gravity and gravity emerging in the fermionic systems with Fermi points. We suggest that quantum hydrodynamics, which together with the quantum gravity is the non-renormalizable theory, may exhibit the anisotropic scaling in agreement with the proposal. The Fermi point scenario of emergent general relativity demonstrates that under general conditions, the infrared Einstein action may be distorted, i.e. the Horava parameter lambda is not necessarily equal 1 even in the low energy limit. The consistent theory requires special hierarchy of the ultra-violet energy scales and the fine-tuning mechanism for the Newton constant.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### 1. Introduction
Volovik tests Horava's proposal for quantum gravity with anisotropic scaling between space and time using two condensed matter examples: acoustic gravity emerging in hydrodynamics and the Fermi point scenario where Lorentz invariance emerges in the infrared together with gauge fields and gravity.

### 2. Particle Spectrum
The asymmetric scaling implies a spectrum interpolating between infrared and ultraviolet:
$E^2(p) = p^2 + E_{\text{micro}}^2(p)$, where $E_{\text{micro}}(p) = p^z / M^{z-1}$.

For $z = 3$, the spectrum is cubic at large $p$: $E(p) \approx p^3/M^2$, and becomes linear at low $p$: $E(p) \approx p$ (emergent Lorentz invariance). In the Fermi point scenario, Lorentz invariance is not accidental but emerges from the topologically stable Fermi point.

### 3. Lorentz Symmetry Violation
For $z = 3$, the Lorentz-violating corrections start with $p^5/M^4$, which is observationally acceptable (as opposed to $z = 2$ where corrections start at $p^3/M^2$, strongly constrained). The $z = 3$ model is also potentially UV-complete because corrections to the cubic spectrum decay rapidly at large $p$.

### 4. Anisotropic Scaling in Quantum Hydrodynamics
Landau's 1941 quantization of hydrodynamics leads to quantum corrections $\omega(k) = c_s k + \gamma(\hbar/\rho)k^5 + \ldots$. The high-frequency spectrum depends only on $\rho$ and $\hbar$, not on $c_s$. Using the acoustic metric for phonons, one obtains $g^{\mu\nu}_{\text{acoustic}}k_\mu k_\nu + (2\gamma\hbar/\rho^2)k^6 = 0$, which corresponds to $z = 3$ anisotropic scaling.

### 5. Newton Constant in Fermi Point Scenario
The gravitational coupling from Sakharov induced gravity gives $1/G \sim M^2 \ln(E_{\text{uv}}^2/M^2)$ for the $z = 3$ scaling. This requires the Lorentz-violating scale $M$ to be between GUT and Planck scales: $E_{\text{GUT}} < M < E_{\text{uv}}$. At $E \sim M$, fermions are not Lorentz invariant, making the induced gravity non-covariant even in the low-energy limit: $\lambda \neq 1$, graviton speed $c_g \neq c$, and gravitons acquire mass $\sim M$.

### 6. Discussion
The cosmological constant problem is irrelevant for this model because in the perfect equilibrium vacuum, zero-point energies are fully compensated by microscopic degrees of freedom. It is the Newton constant that requires fine-tuning, not the cosmological constant. To restore GR in the infrared, one needs a consistent hierarchy of energy scales and a physically motivated mechanism for fine-tuning $G$.

## Key Results

1. The $z = 3$ Lifshitz-Horava anisotropic scaling is supported by hints from quantum hydrodynamics (acoustic gravity)
2. Lorentz-violating corrections for $z = 3$ start at $p^5/M^4$, which is observationally safe even if $M \sim E_{\text{Planck}}$
3. Newton constant estimate: $1/G \sim M^2 \ln(E_{\text{uv}}^2/M^2)$, placing $M$ between GUT and Planck scales
4. Under general conditions, the Horava parameter $\lambda \neq 1$ even in the infrared due to non-relativistic fermion contributions to induced gravity
5. The cosmological constant problem is automatically solved in self-sustained vacuum; the Newton constant requires fine-tuning instead
6. The Fermi point scenario naturally generates Lorentz invariance, gauge fields, and gravity at low energy from topologically stable structures
