## Abstract

We discuss two scenarios of emergent gravity. In one of them the quantum vacuum is considered as superplastic crystal, and the effective gravity describes the dynamical elastic deformations of this crystal. In the other one the gravitational tetrads emerge as the bilinear form of the fermionic fields. In spite of the essentially different mechanisms of emergent gravity, these two scenarios have one important common property: the metric field has dimension of the inverse square of length $[g_{\mu\nu}] = 1/[l]^2$, as distinct from the conventional dimensionless metric, $[g_{\mu\nu}] = 1$, in general relativity. As a result the physical quantities, which obey diffeomorphism invariance, become dimensionless. This takes place for such quantities as Newton constant, the scalar curvature, the cosmological constant, particle masses, fermionic and scalar bosonic fields, etc. This may suggest that the dimensionless physics can be the natural consequence of the diffeomorphism invariance, and thus can be the general property of any gravity, which emerges in the quantum vacuum. One of the nontrivial consequences of the shift of dimensions is related to topology. Due to the shift of dimensions some operators become topological, and contain the integer or fractional prefactors in the action. This in particular concerns the intrinsic 3+1 quantum Hall effect and the Nieh-Yan quantum anomaly in terms of torsion.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### II. Elasticity Tetrads
Crystal deformations are described by elasticity tetrads $E^a_i(x) = \partial_i X^a(x)$, where $X^a$ are crystallographic phase functions. These have dimension $[E^a_\mu] = [l]^{-1}$, giving metric $g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{ab}E^a_\mu E^b_\nu$ dimension $[g_{\mu\nu}] = 1/[l]^2$. The interval $ds$ becomes dimensionless: the distance between crystal nodes is an integer number of crystal surfaces. Torsion from dislocations: $T^a_{kl} = \partial_k E^a_l - \partial_l E^a_k$.

### III. Tetrads as Bilinear Combinations of Fermion Fields
In the Diakonov-Vladimirov (DV) theory, tetrads emerge as order parameters: $e^a_\mu = i\langle\psi^\dagger\Gamma^a\nabla_\mu\psi + \nabla_\mu\psi^\dagger\Gamma^a\psi\rangle$. The symmetry breaking is $L_L \times L_S \to L_{L+S}$. Since $e^a_\mu$ transforms as a derivative, $[e^a_\mu] = 1/[l]$, the same as elasticity tetrads. The analog occurs in superfluid $^3$He-B: $SO(3)_L \times SO(3)_S \to SO(3)_{S+L}$.

### IV. Physics in DV Dimensions
The paper provides comprehensive tables of dimension shifts. Key results:
- **Mass terms**: $[M] = 1$ (dimensionless). From $g^{\mu\nu}p_\mu p_\nu = M^2$: $d_{\text{DV}} = -2 + 1 + 1 = 0$.
- **General relativity**: $[R] = [\Lambda] = [G] = 1$. Only the combination $\Lambda G^2$ matters.
- **Temperature**: $[T] = 1$ (dimensionless), but Tolman temperature has $[T_{\text{Tolman}}] = 1/[l]$.
- **Black hole**: $[T_{\text{BH}}] = [S_{\text{BH}}] = [M_{\text{BH}}] = [A_{\text{BH}}] = [G] = 1$. Area is dimensionless, supporting quantization.

### V. Topological Terms
Operators with $d_{\text{DV}} = 4$ are topological. The Chern-Simons term for 3+1 quantum Hall effect:
$S_{4D}[A_\mu] = \frac{1}{4\pi^2}\sum_a N_a \int d^4x\, E^a_\mu \epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}A_\nu\partial_\alpha A_\beta$
has integer prefactors $N_a$ (winding numbers). The Nieh-Yan anomaly prefactor $\lambda^2$ becomes dimensionless and thus universal in DV dimensions.

## Key Results

1. Two independent emergent gravity scenarios (superplastic crystal, DV bilinear tetrads) both give $[g_{\mu\nu}] = 1/[l]^2$
2. Diffeomorphism-invariant quantities become dimensionless: $[G] = [\Lambda] = [R] = [M] = 1$
3. The spacetime volume $V = \int d^4x\sqrt{-g}$ is dimensionless, suggesting quantized values
4. Black hole area is dimensionless, supporting Bekenstein area quantization
5. The 3+1 quantum Hall effect Chern-Simons term has integer topological prefactors in DV dimensions
6. The Nieh-Yan anomaly prefactor becomes universal (dimensionless) in DV dimensions
7. Only ratios of mass scales are meaningful: "there are no fundamental constants"
