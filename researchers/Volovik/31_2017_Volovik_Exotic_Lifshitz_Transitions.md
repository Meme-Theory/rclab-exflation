## Abstract

Topological Lifshitz transitions involve many types of topological structures in momentum and frequency-momentum spaces: Fermi surfaces, Dirac lines, Dirac and Weyl points, etc. Each of these structures has their own topological invariant ($N_1, N_2, N_3, \tilde{N}_3$, etc.), which supports the stability of a given topological structure. The topology of the shape of Fermi surfaces and Dirac lines, as well as the interconnection of the objects of different dimensions, lead to numerous classes of Lifshitz transitions. The consequences of Lifshitz transitions are important in different areas of physics. The singularities emerging at the transition may enhance the transition temperature to superconductivity; the Lifshitz transition can be in the origin of the small masses of elementary particles in our Universe; the black hole horizon serves as the surface of Lifshitz transition between the vacua with type-I and type-II Weyl points; etc.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### I. Introduction: Fermi Surface, Dirac Line, Weyl Point
The paper classifies topologically stable nodes in fermionic spectra:
- **Fermi surface**: Vortex in 4D $(p,\omega)$-space, stabilized by $N_1$ (winding number of Green's function phase)
- **Weyl point**: Hedgehog (Berry phase monopole) in 3D momentum space, stabilized by $N_3$
- **Dirac line**: Vortex line in 3D momentum space, stabilized by $N_2$

The interplay of these structures and their topological invariants generates numerous classes of Lifshitz transitions.

### II. Fermi Surface and Lifshitz Transitions
The Fermi surface is topologically stable because it is a vortex in $(p,\omega)$-space with winding number $N_1 = \text{tr}\oint \frac{dl}{2\pi i}G(\omega,p)\partial_l G^{-1}(\omega,p)$. Lifshitz transitions include: neck disruption (vortex reconnection), pole-to-zero transitions (Luttinger liquid), Fermi surface to flat band (Khodel-Shaginyan condensate). Flat bands with huge density of states may enhance $T_c$ for superconductivity: $T_c \sim \Delta = gV_{\text{FB}}$ (linear, not exponential).

### III. Lifshitz Transitions Governed by Weyl Point Topology
Weyl points are hedgehogs with topological invariant $N_3$ from Green's function surface integral. Key transitions:
- **Type-I to type-II Weyl point**: Tilting the Weyl cone produces an overtilted cone with electron and hole Fermi pockets. The black hole horizon is the surface of this Lifshitz transition.
- **Pair creation/annihilation of Weyl points**: Massive Dirac fermions $\to$ pair of Weyl points with $N_3 = \pm 1$ at critical value of parameter.
- **Nexus**: Junction point where Dirac lines terminate on a Weyl point. The Dirac line is the analog of the Dirac string connecting magnetic monopoles.

### IV. Combined Lifshitz Transitions
Transitions involving multiple topological objects: Weyl points merging with Dirac lines, nexus formation, and interplay between different-dimensional topological structures.

### V. Dirac Lines and Graphene
Dirac lines in 3D materials (polar phase of $^3$He) and Dirac points in 2D (graphene) are stabilized by $N_2$. The merging of Dirac points in graphene under strain is another class of Lifshitz transition.

### VI. Lifshitz Transition Between Gapped States
The nearly massless vacuum of our Universe suggests proximity to a topological Lifshitz transition between fully gapped vacua. At the transition, fermions necessarily become gapless. This is the topological analog of the Multiple Point Principle.

## Key Results

1. Comprehensive classification of topological Lifshitz transitions using invariants $N_1, N_2, N_3$
2. Black hole horizon is a surface of Lifshitz transition between type-I and type-II Weyl vacua
3. Flat bands from Lifshitz transitions may enable room-temperature superconductivity ($T_c$ linear in coupling)
4. The hierarchy problem (why particle masses $\ll E_{\text{Planck}}$) is explained by proximity to a topological Lifshitz transition between gapped vacua
5. Nexus objects: Dirac lines terminate on Weyl points (Dirac string on monopole analog)
6. Fermi surface stability (Luttinger theorem) is topological: pole-to-zero transition preserves $N_1$
