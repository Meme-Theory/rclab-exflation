# Negative Newton constant may destroy some conjectures

**Author(s):** G.E. Volovik
**Year:** 2022
**Journal:** Published in Modern Physics Letters A
**arXiv:** 2202.12743
**Relevance:** HIGH

## Abstract

The magnitude and sign of the gravitational coupling 1/G depend on the relations between different contributions from scalar, fermionic and vector fields. In principle, this may give the zero and negative values of 1/G in some hypothetical Universes with the proper relations between the fermionic and bosonic species. We consider different conjectures related to gravity, such as the wave function collapse caused by gravity, entropic gravity, and maximum force. We find that some of them do not work in the Universes with zero or negative 1/G, and thus cannot be considered as universal. Thus the extension of the gravitational coupling to 1/G <= 0 provides the test on the universality of the proposed theories of gravity.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### I. Introduction

In both fundamental and Sakharov induced gravity, the gravitational coupling G^{-1} depends on fluctuating vacuum quantum fields. For massless fields at zero temperature:

G^{-1} = G_0^{-1} + (Lambda_uv^2 / 12 pi) (n_0 + n_{1/2} - 4 n_1)

where Lambda_uv is the UV cut-off, n_0 is the number of scalar fields, n_{1/2} is the number of Weyl fields, and n_1 is the number of vector fields. The term 1/G_0 contains other contributions (gravitons, dimension-0 scalars, "fundamental" value).

For massive fields, m^2 corrections appear. For temperature T larger than field masses but smaller than Lambda_uv, there are T^2 contributions. In the effective gravity of superfluid 3He-A with Weyl fermions:

G^{-1}(T) - G^{-1}(T=0) = -(pi/18) n_{1/2} T^2

This thermal correction is universal -- the same for relativistic quantum fields and Weyl-like condensed matter fermions. Similarly, scalars contribute (pi/9) n_0 T^2 to G^{-1}(T), the same from relativistic scalars and superfluid 4He phonons.

Key distinction: Lambda_uv (the Planck energy scale) is always positive, but 1/G can be zero or negative depending on species content. Negative G appears naturally in superfluid 3He effective gravity with massless Dirac quasiparticles, where it produces solid angle excess in the global monopole metric. The zero value 1/G = 0 may follow from Weyl anomaly cancellation (requiring n_0 = n_{1/2} - 4 n_1 = 0) or may occur at the Big Bang considered as a topological quantum phase transition.

### II. Diosi-Penrose scenario of gravitationally induced collapse

The gravitational self-interaction energy for wave function collapse is:

U = -(GM^2/4) integral integral (d^3r d^3r' / |r-r'|) (|Psi_R(r)|^2 - |Psi_L(r)|^2)(|Psi_R(r')|^2 - |Psi_L(r')|^2)

For G > 0, localization of the wave packet is energetically favorable, leading to collapse with time Delta_t ~ 1/U. For G < 0, the quantum superposition has lower energy, leading to "anti-collapse" -- superposition wins. Since the scenario depends on the sign of G, and hence on the species content, it cannot be fundamental.

### III. Verlinde entropic gravity

In Verlinde's entropic gravity, the number of bits on the holographic screen is N = A/G. For 1/G = 0, information is absent (N = 0), yet gravity still exists since the gravitational action starts with quadratic terms (R^2). For 1/G < 0, the formula is meaningless. This demonstrates the entropic approach is not universal.

If the holographic principle holds for G^{-1} <= 0, it would naturally be N ~ A Lambda_uv^2 (determined by the UV cut-off), but this has no relation to gravity when 1/G < 0.

### IV. Jacobson gravity from the first law of thermodynamics

The Jacobson approach derives Einstein equations from thermodynamics on local Rindler horizons. Extension to 1/G < 0 seems problematic since black hole horizons do not exist. However, de Sitter states can exist if the cosmological constant Lambda is negative, with H^2 = (8 pi/3)|G||Lambda|, providing cosmological horizons that may enable the Jacobson derivation.

Two possibilities for horizon entropy with 1/G < 0: S = A/4|G| or S = -A/4|G|. Negative entropy has been discussed for white holes obtained from black holes by tunneling, and in Einstein-Gauss-Bonnet gravity.

For 1/G = 0, gravity consists of R^2 terms (conformal Weyl gravity), entropy is not proportional to area, and the Jacobson approach requires modification.

### V. Maximal force

The maximum force conjecture (Gibbons) states F_max = 1/(4G). In a Universe with 1/G = 0, any force exceeds this limit. With 1/G < 0, black holes do not form, cosmic strings and monopoles produce angle excess instead of deficit, and Gibbons' maximum-angle-deficit arguments fail.

A maximum force might exist as F_max ~ Lambda_uv^2 (determined by UV cut-off), but this is not related to 1/G which can be made arbitrarily small.

### VI. Conclusion

The gravitational coupling 1/G depends on species, masses, and temperature and is not fundamental. Extension to 1/G <= 0 is a universality test. The Diosi-Penrose collapse, Verlinde entropic gravity, and maximum force conjecture all fail this test. The Jacobson approach requires modification.

An important note: in tetrad gravity where tetrads emerge as bilinear combinations of fermionic fields (Diakonov), tetrads have dimension of inverse length (as in elasticity theory of solids). Then 1/G and area A become dimensionless, and the parameter 1/(4G) could be a topological invariant taking any integer value: 1/(4G) = ..., -2, -1, 0, +1, +2, ...

## Key Results

1. The gravitational coupling 1/G is not fundamental -- it depends on temperature, species content, and masses via G^{-1} = G_0^{-1} + (Lambda_uv^2/12 pi)(n_0 + n_{1/2} - 4 n_1)
2. Thermal corrections to 1/G are universal (same in relativistic QFT and condensed matter): -(pi/18) n_{1/2} T^2 for fermions, +(pi/9) n_0 T^2 for scalars
3. Diosi-Penrose gravitational collapse fails for G < 0 (superposition is energetically favored, producing "anti-collapse")
4. Verlinde entropic gravity fails for 1/G = 0 (zero information bits, yet gravity exists via R^2 terms)
5. Maximum force conjecture fails for 1/G <= 0
6. Jacobson thermodynamic gravity requires modification but may survive via cosmological horizons
7. In tetrad gravity with fermionic bilinears, 1/(4G) may be a topological invariant taking integer values including zero and negative
