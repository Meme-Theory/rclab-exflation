# Emergent Weyl spinors in multi-fermion systems

**Author(s):** G.E. Volovik, M.A. Zubkov
**Year:** 2014
**Journal:** Nuclear Physics B 881, 514-538 (2014)
**arXiv:** 1402.5700
**Relevance:** HIGH

---

## Abstract

In Ref. [Horava] it was suggested that the multi-fermion many-body system with topologically stable Fermi surfaces may effectively be described (in a vicinity of the Fermi surface) by the theory with coarse-grained fermions. The number of the components of these coarse-grained fermions is reduced compared to the original system. Here we consider the 3+1D system and concentrate on the particular case when the Fermi surface has co-dimension $p = 3$, i.e. it represents the Fermi point in momentum space. We demonstrate explicitly that in the vicinity of the Fermi point the original system is reduced to the model with two-component Weyl spinors. We generalize the construction to the situation when the original theory contains multi-component Majorana spinors.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### Horava Construction
In a $d+1$ dimensional condensed matter system with $d-p$ dimensional Fermi surface, the system near the Fermi surface is described by coarse-grained fermions with reduced components. For $p = d = 3$ (Fermi point), the system reduces to two-component Weyl spinors with emergent vierbein and gauge field:

$$D = e^\mu_a \sigma^a (p_\mu - p^{(0)}_\mu) + \ldots$$

where $p^{(0)}_\mu$ gives the emergent U(1) gauge field and $e^\mu_a$ is the emergent vierbein.

### Explicit Demonstration
The authors explicitly verify the Horava conjecture. Starting from an $N$-component system, the spectrum near a topologically protected Fermi point has two branches crossing. The crossing is stable because of the topological invariant (Chern number). The effective low-energy theory operates with two-component Weyl spinors experiencing emergent gravity given by the vierbein:

$$e \cdot e^j_i = f^j_i$$

where $f^j_i$ comes from the linear expansion of the Hamiltonian near the Fermi point.

### Majorana Fermion Generalization
The key generalization: the original system consists of real (Majorana) fermions with no Lorentz invariance. The partition function is written purely in terms of real numbers:

$$Z = \int D\psi \exp\left(-\int dt \sum_Q \psi^T_Q (\partial_t + \hat{A}) \psi_Q\right)$$

with a single $N$-component Grassmann variable $\psi$ (not $\psi$ and $\bar{\psi}$). In the low-energy sector, the system reduces to emergent Weyl fermions with:
1. Emergent complex numbers (imaginary unit from topology)
2. Emergent gauge field from Fermi point position
3. Emergent gravitational field from vierbein collective excitation

### Teleparallel Gravity
The emergent gravity is teleparallel: the spin connection $C_\mu$ is absent under reasonable assumptions. This means varying Weitzenb\"{o}ck geometry (torsion without SO(3,1) curvature).

### Froggatt-Nielsen Connection
The construction relates to Froggatt and Nielsen's random dynamics theory, where infinite degrees of freedom reduce to $2 \times 2$ subspace of Hermitian matrices. In superfluid $^3$He-A, the Weyl equation is explicitly obtained from the Bogoliubov-de Gennes Hamiltonian.

### Implications for Standard Model
The pattern suggests that the Weyl equation for quarks and leptons of the Standard Model may be an emergent phenomenon appearing at low energies in a microscopic system of multi-component Majorana fermions. The chiral elementary particles, gauge bosons, Higgs, and the dynamical vierbein may naturally emerge in the low-energy corner of the quantum vacuum with topologically protected Weyl points.

## Key Results

1. Explicit verification of Horava's conjecture for $p = 3$ (Fermi points)
2. Multi-component systems reduce to two-component Weyl spinors near topological nodes
3. Emergent gauge field, gravity (vierbein), and complex numbers from real Majorana system
4. Teleparallel gravity emerges (torsion, no curvature)
5. The construction applies to both complex and real (Majorana) original systems
6. Standard Model and GR may be effective theories emerging from a trans-Planckian multi-fermion vacuum
7. Masslessness of Weyl fermions is topologically protected — solves hierarchy problem
