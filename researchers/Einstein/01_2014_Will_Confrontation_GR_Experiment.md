# The Confrontation between General Relativity and Experiment

**Author(s):** Clifford M. Will
**Year:** 2014
**Journal:** Living Reviews in Relativity, 17, 4
**arXiv:** 1403.7377
**Relevance:** CRITICAL

## Abstract

The status of experimental tests of general relativity and of theoretical frameworks for analyzing them are reviewed and updated. Einstein's equivalence principle (EEP) is well supported by experiments such as the Eotvos experiment, tests of local Lorentz invariance and clock experiments. Ongoing tests of EEP and of the inverse square law are searching for new interactions arising from unification or quantum gravity. Tests of general relativity at the post-Newtonian level have reached high precision, including the light deflection, the Shapiro time delay, the perihelion advance of Mercury, the Nordtvedt effect in lunar motion, and frame-dragging. Gravitational wave damping has been detected in an amount that agrees with general relativity to better than half a percent using the Hulse-Taylor binary pulsar, and a growing family of other binary pulsar systems is yielding new tests, especially of strong-field effects. Current and future tests of relativity will center on strong gravity and gravitational waves.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### 1. The Einstein Equivalence Principle (EEP)

The paper establishes the three-part structure of EEP:
1. **Weak Equivalence Principle (WEP)**: The trajectory of a freely falling test body is independent of its internal structure and composition.
2. **Local Lorentz Invariance (LLI)**: The outcome of any local non-gravitational experiment is independent of the velocity of the freely-falling reference frame.
3. **Local Position Invariance (LPI)**: The outcome of any local non-gravitational experiment is independent of where and when in the universe it is performed.

Will argues that if EEP is valid, then gravitation must be a curved-spacetime phenomenon, and the only viable theories are metric theories satisfying: (i) spacetime is endowed with a symmetric metric, (ii) test bodies follow geodesics, (iii) in local freely falling frames, non-gravitational physics is special-relativistic.

### 2. Tests of WEP

WEP violations are parameterized via the Eotvos ratio $\eta \equiv 2|a_1 - a_2|/|a_1 + a_2|$. If passive gravitational mass $m_P$ differs from inertial mass $m_I$ by internal-energy-dependent corrections $\eta^A E^A / c^2$, then the Eotvos ratio constrains the WEP-violation parameters $\eta^A$. Modern torsion-balance experiments (Eot-Wash group) have achieved $\eta < 2 \times 10^{-13}$. The paper anticipated MICROSCOPE achieving $10^{-15}$.

### 3. Tests of Local Lorentz Invariance

Using the $c^2$-formalism, violations of LLI are parameterized by $\delta \equiv |c^{-2} - 1|$ where $c$ is the speed of electromagnetic radiation relative to the limiting speed of material test particles. Hughes-Drever experiments and modern cavity experiments bound $\delta$ at the $10^{-26}$ level. The Standard Model Extension (SME) of Kostelecky generalizes this to the full standard model, with tensorial Lorentz-violating parameters in all sectors.

### 4. Tests of Local Position Invariance

The gravitational redshift experiment tests LPI. The frequency shift between two clocks at different gravitational potentials is $Z = (1 + \alpha) \Delta U / c^2$, where $\alpha = 0$ in any metric theory. The Gravity Probe-A hydrogen maser rocket experiment yielded $|\alpha| < 2 \times 10^{-4}$. Null redshift experiments comparing different clock types bound the difference in $\alpha$ between clock species at the $10^{-6}$ level.

### 5. Schiff's Conjecture and the $TH\epsilon\mu$ Formalism

Schiff's conjecture states that any complete, self-consistent theory embodying WEP necessarily embodies full EEP. The $TH\epsilon\mu$ formalism of Lightman and Lee provides a test framework with four functions $T(U)$, $H(U)$, $\epsilon(U)$, $\mu(U)$. Metric theories satisfy $\epsilon = \mu = (H/T)^{1/2}$. Non-metric parameters $\Gamma_0$, $\Lambda_0$, $\Upsilon_0$ measure EEP violations. Eotvos experiments bound $|\Gamma_0| < 2 \times 10^{-10}$ and $|\Lambda_0| < 3 \times 10^{-6}$.

### 6. The Parametrized Post-Newtonian (PPN) Formalism

The PPN formalism characterizes the post-Newtonian limit of any metric theory via 10 parameters. The metric is expanded about Minkowski in terms of gravitational potentials with parametric coefficients. The two most important parameters are:
- $\gamma$: measures how much space-curvature is produced by unit rest mass (GR: $\gamma = 1$)
- $\beta$: measures nonlinearity in the superposition law for gravity (GR: $\beta = 1$)

Additional parameters $\xi$, $\alpha_1$, $\alpha_2$, $\alpha_3$, $\zeta_1$-$\zeta_4$ measure preferred-location effects, preferred-frame effects, and conservation law violations. Semi-conservative (Lagrangian-based) theories have five free parameters; fully conservative theories have three.

### 7. Tests of PPN Parameters

**Light deflection**: The deflection angle $\delta\theta = \frac{1}{2}(1+\gamma) \frac{4M_\odot}{d}$ where $d$ is the distance of closest approach. VLBI measurements yield $|1 - \gamma| < 4 \times 10^{-4}$.

**Shapiro time delay**: The Cassini spacecraft measurement yielded $\gamma - 1 = (2.1 \pm 2.3) \times 10^{-5}$, the tightest bound on $\gamma$.

**Perihelion advance**: The predicted advance per orbit is $\Delta\tilde{\omega} = \frac{6\pi m}{p} \left[\frac{1}{3}(2 + 2\gamma - \beta) + \frac{J_2 R^2}{2mp}\right]$, which agrees with Mercury's observed anomalous precession of 42.98 arcsec/century.

**Nordtvedt effect**: The SEP-violating "Nordtvedt parameter" $\eta_N = 4\beta - \gamma - 3 - \frac{10}{3}\alpha_1 - \frac{2}{3}\alpha_2 - \frac{2}{3}\zeta_1 - \frac{1}{3}\zeta_2$ is constrained by lunar laser ranging to $|\eta_N| < 4.4 \times 10^{-4}$.

### 8. Strong Equivalence Principle (SEP)

Will distinguishes two figures of merit for "strong gravity": $\epsilon \sim GM/Rc^2$ (compactness, GR-centric) and $GM/R^3 c^2 \sim \ell^{-2}$ (curvature, particle-physics-centric). SEP extends EEP to self-gravitating bodies and gravitational experiments. GR may be the only metric theory fully satisfying SEP. The SEP discussion leads to scalar-tensor theories (Brans-Dicke and generalizations), vector-tensor theories (Einstein-Aether), and massive gravity.

### 9. Gravitational Waves and Binary Pulsars

The Hulse-Taylor binary pulsar PSR 1913+16 provides the most precise test of gravitational radiation damping. The observed orbital decay rate agrees with GR's quadrupole formula prediction to within $0.2\%$. The double pulsar J0737-3039 provides five independent tests of GR in the strong-field regime, all consistent with GR predictions. Binary pulsars constrain scalar-tensor theories via dipolar gravitational radiation, which depends on the difference in the "sensitivities" $s_a$ of the two bodies.

### 10. Gravitational Wave Tests

Future tests via ground-based (LIGO, Virgo) and space-based (LISA) interferometers will probe: gravitational wave polarization (GR predicts only two tensor modes; alternative theories predict up to six), speed of gravitational waves (GR predicts $v_g = c$), and the phase evolution of inspiral waveforms.

## Key Results

1. WEP verified to $\eta < 2 \times 10^{-13}$ (Eot-Wash torsion balance).
2. LLI verified to $\delta = |c^{-2} - 1| < 10^{-26}$ (Hughes-Drever type experiments).
3. LPI verified to $|\alpha| < 2 \times 10^{-4}$ (Gravity Probe-A).
4. $\gamma - 1 = (2.1 \pm 2.3) \times 10^{-5}$ (Cassini Shapiro delay).
5. $|\beta - 1| < 3 \times 10^{-3}$ (combined perihelion shift and Nordtvedt effect).
6. $|\eta_N| < 4.4 \times 10^{-4}$ (lunar laser ranging Nordtvedt effect).
7. Gravitational radiation damping verified to $0.2\%$ (Hulse-Taylor pulsar).
8. All 10 PPN parameters consistent with GR values.
9. No evidence for preferred-frame or preferred-location effects ($\alpha_1, \alpha_2 < 10^{-4}$).
10. No deviation from inverse-square law found between $10^{-2}$ cm and $10^8$ km scales.
