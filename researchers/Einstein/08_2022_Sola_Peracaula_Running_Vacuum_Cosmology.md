## Abstract

It is well-known that quantum field theory (QFT) induces a huge value of the cosmological constant, Lambda, which is outrageously inconsistent with cosmological observations. We review here some aspects of this fundamental theoretical conundrum ('the cosmological constant problem') and strongly argue in favor of the possibility that the cosmic vacuum density rho_vac may be mildly evolving with the expansion rate H. Such a 'running vacuum model' (RVM) proposal predicts an effective dynamical dark energy without postulating new ad hoc fields (quintessence and the like). Using the method of adiabatic renormalization within QFT in curved spacetime we find that rho_vac(H) acquires a dynamical component O(H^2) caused by the quantum matter effects. There are also O(H^n) (n=4,6,...) contributions, some of which may trigger inflation in the early universe. Remarkably, the evolution of the adiabatically renormalized rho_vac(H) is not affected by dangerous terms proportional to the quartic power of the masses (m^4) of the fields. Traditionally, these terms have been the main source of trouble as they are responsible for the extreme fine tuning feature of the cosmological constant problem. In the context under study, however, the late time rho_vac(H) around H_0 is given by a dominant term (rho^0_vac) plus the aforementioned mild dynamical component proportional to nu(H^2 - H_0^2) (with |nu| << 1), which makes the RVM to mimic quintessence. Finally, on the phenomenological side we show that the RVM may be instrumental in alleviating some of the most challenging problems (so-called 'tensions') afflicting nowadays the observational consistency of the 'concordance' LCDM model, such as the H_0 and sigma_8 tensions.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### Section 2: Vacuum Energy in Flat Spacetime (ZPE and SSB)

The paper begins with a review of the zero-point energy (ZPE) and spontaneous symmetry breaking (SSB) contributions to the vacuum energy density in Minkowski spacetime. The effective potential splits as V_e(phi) = V_ZPE + V_scal(phi). The one-loop ZPE in the MS-bar scheme yields V^(1)_ZPE(mu) = m^4 / (4(4pi)^2) [ln(m^2/mu^2) - 3/2], which is finite but depends on the arbitrary renormalization scale mu. The key observation is that this flat-spacetime result has no implications for the cosmological constant problem -- curved spacetime is essential.

### Section 3: Non-minimally Coupled Scalar Field in FLRW Background

The scalar field action S[phi] = -int d^4x sqrt(-g) [1/2 g^{mu nu} partial_mu phi partial_nu phi + 1/2(m^2 + xi R) phi^2] defines the framework. The Klein-Gordon equation in FLRW spacetime leads to mode functions h_k(eta) satisfying h_k'' + Omega_k^2 h_k = 0, with Omega_k^2 = omega_k^2(m) + a^2(xi - 1/6)R. The WKB expansion W_k = omega^(0)_k + omega^(2)_k + omega^(4)_k + ... provides the adiabatic regularization framework, with each order tracking time derivatives of the scale factor.

### Section 4-5: Computing the ZPE and the Running Vacuum Model

The renormalized VED emerges from the subtraction prescription: <T^mu_nu>_Ren(M) = <T^mu_nu>(m) - <T^mu_nu>^(0-4)(M), performed at an arbitrary mass scale M. The critical result is that the quartic mass terms m^4 exactly cancel between the renormalized Lambda(M) and the renormalized ZPE, leaving the vacuum energy density free from fine-tuning. The canonical RVM form emerges:

rho_vac(H) = rho^0_vac + (3 nu_eff(H))/(8 pi) (H^2 - H_0^2) m_Pl^2

where nu_eff is approximately constant and given by nu_eff ~ (1/2)(xi - 1/6)(m^2/m_Pl^2) ln(m^2/H_0^2).

### Section 6: Effective Action Derivation

An independent derivation via the effective action W confirms the EMT results. The renormalized quantum vacuum Lagrangian yields shifts Delta_Lambda(M), Delta M_Pl^2(M), and Delta alpha(M) that are all finite and scale-dependent. The beta-function for the VED running is beta_vac = M (partial rho_vac / partial M) = (xi - 1/6) 3H^2 / (8 pi^2) (M^2 - m^2) + higher-order H terms. No m^4 terms survive.

### Section 7: RVM-Inflation

At 6th adiabatic order, finite H^6 contributions appear: rho^inf_vac = (nu_tilde / 80 pi^2) m^2 H^6. These drive inflation without requiring an ad hoc inflaton field. The vacuum decays into radiation with rho_r(a) = rho_I (a/a_*)^8 / [1 + (a/a_*)^8]^{3/2}, yielding a non-singular universe (rho_vac(0) = rho_I finite, rho_r(0) = 0).

### Section 8: Phenomenological Applications

The RRVM (running with R = 12H^2 + 6 dH/dt) is tested against cosmological data. Type-I RRVM (vacuum interacts with CDM) solves the sigma_8 tension with DIC > +10 (very strong evidence). Type-II RRVM (evolving G_eff) alleviates both H_0 and sigma_8 tensions simultaneously, with H_0 = 70.93 +/- 0.90 km/s/Mpc and DIC = +5.5.

## Key Results

1. The adiabatic renormalization of the ZPE in FLRW spacetime eliminates m^4 fine-tuning contributions to the vacuum energy density.
2. The renormalized VED acquires a mild dynamical component proportional to nu H^2 with |nu| ~ 10^{-3}.
3. No true cosmological constant exists in the RVM framework; Lambda(H) = 8 pi G_N rho_vac(H) evolves mildly with expansion.
4. RVM-inflation is driven by H^6 terms from 6th adiabatic order, without an inflaton field.
5. Type-II RRVM alleviates both H_0 and sigma_8 tensions with strong statistical support (DIC = +5.5).
6. The quantum vacuum EoS deviates from w = -1 by a small amount proportional to dH/dt, mimicking quintessence.

## Relevance to Phonon-Exflation

The RVM provides a QFT-derived framework for dynamical vacuum energy that directly parallels the phonon-exflation program. The tau modulus evolution in the M4 x SU(3) framework functions analogously to the Hubble parameter H in the RVM: both drive a running of the vacuum energy density through quantum effects without introducing ad hoc scalar fields. The RVM's prediction that rho_vac evolves as nu H^2 (with nu ~ 10^{-3}) is structurally similar to the framework's prediction w = -1 + O(10^{-29}), though the RVM predicts a much larger deviation. The adiabatic renormalization technique that eliminates m^4 contributions resonates with the framework's spectral action approach, where the Seeley-DeWitt coefficients a_2 and a_4 play analogous roles. The RVM's ability to simultaneously alleviate H_0 and sigma_8 tensions (via Type-II with evolving G_eff) provides an empirical benchmark against which the tau-modulus-driven expansion must be compared.
