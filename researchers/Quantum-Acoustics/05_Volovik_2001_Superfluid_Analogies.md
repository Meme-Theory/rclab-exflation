## Abstract

In a modern viewpoint the relativistic quantum field theory is the emergent phenomenon arising in the low energy corner of the physical fermionic vacuum -- the medium, whose nature remains unknown. The same phenomenon occurs in the condensed matter systems: In the extreme limit of low energy the condensed matter system of special universality class acquires all the symmetries, which we know today in high energy physics: Lorentz invariance, gauge invariance, general covariance, etc. The chiral fermions as well as gauge bosons and gravity field arise as fermionic and bosonic collective modes of the system. The inhomogeneous states of the condensed matter ground state -- vacuum -- induce nontrivial effective metrics of the space, where the free quasiparticles move along geodesics. This conceptual similarity between condensed matter and quantum vacuum allows us to simulate many phenomena in high energy physics and cosmology, including axial anomaly, baryoproduction and magnetogenesis, event horizon and Hawking radiation, cosmological constant and rotating vacuum, etc., probing these phenomena in ultra-low-temperature superfluid helium, atomic Bose condensates and superconductors. Some of the experiments have been already conducted.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### I. Physical Vacuum as Condensed Matter

Volovik presents the "anti-grand-unification" view: rather than symmetry being broken at low energies from a higher symmetry at high energies, the opposite may hold -- symmetries emerge at low energy as fixed-point properties of the vacuum. Superfluid $^3$He-A provides a concrete example: at high temperature $^3$He has ordinary condensed matter symmetries, but in the low-temperature superfluid A-phase, Lorentz invariance, local gauge invariance, and elements of general covariance gradually emerge from nothing in the $T \to 0$ limit. This happens because $^3$He-A belongs to a special universality class of Fermi systems with topologically stable Fermi points.

The paper argues that gravity, gauge fields, and elementary chiral fermions are all low-energy collective modes of an underlying "condensed matter." At the Planck scale, these modes merge with the continuum of high-energy degrees of freedom and cannot be separated. Since gravity is not fundamental but an effective field in the infrared, quantizing it deeper than graviton quanta makes no physical sense -- precisely as quantizing the classical theory of elasticity cannot reconstruct the quantum structure of a real crystal.

### II. Landau-Khalatnikov Two-Fluid Hydrodynamics as Effective Gravity

The superfluid vacuum is characterized by particle number density $n(\mathbf{r},t)$ and superfluid velocity $\mathbf{v}_s(\mathbf{r},t)$. The equations of motion are the continuity equation and the London equation:

$$\frac{\partial n}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot \mathbf{J} = 0, \quad m\frac{\partial \mathbf{v}_s}{\partial t} + \nabla \frac{\delta E}{\delta n} = 0$$

The quasiparticle (phonon) spectrum in superfluid $^4$He is linear in the low-energy limit: $E(\mathbf{p},n) \to c(n)|\mathbf{p}|$, where $c$ is the speed of sound with $c^2(n) = (n/m)(d^2\epsilon/dn^2)$. The Lagrangian for sound waves propagating above a smoothly varying background has the Lorentzian form:

$$\mathcal{L} = \frac{m}{2}n\left((\nabla\alpha)^2 - \frac{1}{c^2}(\dot{\alpha} + (\mathbf{v}_s \cdot \nabla)\alpha)^2\right) = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-g}\, g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu \alpha\, \partial_\nu \alpha$$

This defines the acoustic metric:

$$g^{00} = -\frac{1}{mnc}, \quad g^{0i} = -\frac{v^i_s}{mnc}, \quad g^{ij} = \frac{c^2\delta^{ij} - v^i_s v^j_s}{mnc}$$

$$g_{00} = -\frac{mn}{c}(c^2 - v_s^2), \quad g_{0i} = -\frac{mn v_{si}}{c}, \quad g_{ij} = \frac{mn}{c}\delta_{ij}$$

### III. Vacuum Energy and Cosmological Constant

The vacuum energy density $\tilde{\epsilon}(n) = \epsilon(n) - \mu n$ is related to vacuum pressure by $\tilde{\epsilon}_{\text{vac, eq}} = -P_{\text{vac}}$. For a self-sustained liquid (zero external pressure), the vacuum energy is identically zero:

$$\tilde{\epsilon}_{\text{vacuum of self-sustaining system}} \equiv 0$$

This nullification persists even through phase transitions -- the chemical potential adjusts automatically to maintain zero pressure. In contrast, the effective-theory calculation using phonon zero-point energy gives $\epsilon_{\text{eff}} \sim \Theta^4/(\hbar^3 c^3)$, which is $\sim E_P^4/c^3$ when mapped to cosmology -- 120 orders of magnitude above the observed cosmological constant. This confirms that effective field theory cannot predict vacuum energy; only the microscopic (trans-Planckian) stability condition can.

### IV. Universality Classes of Fermionic Vacua

Volovik classifies fermionic vacua by their momentum-space topology:

1. **Fermi surface** (topological invariant $N_1$): Landau Fermi liquids, normal metals.
2. **Fully gapped systems**: Dirac particles in superconductors and $^3$He-B.
3. **Fermi points** (topological invariant $N_3$): Chiral fermions, $^3$He-A, Standard Model.
4. **Gapped systems with nontrivial topology in 2+1 dimensions**: Quantum Hall states.

The Fermi point is topologically stable, described by the invariant:

$$N_3 = \frac{1}{24\pi^2} e_{\mu\nu\lambda\gamma} \text{tr} \int_\sigma dS_\gamma\, G\partial_{p_\mu}G^{-1} G\partial_{p_\nu}G^{-1} G\partial_{p_\lambda}G^{-1}$$

For chiral fermions, $N_3 = C_a$ (chirality). This topological classification protects the masslessness of chiral fermions and implies that Lorentz invariance, gauge invariance, and general covariance emerge near the Fermi point without being imposed.

### V. Fermi Points: $^3$He-A vs Standard Model

In $^3$He-A, the BCS order parameter produces Fermi points at $\mathbf{p} = \pm p_F \hat{\mathbf{l}}$ with $N_3 = \mp 2$. Near these points, the quasiparticle spectrum becomes "relativistic": $E^2 = c_\perp^2(p_x^2 + p_y^2) + c_\parallel^2 p_z^2$, with the speed of light being anisotropic. The spin-orbit coupling generates SU(2) gauge fields, the orbital $\hat{\mathbf{l}}$ texture generates effective gravity. The Standard Model fermions share the same Fermi-point universality class with $N_3 = \pm 1$.

### VI. Chiral Anomaly

The Adler-Bell-Jackiw equation is derived in the condensed matter context. The spectral flow through the Fermi point creates quasiparticles from the vacuum, exactly analogous to baryogenesis via the chiral anomaly. This was experimentally verified in rotating $^3$He-A.

### VII-VIII. Parity Violation and Chern-Simons Terms

The Chern-Simons energy in $^3$He-A is the condensed matter analogue of the Chern-Simons term in the Standard Model. The helical instability driven by chiral fermions produces "magnetogenesis" -- experimentally observed in rotating $^3$He-A.

### XIII. Horizons, Ergoregions, and Hawking Radiation

Event horizons form in the vierbein wall geometry where the flow velocity exceeds the "speed of light." The Hawking temperature is:

$$T_H = \frac{\hbar}{2\pi}\kappa_S, \quad \kappa_S = \left(\frac{dv_s}{dx}\right)_h$$

The paper discusses the Painleve-Gullstrand metric in superfluid effective gravity, vacuum resistance to horizon formation, and the role of trans-Planckian dispersion in the Hawking process.

### XV. Discussion

The key conclusion: superfluid $^3$He-A provides a complete BCS model incorporating not only the "relativistic" infrared regime but also several successive scales of short-distance physics. Since gravity is an effective field, the only output of its quantization would be graviton quanta -- deeper quantization is not meaningful. The vacuum energy problem is resolved by the stability condition of the self-sustained system, not by effective field theory calculations.

## Key Results

1. **Anti-grand-unification:** Symmetries (Lorentz, gauge, general covariance) emerge at low energy as fixed-point properties of the fermionic vacuum, rather than being broken remnants of a higher symmetry.
2. **Momentum-space topology:** Fermi points are characterized by topological invariant $N_3$ (generalization of chirality), ensuring topological stability of massless chiral fermions.
3. **Acoustic metric from superfluid hydrodynamics:** The Landau-Khalatnikov two-fluid equations produce an effective Lorentzian metric for phonons, with the superfluid velocity playing the role of the gravitational shift function.
4. **Vacuum energy nullification:** For a self-sustained quantum liquid, the relevant vacuum energy is exactly zero ($\tilde{\epsilon} = 0$) at $T = 0$, independent of microscopic details. Effective field theory gives the wrong answer by 120 orders of magnitude.
5. **Standard Model shares universality class with $^3$He-A:** Both systems have Fermi points with the same topological structure, providing a concrete condensed-matter analogue of the Standard Model fermion sector.
6. **Experimental verification of chiral anomaly:** The ABJ equation was verified in rotating $^3$He-A experiments.
7. **Hawking radiation from acoustic horizons:** The Hawking temperature formula applies to superfluid horizons with surface gravity determined by the velocity gradient.
