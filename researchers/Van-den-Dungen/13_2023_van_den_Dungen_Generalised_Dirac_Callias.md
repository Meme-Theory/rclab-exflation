# Generalised Dirac-Schrödinger Operators and the Callias Index Theorem

**Author(s):** Koen van den Dungen
**Year:** 2023
**Journal:** Forum of Mathematics, Sigma (accepted 2024)
**arXiv:** 2312.17600

---

## Abstract

We examine generalised Dirac-Schrödinger operators composed of a self-adjoint elliptic first-order differential operator D with a skew-adjoint "potential" given by a family of unbounded operators. The primary contribution is a generalization of the classical Callias theorem: the index of the Dirac-Schrödinger operator can be computed on a suitable compact hypersurface. The operator's index represents the Kasparov product between the K-theory class of the potential and the K-homology class of D, and shows that spectral flow along paths of relatively compact perturbations depends exclusively on endpoint data.

---

## Key Results

1. **Callias Theorem Extension**: The index of generalised Dirac-Schrödinger operators is computable on a compact hypersurface—essential for systems with non-compact domains.

2. **Endpoint Dependence**: Spectral flow depends only on endpoints, not intermediate behavior—spectral invariants are determined by boundary conditions.

3. **Kasparov Product Formula**: Index = ⟨[V], [D]⟩, the pairing of potential and kinetic term in K-theory.

---

## Framework Relevance

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS AND COSMOLOGY**: In phonon-exflation, the universe has a "boundary" (initial conditions at Big Bang, or asymptotic behavior at infinity). Van den Dungen's Callias theorem shows:

1. Topological invariants are determined by boundary conditions (initial+final states of BCS evolution).

2. The index (instanton number) depends only on how the spectrum changes at the boundaries, not the path taken—explaining why S_inst is insensitive to the detailed dynamics.

3. For foliated spacetime (families of spatial slices), boundary conditions at t=0 and t=T determine all topological properties—enabling predictions without solving the full dynamics.

**Application**: Predicting dark matter abundance from initial conditions alone, without detailed intermediate evolution.
