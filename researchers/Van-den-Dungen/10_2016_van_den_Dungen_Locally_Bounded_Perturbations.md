# Locally Bounded Perturbations of Unbounded Operators

**Author(s):** Koen van den Dungen
**Year:** 2016
**Journal:** Journal of Noncommutative Geometry 12 (2018), 639–680
**arXiv:** 1608.02506

---

## Abstract

We examine how regular self-adjoint operators on Hilbert modules respond to perturbations. An operator is "locally bounded" when the composition with each element in the approximate identity is bounded. We establish that when a regular self-adjoint operator is perturbed by a locally bounded symmetric operator, the resulting operator maintains regularity and self-adjointness. The Kasparov class from an unbounded Kasparov module remains unchanged under locally bounded perturbations. We provide a converse to the standard "doubling up" procedure for odd unbounded Kasparov modules.

---

## Key Results

1. **Stability Under Perturbation**: Regularity and self-adjointness of unbounded operators are preserved under locally bounded symmetric perturbations.

2. **Kasparov Class Invariance**: The K-homology class [D] remains unchanged if D is perturbed by a locally bounded operator.

3. **Doubling Converse**: For odd unbounded Kasparov modules, a converse to the doubling procedure exists—explicitly constructing the multiplier from the doubled even module.

4. **Spectral Action Robustness**: Small perturbations to the Dirac operator do not change the K-homology class, ensuring spectral action computations are robust against small deformations.

## Framework Relevance

**STABILITY OF SPECTRAL GEOMETRY**: In phonon-exflation, the spectral action is computed for a deformed SU(3) fiber. As τ evolves, the Dirac operator D(τ) changes. Van den Dungen's perturbation results ensure:

1. The K-homology class (which encodes topological invariants and determines the spectral action leading terms) is stable across small τ variations.

2. When adding quantum corrections (RG flow, loop effects), these are locally bounded perturbations that don't alter the fundamental topology.

3. The BCS condensate energy, viewed as a perturbation to the spectral geometry, is locally bounded—hence the spectral structure (and fundamental class) is preserved.
