# The quantum equivariant cohomology of toric manifolds through mirror symmetry

**Author(s):** J. M. Baptista
**Year:** 2008 (published 2009)
**Journal:** J. Geom. Phys. 59, 833 (2009)
**arXiv:** 0806.2091
**Relevance:** LOW (vortex foundations, not directly used in KK program)

---

## Abstract

Using mirror symmetry as described by Hori and Vafa, we compute the quantum equivariant cohomology ring of toric manifolds. This ring arises naturally in topological gauged sigma-models and is related to the Hamiltonian Gromov-Witten invariants of the target manifold.

---

## Key Results

1. **Quantum equivariant cohomology ring**: For a k-dimensional toric Fano manifold X obtained as the Kahler quotient of C^n by a torus T^{n-k} with charges Q^a_j, the quantum T^k-equivariant cohomology ring is computed to be QH^*_T(X) = C[w_1,...,w_n] / Q(X), where Q(X) is the ideal generated by specific binomials involving the charges.

2. **Mirror symmetry derivation**: The result is obtained via Hori-Vafa mirror symmetry, dualizing the gauged sigma model with target X and group T^k to a gauged Landau-Ginzburg model with superpotential W.

3. **Relation to classical rings**: The quantum equivariant ring deforms the classical equivariant cohomology ring H^*_T(X) = C[w_1,...,w_n] / J (where J is the Stanley-Reisner ideal) in the same way that the quantum cohomology ring deforms the ordinary de Rham cohomology.

4. **Gauge field contribution**: In the dual Landau-Ginzburg topological theory, the gauge field strengths sigma_b contribute to the ring of observables but NOT to the ideal of relations. Only the matter-field derivatives d_{theta_b} W generate the ideal.

5. **Example -- projective spaces**: For X = CP^k, the quantum equivariant cohomology is QH^*_{T^k}(CP^k) = C[w_1,...,w_{k+1}] / (w_1 ... w_{k+1} - e^{-t'}), which in the classical limit t' -> infinity reduces to H^*_{T^k}(CP^k) = C[w_1,...,w_{k+1}] / (w_1 ... w_{k+1}).

6. **Toric flop invariance**: Toric targets related by flop-type birational transformations (isomorphic in codimension 1) have the same quantum equivariant ring.

## Relevance to Phonon-Exflation

This paper computes quantum equivariant cohomology rings of toric manifolds using mirror symmetry, extending the classical Batyrev/Morrison-Plesser results to the equivariant (gauged) setting. The techniques for computing cohomology rings of Kahler quotients and the role of the moment map and toric charges are mathematical infrastructure relevant to understanding the topology of compact internal spaces in Kaluza-Klein reductions. The equivariant perspective on the target manifold connects naturally to Baptista's later work where the internal space carries a group action.
