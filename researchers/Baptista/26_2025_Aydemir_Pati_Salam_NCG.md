## Abstract

The lack of clear new-physics signals at the LHC searches motivates models that can guide current and future collider searches. The spectral action principle within the noncommutative geometry (NCG) framework yields such models with distinctive phenomenology. This formalism derives the actions of the Standard Model, General Relativity, and beyond from the underlying algebra, putting them on a common geometric footing. Certain versions of Pati-Salam (PS) models with gauge coupling unification and limited scalar content can be derived from an appropriate noncommutative algebra. In this paper, I review these gauge-coupling-unified Pati-Salam models and discuss their phenomenological aspects, focusing on the S1 scalar leptoquark.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### Section 1: Introduction

NCG describes nature in terms of operator algebras instead of point sets. The spectral triple (A, H, D) is augmented by chirality operator Gamma (generalized gamma_5) and charge conjugation J. The spectral data is given as a product of the ordinary 4D manifold M with a finite noncommutative space (two-sheeted spacetime M x Z_2). The spectral action S = S_F + S_B = (J psi, D_A psi) + Tr[chi(D_A/Lambda)] yields both fermionic (including Yukawa) and bosonic sectors.

### Section 2: Minimal NCG Framework — Spectral Standard Model

With the algebra A_F = C + H + M_3(C), the spectral action yields the SM coupled to modified gravity. The bosonic action is:

S_B = integral [(1/2kappa_0^2) R + alpha_0 C_{mu nu rho sigma}^2 + gamma_0 + tau_0 R*R* + (f_0/2pi^2)(g_3^2 G^2 + g_2^2 F^2 + (5/3)g_1^2 B^2) + |D_mu H|^2 - mu_0^2 |H|^2 - xi_0 R|H|^2 + lambda_0 |H|^4] sqrt(g) d^4x

Gauge coupling unification requires g_3^2 = g_2^2 = (5/3) g_1^2 at scale M_U. With SM field content alone, this unification is not achievable.

### Section 3: Pati-Salam Models from NCG

Using the algebra A_F = H_R + H_L + M_4(C), the framework yields Pati-Salam SU(4) x SU(2)_L x SU(2)_R. Three models arise from the order-one condition:

**Model A:** G_{422}, scalars phi(1,2,2), Sigma(15,1,1), Delta-tilde_R(4,1,2)
**Model B:** G_{422}, scalars phi(1,2,2), Sigma-tilde(15,2,2), Delta_R(10,1,3), H_R(6,1,1)
**Model C:** G_{422D} (with D = left-right symmetry), all of Model B plus Delta_L(10,3,1), H_L(6,1,1)

Symmetry breaking: NCG -> G_{422D} at M_U -> G_{321} at M_C via <Delta_R>.

Fermion content: (4, 2, 1) and (4, 1, 2), which is SM + right-handed neutrinos per generation.

The Yukawa sector (Eq. 13): L_Y = [psi-bar^{aI} gamma_5 Sigma^{bJ}_{aI} psi_{bJ} + psi^C_{aI} gamma_5 H^{aIbJ} psi_{bJ} + psi^C_{aI} gamma_5 H^{aI bJ} psi_{bJ}] + h.c.

The gamma_5 in the Yukawa terms arises from the Z_2 grading of the product geometry Gamma = gamma_5 tensor gamma_F.

### Phenomenological Remarks: S1 Leptoquark

The S1(3, 1, 1/3) scalar leptoquark provides tree-level solutions to the R_{D(*)} anomaly. After Pati-Salam breaking, the H_L(6,1,1) decomposes to include H_{3L} and H-bar_{3L}, where H*_{3L} is the "good" leptoquark with left-handed couplings but NO diquark couplings — ensuring proton stability.

The absence of diquark couplings is automatic in NCG-PS, not imposed ad hoc. This comes from the geometric construction: the Yukawa terms L_Y = 2[d^C_{Lj} nu_L - u^C_{Lj} e_L] H*j_{3L} + epsilon_{ijk} u^C_{Li} d_{Lj} H*_{3Lk} + h.c., where the first term mediates R_{D(*)} while the second (diquark) is absent.

NCG-PS advantages over ordinary PS: (1) gauge coupling unification required, (2) restricted scalar content, (3) proton stability from geometry.

## Key Results

1. NCG-based Pati-Salam models require gauge coupling unification (unlike ordinary PS)
2. Three models (A, B, C) with different scalar content determined by the order-one condition
3. The S1 leptoquark H*_{3L} in (6,1,1) is "safe" — no diquark couplings due to NCG geometry, ensuring proton stability
4. The gamma_5 in Yukawa terms arises from the grading Gamma = gamma_5 tensor gamma_F of the product geometry
5. Fermion content = SM + right-handed neutrinos (16 Weyl per family)
6. Exclusive left-handed S1 couplings reduce parameter space for R_{D(*)} solutions but do not prevent them
7. The restricted Lagrangian (some terms missing vs. ordinary PS) increases predictivity
