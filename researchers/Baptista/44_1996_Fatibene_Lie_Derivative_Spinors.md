# A geometric definition of Lie derivative for Spinor Fields

**Author(s):** Lorenzo Fatibene, Marco Ferraris, Mauro Francaviglia, Marco Godina
**Year:** 1996
**Journal:** Istituto di Fisica Matematica "J.-L. Lagrange," Universita di Torino (conference proceedings)
**arXiv:** gr-qc/9608003
**Relevance:** MEDIUM

---

## Abstract

Relying on the general theory of Lie derivatives a new geometric definition of Lie derivative for general spinor fields is given, more general than Kosmann's one. It is shown that for particular infinitesimal lifts, i.e. for Kosmann vector fields, our definition coincides with the definition given by Kosmann more than 20 years ago.

---

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### 1. Introduction and Motivation (Section 1)

The paper addresses the fundamental difficulty of defining Lie derivatives of spinor fields on curved spacetimes. The problem: $\text{SO}(M,g)$ and any spin bundle $\text{Spin}(M,g)$ covering it are not natural bundles — there is no natural way to map spinor fields under spacetime diffeomorphisms. However, these bundles are gauge-natural bundles, and the authors show that any vector field $\xi \in \mathfrak{X}(M)$ can be lifted uniquely to the spin bundle.

The resulting vector fields $\tilde{\xi}_K \in \mathfrak{X}(\text{Spin}(M,g))$ and $\xi_K \in \mathfrak{X}(\text{SO}(M,g))$ are called **Kosmann vector fields**.

### 2. Spin Structures (Section 2)

The Clifford algebra $\text{Cl}(p,q)$ is the real algebra generated by $\{I, e_a\}$ with $e_a e_b + e_b e_a = -2\eta_{ab}I$. For even dimension $n = 2d$, the complexified algebra $\mathbb{C}\text{l}(p,q) \cong M_{2^d}(\mathbb{C})$.

The spin group $\text{Spin}(p,q) = \text{Pin}(p,q) \cap \text{Cl}^0(p,q)$ (even elements of unit-length products). A spin structure on $\text{SO}(M,g)$ is a $\text{Spin}(p,q)$-structure $\eta: \text{Spin}(M,g) \to \text{SO}(M,g)$ equivariant under $\rho: \text{Spin}(p,q) \to \text{SO}(p,q)$.

The Levi-Civita connection lifts to the spin bundle:
$$\sigma = (\rho')^{-1} \circ (\eta^*\omega)$$
where $\rho' = T_e\rho: \text{spin}(p,q) \to \text{so}(p,q)$ is the Lie algebra isomorphism.

Spinor fields are sections $\psi: M \to S(M) = \text{Spin}(M,g) \times \mathbb{C}^d / \text{Spin}(p,q)$.

### 3. Lie Derivative of Spinor Fields (Section 3)

**Generalized spinorial transformation**: An automorphism $\Phi: \text{Spin}(M,g) \to \text{Spin}(M,g)$ inducing $\phi: \text{SO}(M,g) \to \text{SO}(M,g)$ and $\varphi: M \to M$.

**Generalized Lie derivative**: For a vector field $\tilde{\Xi} \in \mathfrak{X}(\text{Spin}(M,g))$ generating automorphisms:
$$\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\tilde{\Xi}}\psi = T\psi \circ \xi - \tilde{\Xi}_{S(M)} \circ \psi$$

The **restricted Lie derivative** (second component):
$$(\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{\Xi}}\psi)(x) = \lim_{t \to 0} \frac{1}{t}\left((\Phi_{-t})_{S(M)} \circ \psi \circ \varphi_t(x) - \psi(x)\right)$$

**Kosmann lift**: For any vector field $\xi$ on $M$, the Kosmann vector field $\xi_K$ on $\text{SO}(M,g)$ has coefficients:
$$(\xi_K)^a = \xi^a, \quad (\xi_K)^{ab} = -\left\{\nabla^{[a}\xi^{b]} + \omega^{[ab]}{}_c\xi^c\right\}$$

The lift to $\text{Spin}(M,g)$:
$$(\tilde{\xi}_K)^i{}_j = -\frac{1}{4}(\xi_K)_{ab}(\gamma^a\gamma^b)^i{}_j$$

**Main Proposition**: The Lie derivative of a spinor field $\psi$ with respect to $\xi$, as defined by Kosmann, equals the restricted Lie derivative with respect to the Kosmann lift:
$$\mathcal{L}_\xi\psi = \mathcal{L}_{\tilde{\xi}_K}\psi$$

In coordinates:
$$\mathcal{L}_\xi\psi = \left[\xi^a\nabla_a\psi^i - \frac{1}{4}\nabla_{[a}\xi_{b]}(\gamma^a\gamma^b)^i{}_j\psi^j\right]f_i$$

**Kosmann lifting is NOT a Lie algebra homomorphism**, but satisfies:
$$[\xi,\zeta]_K = [\xi_K, \zeta_K] - \frac{1}{2}\mathcal{L}_\xi(g_{\lambda\mu})g^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}_\zeta(g_{\nu\sigma})A^{\lambda\sigma}$$

For Killing vector fields $\xi$, the Kosmann lifting reduces to the natural isometry lift, and the Lie derivative has the classical interpretation.

## Key Results

1. **New geometric definition** of Lie derivative for general spinor fields, using the theory of gauge-natural bundles — more general than Kosmann's original definition.
2. **Kosmann's formula recovered** as a special case: for Kosmann vector fields (the unique lift of spacetime vector fields to the spin bundle), the generalized definition coincides with Kosmann's 1972 definition.
3. **Unique lift theorem**: Any vector field $\xi \in \mathfrak{X}(M)$ admits a unique lift to both $\text{SO}(M,g)$ (the Kosmann vector field $\xi_K$) and $\text{Spin}(M,g)$ (the lift $\tilde{\xi}_K$).
4. **Non-naturality of the Kosmann lifting**: The map $\xi \mapsto \xi_K$ is not a Lie algebra homomorphism — the discrepancy involves the Lie derivative of the metric, vanishing for Killing fields.
5. **Spin connection coefficients**: $\sigma^i{}_{jc} = -\frac{1}{4}\omega_{abc}(\gamma^a\gamma^b)^i{}_j$ where $\omega_{abc}$ are Ricci rotation coefficients.

1. **Kosmann lift in the framework**: The framework's Dirac operator $D_K(\tau)$ on SU(3) explicitly uses the Kosmann lift to define how spinor fields respond to the Jensen deformation. This paper gives the geometric meaning: $D_K$ uses the unique lift of the isometry-breaking deformation to the spin bundle via the Kosmann construction. 2. **Non-naturality matters**: The Kosmann lifting is NOT a Lie algebra homomorphism (eq. 3.18). 3. **Spin connection from Levi-Civita**: The formula $\sigma^i{}_{jc} = -\frac{1}{4}\omega_{abc}(\gamma^a\gamma^b)^i{}_j$ is exactly how the framework constructs the spin connection on SU(3) from the Ricci rotation coefficients of the Jensen metric — the foundation of all spectral computations.
