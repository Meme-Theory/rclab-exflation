## Abstract

We lay the foundations for a general approach to nonassociative spectral geometry as an extension of Connes' noncommutative geometry by explaining how to construct finite-dimensional, discrete spectral geometries with exceptional symmetry, and gauge covariant Dirac operators. We showcase an explicit construction of a geometry corresponding to the internal space of a G2 x G2 gauge theory with charged scalar content and scalar representations restricted by novel conditions arising from the associative properties of the coordinate algebra. Our construction motivates a new definition of bimodules over nonassociative algebras and a novel form of bimodule over semi-simple octonion algebras.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### Section 1: Introduction

Connes' NCG is built on modules: the algebra A is faithfully represented on H, with bimodules Omega^i_DA functioning as differential forms. Because the representation theory of nonassociative algebras is more challenging, it is often assumed that spectral geometry requires associative coordinate algebras. This paper shows this assumption is incorrect.

The main challenge for nonassociative geometries: it is technically simpler to build models with uncharged Higgs fields, because one can use elements from the associative nucleus (like the identity) to construct Dirac operators. But the associative nucleus is typically invariant under internal symmetries, so the resulting Higgs fields are singlets. The paper addresses the full challenge of constructing charged Higgs fields.

The key technical tool is the associative specialization of alternative algebras (Eq. 1): L_{ab} = L_a L_b + [L_a, R_b], where L_a, R_a are left and right multiplication operators. This plays the role analogous to pi_{ab} = pi_a pi_b for associative representations.

### Section 2: Bimodules in Nonassociative Spectral Geometry

**Standard definition (Def. 2.1-2.2):** A bimodule M for algebra A in a class C is defined via square-zero extensions B = A + M, where B inherits the algebraic properties of A.

Inner derivations of different algebra types (Eqs. 6-9):
- Associative: delta_a = L_a - R_a
- Jordan: delta_{ab} = [L_a, L_b]
- Lie: delta_a = L_a
- Alternative: delta_{ab} = [L_a, L_b] + [L_a, R_b] + [R_a, R_b]

The key limitation for nonassociative algebras: conventional bimodule definitions often yield only free modules M = A tensor V, where external derivations Delta: A -> M inherit their structure from inner derivations — preventing the construction of Dirac operators that connect discrete points.

**New approach (Def. 2.3):** The paper proposes a new definition of bimodules specifically for spectral geometry, requiring compatibility with the Dirac-type external derivation rather than inheriting all algebraic identities from A.

### Section 3: Differential Forms and Gauge Covariant Dirac Operators

In associative spectral geometry, 1-forms Omega^1_dA are generated by d[a] satisfying d[ab] = a.d[b] + d[a].b. The key construction: composing an automorphism-invariant external derivation Delta_{I tensor I} with bimodule homomorphisms Phi gives new derivations Delta_Phi = Phi o Delta that TRANSFORM (rather than being invariant) under lifted automorphisms. This is how charged Higgs fields appear.

**Split alternative bimodules** (Eqs. 32-33): For alternative algebras A = direct-sum A_i, the bimodule M decomposes as M ~ A tensor V tensor A with actions a.(h tensor v^{ij} tensor k) = a_i h tensor P^{ij}(v) tensor k and similarly for right action.

**Theorem (Section 3.2):** Bimodule homomorphisms between split alternative bimodules take the restricted form Phi(a tensor v^{ij} tensor b) = a tensor Gamma(v^{ij}) tensor b, where Gamma acts only on the vector space V. This means they commute with lifted automorphisms — so composing them with an invariant derivation cannot produce a covariant one.

### Section 4: G2 x G2 Gauge Theory Construction

The coordinate algebra is A = O + O (two copies of octonions), with automorphism group G2 x G2.

**Split alternative bimodule approach (naive):** With M ~ O tensor R tensor O, the Dirac operator D remains invariant under G2 x G2 — only producing uncharged (singlet) Higgs fields.

**Reconstituted derivation bimodules (novel construction):** Using the specialization identity L_{ab} = L_a L_b + [L_a, R_b], the paper constructs a new type of bimodule that is NOT a split alternative bimodule. The key idea: the commutator term [L_a, R_b] in the specialization identity provides additional structure beyond the split alternative decomposition. By reconstituting the 1-forms using this identity, one obtains a bimodule that supports derivation-compatible maps which DO transform covariantly under G2 x G2.

The resulting geometry has:
- Gauge group: G2 x G2
- Charged Higgs fields in specific G2 representations
- Scalar representations constrained by conditions arising from the associative properties of the octonion algebra

### Sections 5-6: Formalization and Outlook

Section 5 formalizes the reconstituted derivation bimodules over semisimple octonion algebras and the derivation-compatible maps from which gauge-covariant Dirac operators are constructed.

Section 6 discusses the extension to completely general nonassociative spectral geometry, collating lessons from associative, special Jordan, exceptional Jordan, and alternative geometries.

## Key Results

1. Nonassociative spectral geometry with exceptional (G2 x G2) symmetry is explicitly constructed
2. The naive approach using split alternative bimodules yields only uncharged (singlet) Higgs fields
3. A novel "reconstituted derivation bimodule" construction produces charged Higgs fields
4. The octonion specialization identity L_{ab} = L_a L_b + [L_a, R_b] is the key technical tool
5. Bimodule homomorphisms between split alternative bimodules are extremely restricted (Thm in Sec. 3.2) — they commute with automorphisms
6. New definition of bimodules for nonassociative spectral geometry proposed
7. Scalar representations are constrained by the associative properties of the coordinate algebra
8. The construction extends the frontier of spectral geometry beyond associative algebras
