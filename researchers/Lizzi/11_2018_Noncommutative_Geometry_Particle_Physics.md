# Noncommutative Geometry and Particle Physics

**Authors:** Fedele Lizzi
**Year:** 2018
**arXiv:** 1805.00411v1

---

## Abstract

Broad pedagogical review of noncommutative geometry applied to particle physics. Covers spectral geometry foundations, fermion and boson actions, standard model implementation, and the Higgs mass problem. Discusses issues with Wick rotation and the role of neutrino Majorana masses in generating lower-dimensional operators.

---

## Key Review Topics

### 1. Spectral Geometry Foundations
- Spectral triples and Dirac operators
- Algebraic vs. geometric description
- Heat kernel expansion and Seeley-DeWitt coefficients
- Connection to particle physics

### 2. Fermionic and Bosonic Actions
- Fermionic action: natural Dirac bilinear form
- Bosonic action from spectral trace
- Comparison of cutoff vs. zeta regularization
- Coupling structure and gauge invariance

### 3. Standard Model Structure
- Almost commutative geometry: spacetime × internal algebra
- Internal algebra for SM: C ⊕ H ⊕ M₃(C)
- Matter multiplets and Yukawa couplings
- Gauge boson emergence from connection fluctuations

### 4. Higgs Mass Problem
- Spectral action predicts m_H ~ 170 GeV (pre-Higgs discovery)
- Post-2012 crisis: m_H = 125 GeV, not 170 GeV
- Solutions: scalar dilaton field, grand symmetry, violation of first-order condition
- Current consensus: multiple viable approaches restore agreement

### 5. Neutrino Majorana Role
- Right-handed neutrino mass term essential for generating lower-dim operators
- Without Majorana term: no cosmological constant, Higgs mass, gravity in spectral action
- Physical interpretation: neutrino mass is geometrically fundamental

### 6. Beyond Standard Model
- Grand symmetry and Pati-Salam unification
- Extended scalar sector (Paper 08)
- Leptoquarks and their role
- Connection to asymptotic safety

## Key Results and Insights

**Spectral approach strengths**:
1. Purely geometric derivation of SM from spectral data
2. Unification of gravity and gauge theories at conceptual level
3. Natural framework for neutrino physics
4. Predictive: constrains free parameters

**Spectral approach limitations**:
1. Higgs mass prediction requires corrections (solved post-2012)
2. Lorentz signature requires twisted triples (Paper 10)
3. Cosmological constant problem persists
4. Experimental tests limited (mostly precision tests at low energy)
