**Authors:** Fedele Lizzi

## Abstract

We discuss how the concept of **basho** (developed by Japanese philosopher Nishida Kitaro nearly a century ago)—meaning "place" or "topos" in a topological sense—gives insight into understanding points in modern quantum gravity. A quantum spacetime requires rethinking primitive geometric concepts. In NCG, classically-defined points dissolve into place-like topological structures.

---

## Key Arguments

### 1. Classical Point Concept
In Euclid and modern differential geometry: point = indivisible, dimensionless location.

Problem for quantum gravity: Points cannot exist below Planck scale due to uncertainty in spacetime structure itself.

### 2. Nishida's Basho
Nishida (1870-1945): "Place is not something determined by things; rather, things are determined by the place they occupy."

**Not Cartesian**: Place is NOT a container holding objects.
**Not objectual**: Place is a topological locus, a region of influence.
**Not absolute**: Places overlap, determine each other mutually.

### 3. NCG Realization
In spectral NCG:
- Points dissolve into irreducible representations of the operator algebra
- Each "point" is defined by what operators **can be simultaneously measured** there (maximal abelian subalgebra)
- Multiple overlapping "places" where different observable combinations have sharp values

**Result**: Quantum geometry is naturally described as a hierarchy of interlocking basho structures.

### 4. Local Quantum Physics Interpretation
The **local quantum physics** (Haag-Kastler) framework uses von Neumann algebras localized in spacetime regions (basho). Each region has:
- Its own algebra of observables
- Its own vacuum state (possibly)
- Its boundaries where causality is broken (for spacelike separated regions)

Basho is the **mathematical embodiment** of this localization.

### 5. Phononic Interpretation
A phonon in the framework:
- Not a point particle at position x with momentum p
- Rather, a **topos (basho) of excitations** defined by which observables have sharp values
- Multiple overlapping phononic basho can coexist (non-orthogonal excitations)

This provides **philosophical grounding** for why phonons are collective, delocalized objects.

## Key Insights

1. **Ancient philosophy meets modern physics**: Nishida's basho concept, developed independently of quantum mechanics, turns out to be the natural language for quantum gravity.

2. **Place vs. Object**: In quantum geometry, places (topological regions) are more fundamental than objects (points, particles).

3. **Emergent Spacetime**: Classical spacetime geometry emerges as a coarse-graining of basho structure—averaging over quantum overlaps.

4. **Philosophical coherence**: The framework's description of phonons as "excitations in the fabric" gains philosophical grounding in basho thinking.

- Particles propagating through spacetime

- Points connected by propagators

Rather, it describes:
- **Places (basho) of excitation** in an irreducible algebraic structure
- **Overlapping topological regions** where different observable patterns are stable
- **Coherent superposition of localization patterns** that form phonons
