## Abstract

In the early sixties Leonard Parker discovered that the expansion of the universe can create particles out of the vacuum, opening a new and fruitful field in physics. We give a historical review in the form of an interview that took place during the Conference ERE2014 (Valencia 1–5, September, 2014).

## Key Arguments and Derivations

This is a historical review in interview format. The physics content recapitulates and extends Parker's thesis work (paper 20).

**Preamble.** Prof. Parker is identified as the pioneer of quantum field theory in curved spacetime. His 1966 Harvard Ph.D. thesis (under Sidney Coleman) established that the expansion of the universe creates particles out of the vacuum — pulling together quantum mechanics and general relativity. The COBE (1992) detection of CMB temperature fluctuations and subsequent measurements by PLANCK fit the mechanism: quantum field theory in curved spacetime drives the primordial perturbations. The BICEP2 announcement (2014) of B-mode polarization was tentatively advertised as direct evidence for cosmological graviton production during inflation; subsequent Planck analysis showed dust contamination dominated the signal.

**Section II (the discovery; 1962–1966).** Parker started his Harvard Ph.D. in 1962, wanting to work at the interface of general relativity and quantum field theory. He decided against quantizing the gravitational field directly (too hard at the time) and instead studied quantized matter fields on the *classical* expanding-universe backgrounds that solve Einstein's equations. He took the simplest generally covariant extension of the flat-space Klein–Gordon equation and evolved it smoothly from an initial Minkowski phase ($a(t) = a_1$ constant) to a changing $a(t)$ (with continuous first and second time derivatives) and then to a final Minkowski phase ($a(t) = a_2$). The theorem for the general solution of a second-order differential equation immediately showed that the initial creation/annihilation operators evolve into *superpositions* of creation and annihilation operators — a Bogoliubov transformation — so there are real particles present in the final Minkowski region even though the initial state was the vacuum. Parker also treated spin-1/2 particles using the Bargmann–Schrödinger formalism (translated by hand from the German) and proved, using Penrose's short 1964 Les Houches article on conformally invariant equations for arbitrary spin, that no creation of conformally invariant massless particles occurs on conformally flat isotropic expanding universes.

**Publication delay and reception.** Sidney Coleman was away in Europe for two years, delaying the thesis defense. Parker sent the chapter to Bryce DeWitt at Chapel Hill (UNC) in early 1966, who offered a postdoc-level position. Parker defended the thesis in the fall of 1966 before Coleman, Glashow, and Walter Gilbert. The first publication (Phys. Rev. Lett. 21, 562 (1968)) was followed by the full Phys. Rev. 183, 1057 (1969); the second paper was written during a 1969 blizzard that stranded him in Queens. Andrei Sakharov credits Parker's work in his 1990 *Memoirs* (Ch. 18). In 1970 Remo Ruffini and Steven Fulling visited, and in 1971 Parker was invited to Princeton, working with John Wheeler's group. Fulling defended his thesis under Arthur Wightman in 1972 and became Parker's first postdoc (UWM); Larry Ford was his second.

**Black holes (1974).** Parker had already proposed to calculate particle creation by collapsing matter as part of his 1968 NSF proposal; Hawking's 1974 paper solved the problem beautifully before Parker could. Hawking's original paper did not cite Parker's 1968/1969 papers; Parker wrote to Hawking, and Hawking added the reference in a later review. Fulling's 1973 paper (same mode-mixing mechanism in Rindler coordinates for accelerated observers) later provided the bridge reference in Hawking's text. Parker then (1975, Phys. Rev.) computed the detailed probability distribution of pairs created by a black hole, using the method developed in his thesis for cosmological pair creation.

**Connection to thermodynamics.** Hawking radiation from black holes was the crucial step that reconciled Bekenstein's suggestion (area $\propto$ entropy) with the second law of thermodynamics. The thermal spectrum and temperature fix the proportionality constant and show that general relativity is consistent with thermodynamics when black-hole particle creation is included.

**Graviton creation (Ford–Parker 1977).** Parker had initially believed (based on Penrose's conformally invariant spin-2 equation) that gravitons would *not* be produced in isotropic expansion. At Princeton, Karel Kuchař pointed out that the conformally-invariant spin-2 equation does not match Einstein's linearized gravity. Grishchuk (1975, JETP) later noted that E. M. Lifshitz (1948) had shown that in the Lifshitz gauge, the linearized Einstein equation reduces to *minimally-coupled* massless scalar equations for each of the two graviton polarizations — exactly the operator Parker had shown *does* create particles. Ford–Parker (Phys. Rev. D 16, 1601 (1977)) quantized the Einstein graviton field on isotropic 3-curved expanding backgrounds and showed particles are created at the same rate as for minimally coupled scalars, with no infrared divergences for power-law expansions. The polarization patterns induced in the CMB by these cosmological gravitons are what BICEP2 and Planck are trying to disentangle.

**Inflation and the 1975 Nature paper.** Already in the 1960s Parker noted that particle-creation rates scale as $(\dot a/a)^2$ and $\ddot a/a$, so creation is largest in the early universe. In 1975 (Nature 261, 20) he published a class of models with a rapid early expansion that creates a thermal spectrum of particles, including exponentially expanding (de Sitter) stages; this anticipates inflationary reheating. The Glenz–Parker 2009 paper (Phys. Rev. D 80, 063534) smoothly joins a pre-inflationary Minkowski era to a $>60$ e-fold de Sitter stage and back to a late-time Minkowski era, showing that the initial Poincaré vacuum evolves into the de Sitter (Bunch–Davies) vacuum of the inflationary era. The paper argues that any observational difference between the standard Bunch–Davies inflation and a spontaneous-fluctuation start from a Minkowski vacuum lies beyond the present observable horizon.

**Graviton as the most-wanted particle.** The Hulse–Taylor binary pulsar timing implies gravitational-wave emission; LIGO is sensitive (this interview predates the 2015 detection). Direct detection of individual gravitons requires the cosmological amplification via the very-early-universe expansion — the CMB B-mode polarization is the signature. The neutrino (with potentially-massive mass) is treated analogously.

**Personal.** Parker stayed at the University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee (UWM) for family reasons; UWM's Center for Gravitation, Cosmology and Astrophysics grew out of his group (Fulling, Ford, John Friedman, Bruce Allen). Parker also plays piano and collects 17th-century paintings.

## Key Results

1. Historical confirmation that Parker's 1968/1969 Phys. Rev. Lett. and Phys. Rev. papers initiated QFT in curved spacetime.
2. Identification of the Bogoliubov transformation between asymptotic in/out operators as the mechanism of gravitational particle creation.
3. Proof that isotropic expansion does *not* create conformally invariant massless particles (photons, neutrinos) but *does* create gravitons (Ford–Parker 1977) because the linearized Einstein equation in the Lifshitz gauge reduces to minimally coupled massless scalars.
4. Schrödinger (1939) anticipated the classical analog via wave backscattering but lacked the quantum-field framework.
5. Cosmological gravitons generated in the earliest expansion phase are the physical source of the B-mode polarization patterns targeted by BICEP2 and Planck.
6. Glenz–Parker (2009, Phys. Rev. D 80, 063534) showed that a pre-inflationary Minkowski vacuum smoothly evolves into the Bunch–Davies de Sitter vacuum for $>60$ e-folds, making inflationary predictions essentially insensitive to the initial-vacuum choice for accessible modes.
