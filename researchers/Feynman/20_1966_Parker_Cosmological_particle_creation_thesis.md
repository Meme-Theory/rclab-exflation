## Abstract (verbatim from the thesis)

The interaction of a classical gravitational field with quantized matter fields of spin zero and spin one-half is considered in this thesis. In particular, the particle creation resulting from that interaction is studied in order to place upper bounds on the creation rates for the production of mesons and fermions, which is occurring at the present time as a result of the expansion of the universe. The equations governing the quantized fields are the general relativistic Klein-Gordon and Dirac equations. The metric is that of a 3-dimensionally Euclidean expanding universe (for the scalar field the results are shown to be also of the same order of magnitude in a closed expanding universe).

During the time-interval of a single measurement of the particle number, an adiabatic approximation is used to express the fields in a form similar to that in a static universe. Creation and annihilation operators which obey the correct commutation relations and are time-independent and unique to within the degree of approximation are then defined. The particle number operator can then be expressed in terms of those operators. By investigating the change in the particle number operator between two measurements widely spaced in time, it is possible to place an upper bound on the particle creation rate per unit volume. The above method of defining the particle number overcomes divergence difficulties which occur in integrating over all modes when more naive definitions of the particle number are attempted. The creation occurs in pairs (even for neutral particles). There is equal creation of matter and antimatter. The upper bounds obtained for the present creation rates per unit volume are independent of the past history of the universe, being functions of Hubble's constant, the present average density of matter in the universe, and the mass of the particle under consideration. The upper bound on the absolute value of the creation rate per unit volume for $\pi$-mesons is $10^{-105}\,\mathrm{gm\,cm^{-3}\,sec^{-1}}$. For electrons it is $10^{-69}\,\mathrm{gm\,cm^{-3}\,sec^{-1}}$, and for protons it is $10^{-64}\,\mathrm{gm\,cm^{-3}\,sec^{-1}}$. The largest of these upper bounds corresponds to the average creation of less than one proton per litre of volume every $10^{30}$ years. Such creation rates are unlikely ever to be directly experimentally observed.

The creation of massless particles of arbitrary spin in the 3-dimensionally Euclidean expanding universe is also considered. From the fact that the classical equations governing the fields of non-zero spin are all conformally invariant, it follows that the positive and negative frequency parts of the field remain distinct during the expansion. Barring complications which might arise in the quantization of the fields of higher spin, it is therefore concluded that no creation of massless particles of non-zero spin occurs.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

**Foreword / retyping preface (Navarro-Salas et al., 2025).** Places the work in historical context: Heisenberg–Euler (1936) on Dirac sea instability, Schwinger (1951) on vacuum pair creation in an electric field, Schrödinger (1939) on wave backscattering in an expanding universe as a classical analog of induced pair creation. Parker's thesis is the first quantum-field-theoretic treatment of spontaneous particle creation by the expansion of the universe.

**Framework.** The thesis considers the general-relativistic Klein–Gordon (spin 0) and Dirac (spin 1/2) equations on a 3-dimensionally Euclidean (spatially flat FLRW) expanding universe with scale factor $a(t)$. The scalar case is shown to have the same order of magnitude in the closed case.

**Adiabatic quantization.** Over a single measurement interval the fields are expanded in an adiabatic approximation as if the universe were momentarily static, giving time-independent creation/annihilation operators $A^\dagger_k, A_k$ with the correct canonical commutation relations, unique up to the order of approximation. The particle-number operator $N_k$ is written in terms of these operators, and changes in $N_k$ between two widely separated measurements bound the creation rate.

**Mode evolution and Bogoliubov transformation (Parker's discovery).** Smooth evolution of the free-field equation from an initial Minkowski era ($a(t) = a_1$ constant) through an expanding phase to another Minkowski era ($a(t) = a_2$ constant), with $a$ and its first two time derivatives continuous, forces the creation/annihilation operators to evolve into linear superpositions of creation and annihilation operators — the Bogoliubov transformation. Non-zero mixing of positive- and negative-frequency parts is the mathematical signature of pair creation. Creation is in particle–antiparticle pairs, even for real (neutral) fields, and matter/antimatter are produced in equal amounts.

**Resolution of infrared/ultraviolet difficulties.** Naive definitions of particle number that integrate over all modes are UV-divergent; the adiabatic definition above is free of these divergences within its regime of validity.

**Spin-1/2 (Dirac neutrinos/electrons).** Parker extends the construction to two-component spinor fields using the Bargmann–Schrödinger 1930s formalism for spin-1/2 particles in curved spacetime (written up as a thesis appendix in English since the originals were in German). The massless Weyl equation is conformally invariant in any isotropically expanding universe (which is conformally flat), so no massless neutrinos are produced.

**Upper bounds on present creation rates.** Independent of the past history of the universe; functions only of Hubble's constant $H_0$, present average mass density $\bar\rho$, and the particle mass $m$. Quantitative caps:
- $\pi$-mesons: $|\dot\rho_{\mathrm{cre}}| \lesssim 10^{-105}\,\mathrm{gm\,cm^{-3}\,sec^{-1}}$.
- Electrons: $\lesssim 10^{-69}\,\mathrm{gm\,cm^{-3}\,sec^{-1}}$.
- Protons: $\lesssim 10^{-64}\,\mathrm{gm\,cm^{-3}\,sec^{-1}}$ (the largest) — fewer than one proton per litre every $10^{30}$ years.

**Conformal invariance and massless particles of non-zero spin.** For spin 1/2, 1, and 2, the free classical field equations can be made conformally invariant (Penrose's formalism for massless fields of arbitrary spin; Parker, 1964, while reading *Relativity, Groups and Topology*, realised his unique-0-particle-creation scalar operator coincides with Penrose's conformally invariant scalar equation). Since isotropic 3-flat expansions are conformally flat, the positive/negative-frequency split is preserved and no pair creation of conformally invariant massless particles occurs. (The massless Einstein graviton, in contrast, is *not* conformally invariant — each polarization satisfies a minimally coupled scalar equation in the Lifshitz gauge, so gravitons *are* produced by isotropic expansion. This fact, proven explicitly in Ford–Parker 1977, was one of the direct observational implications probed later by BICEP2/Planck.)

## Key Results

1. Quantum field theory in an expanding FLRW spacetime produces spontaneous particle creation from vacuum via a Bogoliubov transformation between asymptotic in/out Minkowski vacua.
2. Creation is always in particle–antiparticle pairs, with equal matter and antimatter; neutral scalars are produced as neutral pairs.
3. Adiabatic particle-number operator resolves the UV divergences inherent to naive mode-counting definitions.
4. Upper bounds on present-day cosmological pair-creation rates for pions ($10^{-105}$), electrons ($10^{-69}$), and protons ($10^{-64}$) gm cm$^{-3}$ sec$^{-1}$ — unobservably small.
5. Massless conformally invariant fields (all non-zero-spin massless fields per Penrose) are *not* produced in isotropic expansion (only the graviton field, which is not conformally invariant in the Einstein gauge, is produced — this key extension appears in later work).
6. The general mechanism scales strongly with $\dot a/a$ and $\ddot a/a$, so particle creation is most important in the earliest stages of the universe — an observation Parker flagged and that became the germ of "reheating" in inflationary cosmology.
