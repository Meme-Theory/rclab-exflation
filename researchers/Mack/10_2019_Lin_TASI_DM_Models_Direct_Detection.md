# TASI Lectures on Dark Matter Models and Direct Detection

**Author(s):** Tongyan Lin
**Year:** 2019
**Journal/ArXiv:** arXiv:1904.07915, TASI 2018 Lecture Series

---

## Abstract

These pedagogical lectures provide a comprehensive introduction to dark matter models and direct detection strategies spanning the full dark matter mass range, from ultralight (10^{-22} eV) to super-heavy (>10^{15} GeV) candidates. The material emphasizes the keV-TeV mass range where particles are naturally produced through thermal mechanisms in standard Big Bang cosmology (thermal relic dark matter). The lectures examine general features and motivations for major dark matter candidate classes: WIMPs (weakly interacting massive particles), axionic particles, and dark sector models. The content covers direct detection theory and experimental methodologies, nuclear recoil physics, sub-GeV dark matter, dark sector portal models, and emerging detection techniques. The material originated from TASI (Theoretical Advanced Study Institute) 2018 lectures on "Theory in an Era of Data," presented to graduate-level students and early-career researchers.

---

## Historical Context

Dark matter detection research has evolved substantially since the field's inception in the 1980s. Early work focused on WIMPs at the electroweak scale (~100 GeV), naturally produced as thermal relics. However, experimental constraints have progressively eliminated regions of WIMP parameter space, motivating interest in alternative mass ranges and interaction mechanisms.

The standard WIMP paradigm assumes dark matter couples through the weak force, achieving a relic abundance matching observed dark matter density through thermal freeze-out. This "WIMP miracle" motivated decades of searches. However, the lack of positive signals at increasingly sensitive experiments suggests either (1) WIMPs exist at lower masses or lower cross sections; (2) WIMPs couple through harder-to-detect channels (spin-dependent, momentum-dependent); or (3) dark matter's nature differs fundamentally from the WIMP paradigm.

Lin's lectures synthesize contemporary understanding of this expanded dark matter landscape, covering candidates and detection methods beyond traditional WIMPs.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### Dark Matter Candidates Across Mass Ranges

**Ultra-light bosons (10^{-22} to 10^{-10} eV)**: Examples include axions and other pseudoscalar particles. Axions of mass $m_a \sim 10^{-5}$ eV can be produced non-thermally through the Peccei-Quinn mechanism, solving the strong CP problem while simultaneously serving as dark matter.

**Sterile neutrinos (keV mass)**: Hypothetical particles coupling only through gravity, produced in the early universe via lepton number violating processes. A keV-mass sterile neutrino with correct abundance constitutes warm dark matter, affecting large-scale structure formation differently than cold dark matter.

**WIMPs (1 GeV to 10 TeV)**: The canonical dark matter candidate at the electroweak scale. Examples include:

1. **Neutralinos** (supersymmetry): The lightest supersymmetric particle, coupling through Z and Higgs exchange
2. **WIMPless dark matter**: Particles heavier than typical WIMPs but with similar cross sections
3. **Kaluza-Klein dark matter** (extra dimensions): The lightest KK mode of a bulk particle in compactified space

**Asymmetric dark matter**: Models where dark matter density relates to baryon asymmetry, naturally explaining comparable dark and baryonic matter densities.

**Dark photons and hidden sectors**: Kinetically mixed new gauge bosons coupling weakly to the Standard Model.

### Direct Detection Theory

The nuclear recoil rate from WIMP-nucleus scattering is:

$$\frac{dR}{dE_r} = \frac{\rho_\chi}{\mu m_\chi} \int_{v_{min}}^{\infty} f(v) v \sigma(E_r) d^3v$$

where $\rho_\chi$ is the local dark matter density, $\mu$ is the reduced mass, $f(v)$ is the velocity distribution, $v_{min} = \sqrt{m_N E_r / (2\mu^2)}$ is the minimum velocity, and $\sigma(E_r)$ is the energy-dependent cross section.

**Spin-independent scattering** (scalar exchange or Higgs portal):

$$\sigma_{SI} \propto [Z g_p + (A-Z) g_n]^2 F^2(q)$$

where $g_p$, $g_n$ are proton and neutron couplings and $F(q)$ is the nuclear form factor.

**Spin-dependent scattering** (axial-vector coupling):

$$\sigma_{SD} \propto [S_p \Delta p + S_n \Delta n]^2$$

where $\Delta p$, $\Delta n$ are the spin content of protons and neutrons.

### Neutrino Floor and Background Challenges

Coherent elastic neutrino-nucleus scattering (CNS) cross section:

$$\sigma_\nu = \frac{G_F^2}{\pi} Q_w^2 [1 - \cos\theta_{cm}]^2 / 2$$

where $Q_w$ is the weak charge. For dark matter cross sections below ~10^{-47} cm^2, neutrino-induced nuclear recoils become indistinguishable from WIMPs, setting the "neutrino floor." This motivates directional detection or non-recoil signatures.

### Sub-GeV Dark Matter Detection

For dark matter below the GeV scale, traditional nuclear recoil detectors become insensitive. Alternative methods include:

1. **Scattering off electrons**: Lower mass transfer enables recoil energy above ionization threshold
2. **Absorption in targets**: Dark matter absorbed by nuclei or electrons
3. **Helioscope searches**: Using sunlight conversion to dark matter in strong magnetic fields (ADMX, IAXO)

## Key Results and Topics

1. **Unified landscape**: Dark matter spans ~40 orders of magnitude in mass, from ultralight axions to super-heavy relics. Each mass range has natural particle physics motivation and detection strategies. The keV-TeV "thermal relic" range encompasses candidates (WIMPs, axions, sterile neutrinos) naturally produced in standard cosmology.

2. **Thermal relic abundance**: For weak-scale particles:

$$\Omega_\chi h^2 \approx \frac{3 \times 10^{-27} \text{ cm}^3/\text{s}}{\langle \sigma v \rangle}$$

This "WIMP miracle" matches observed dark matter density for natural weak-scale interactions, motivating extensive searches despite null results.

3. **Detection hierarchy**: Different detectors optimize for different mass/coupling ranges:
   - **Ton-scale cryogenic** (CDMS, SuperCDMS): High-mass WIMPs, spin-independent
   - **Directional detectors** (DRIFT, NEWAGE): Light WIMPs, neutrino floor bypass
   - **Superfluid helium** (SUPERFLUID): Sub-GeV dark matter, electron recoils
   - **Helioscopes** (ADMX, IAXO): Axions, photon conversion

4. **Sub-GeV frontier**: The sub-GeV range (10^{-3} to 1 GeV) remains largely unexplored experimentally. This range offers natural models (asymmetric dark matter, dark photons) and expanded parameter space.

5. **Dark sector models**: Hidden sectors with multiple particles and self-interactions expand dark matter's theoretical landscape. Dark matter can decay to lighter particles, produce asymmetries, or undergo kinetic equilibration post-freeze-out.

6. **Multi-messenger approach**: Modern searches combine direct detection, indirect detection (annihilation gamma rays), collider production, and astrophysical probes (structure formation, stellar objects).

## Impact and Legacy

Lin's pedagogical lectures have become a standard reference for graduate education in dark matter physics. The material articulates the expanded landscape beyond the traditional WIMP-centric paradigm. The lectures have influenced subsequent experimental and theoretical work exploring sub-GeV and axion dark matter, directional detection, and dark sector models.

The lectures exemplify how observational null results motivate theoretical creativity and experimental innovation, driving the field toward new models and detection techniques.
