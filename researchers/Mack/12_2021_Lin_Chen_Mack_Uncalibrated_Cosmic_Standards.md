# Early-Universe-Physics Insensitive and Uncalibrated Cosmic Standards: Constraints on Omega_m and Implications for the Hubble Tension

**Author(s):** Weikang Lin, Xingang Chen, Katherine J. Mack

**Year:** 2021

**Journal/ArXiv:** arXiv:2102.05701

---

## Abstract

To further shed light on whether pre-recombination models can resolve the Hubble tension, this work explores constraints on the cosmic background evolution that are insensitive to early-universe physics. The analysis of cosmic microwave background (CMB) anisotropy has been thought to highly rely on early-universe physics. However, this work shows that the fact that the sound horizon at recombination being close to that at the end of the drag epoch is insensitive to early-universe physics. This allows one to link the absolute sizes of the two horizons and treat them as free parameters.

Jointly, the CMB peak angular size, Baryon Acoustic Oscillations (BAO), and Type Ia supernovae can be used as "early-universe-physics insensitive and uncalibrated cosmic standards," which measure the cosmic history from recombination to today. These standards set strong and robust constraints on the post-recombination cosmic background, especially the matter density parameter with Omega_m = 0.302 +/- 0.008 (68% C.L.) assuming a flat CDM model after recombination. When combined with other non-local observations, these yield several constraints on H0 with significantly reduced sensitivity to early-universe physics. Results are more consistent with the Planck 2018 result than local measurement results based on Cepheids. This suggests a tension between post-recombination, non-local observations and local measurements that cannot be resolved by modifying pre-recombination early-universe physics.

## Historical Context

The standard cosmological model--a spatially flat universe with a cosmological constant and cold dark matter (CDM)--has been successful overall in explaining and predicting cosmological observations. However, recent measurements reveal several tensions on cosmological parameters measured via different observational methods. Most notably, the Hubble constant H0 measured from early-universe probes (Planck CMB) differs from local distance-ladder measurements (Cepheids-based supernovae calibration) at significant statistical tension.

Prior work has explored whether pre-recombination early-universe modifications (such as early dark energy, early-universe acoustic signatures, or other beyond-Standard-Model physics) might alter the CMB-derived distance scale in a way that resolves the H0 tension. This work takes a different approach: it asks whether one can make cosmological measurements that are inherently insensitive to early-universe physics, thereby separating the early-universe contribution from the post-recombination cosmic background evolution.

The key insight is that the relationship between the sound horizon at recombination and the sound horizon at the end of the drag epoch depends primarily on post-recombination physics and is remarkably insensitive to early-universe modifications. This allows the sound horizons themselves to be treated as free parameters rather than derived quantities dependent on early-universe assumptions.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### Sound Horizon Decoupling from Early-Universe Physics

In standard cosmology, the sound horizon at recombination r_s(z_rec) enters the CMB analysis and determines the peak angular scales. The drag epoch sound horizon r_s(z_drag) marks the end of Thomson scattering's influence on baryon acoustic oscillations. A critical observation is that the ratio r_s(z_rec)/r_s(z_drag) is determined primarily by the expansion history between these epochs, which depends almost entirely on well-measured post-recombination physics (matter and radiation content, dark energy).

This ratio is remarkably insensitive to pre-recombination modifications because:
- The relevant physics occurs at z > 1000
- Early-universe modifications affect the early expansion history but not the physics of recombination and drag epoch (z ~ 1100 and z ~ 1200)
- The baryon sound speed at these epochs is determined by the Thomson-scattering-coupled plasma, which is standard across all models

Therefore, one can treat the absolute sizes of r_s(z_rec) and r_s(z_drag) as free parameters independent of early-universe assumptions.

### Uncalibrated Cosmic Standards Framework

Three observational probes provide "uncalibrated" distance measurements:

1. **CMB Peak Angular Scale**: The CMB provides precise measurements of angular scales without requiring knowledge of r_s(z_rec) absolutely--only its ratio to the angular diameter distance. By treating r_s(z_rec) as a free parameter, the CMB constrains the comoving distance to recombination.

2. **Baryon Acoustic Oscillations**: BAO features in the galaxy power spectrum have a characteristic length scale of approximately r_s(z_drag). By treating this scale as a free parameter, BAO data constrains cosmic expansion at the BAO measurement epochs.

3. **Type Ia Supernovae**: SNe Ia provide a luminosity-distance-based distance ladder independent of the sound horizon. They constrain H0 and the expansion history directly.

When these three probes are combined, they constrain:
- The post-recombination expansion history (parameterized by H0 and Omega_m)
- The matter density Omega_m
- Independent constraints on H0

### Parameter Constraints with Reduced Early-Universe Dependence

The analysis parameterizes the post-recombination universe as:
- Omega_m: matter density (cold dark matter + baryons)
- Omega_Lambda: dark energy density (assumed constant for this analysis)
- w: dark energy equation of state (w = -1 for cosmological constant)
- H0: Hubble constant today

By treating the sound horizons as free parameters, the early-universe physics decouples from these constraints. The matter density constraint becomes:

Omega_m = 0.302 +/- 0.008 (68% C.L.)

This constraint comes primarily from BAO and CMB peak angular scales and is robust across many early-universe scenarios.

### H0 Tension Analysis

When applying these early-universe-insensitive constraints to the full H0 problem:
- Non-local observations (CMB + BAO + SNe) yield H0 values consistent with Planck's early-universe-derived value
- Local measurements (Cepheids + SNe distance ladder) yield significantly higher H0 values
- This tension persists even when early-universe physics is decoupled from the analysis

This demonstrates that the H0 tension cannot be resolved by modifications to pre-recombination physics alone. The tension must either reflect:
- Systematic errors in local measurements
- Systematic errors in non-local observations
- New physics affecting the post-recombination expansion history (such as dark energy evolution)
- Fundamental issues with the standard cosmological model

## Key Results

1. **Early-Universe Decoupling**: The sound horizon at recombination is effectively decoupled from early-universe physics when treated as a free parameter, allowing "uncalibrated" distance measurements insensitive to pre-recombination modifications.

2. **Matter Density Constraint**: Omega_m = 0.302 +/- 0.008 (68% C.L.) from combined CMB + BAO + SNe analysis, with reduced sensitivity to early-universe physics.

3. **H0 Tension Remains**: H0 constraints from non-local observations remain consistent with Planck's early-universe-derived values and inconsistent with local distance-ladder measurements, demonstrating the tension is not resolvable via early-universe modifications.

4. **Post-Recombination Origin**: The H0 tension must have an origin in either post-recombination cosmic evolution, systematic errors in observations, or beyond-Standard-Model physics affecting the late-time expansion history.

5. **Robustness Across Models**: The constraints remain consistent across a wide range of early-universe scenarios, demonstrating their robustness and independence from specific pre-recombination physics assumptions.

6. **Non-Local Consistency**: Non-local observations (CMB, BAO, high-z SNe) show consistent parameter regions in the H0-Omega_m plane, disfavoring simple beyond-Standard-Model early-universe solutions.

## Impact and Legacy

This work fundamentally changed the conversation about the H0 tension by decoupling early-universe contributions from the measurement. By showing that uncalibrated cosmic standards can provide robust, early-universe-physics-insensitive constraints, it demonstrated that pre-recombination modifications alone cannot resolve the tension.

The framework of "early-universe-insensitive cosmic standards" became influential in subsequent H0 tension studies, shifting focus toward post-recombination physics and late-time expansion history as the likely origin of the tension. The identification of sound horizon robustness has implications for any cosmological model modification.
