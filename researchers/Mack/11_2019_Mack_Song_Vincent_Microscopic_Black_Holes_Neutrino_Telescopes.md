# Signatures of Microscopic Black Holes and Extra Dimensions at Future Neutrino Telescopes

**Author(s):** Katherine J. Mack, Ningqiang Song, Aaron C. Vincent

**Year:** 2019

**Journal/ArXiv:** arXiv:1912.06656

---

## Abstract

In scenarios with large extra dimensions (LEDs), the fundamental Planck scale can be low enough that collisions between high-energy particles may produce microscopic black holes. High-energy cosmic neutrinos can carry energies much larger than a PeV, opening the door to a higher energy range than Earth-based colliders. Here, for the first time, we identify a number of unique signatures of microscopic black holes as they would appear in the next generation of large-scale neutrino observatories such as IceCube-Gen2 and the Pacific Ocean Neutrino Explorer. These signatures include new event topologies, energy distributions, and unusual ratios of hadronic-to-electronic energy deposition, visible through Cherenkov light echoes due to delayed neutron recombination. The work finds that the next generation of neutrino telescopes can probe LEDs with a Planck scale up to 6 TeV, though the identification of unique topologies could push their reach even further.

---

## Historical Context

In models with Large Extra Dimensions (LEDs), the large hierarchy between gravity and the electroweak scales can be at least partly explained by confining the Standard Model gauge interactions to a 3+1-dimensional brane while allowing gravity to "leak" into one or more extra bulk dimensions. This framework allows a true Planck scale M* that can be much lower than the observed Planck mass M_Pl ~ 10^18 GeV.

The possibility of producing microscopic black holes in particle collisions has been studied extensively in the context of high-energy colliders by searching for the high-multiplicity signature from rapid Hawking evaporation. However, the reach of collider experiments is limited by the finite center-of-mass collision energy. Cosmic accelerators provide an alternative, as high-energy cosmic rays can reach much higher momenta than Earth-based accelerators. Prior to this work, extensive studies were performed on the production of black holes in air showers, particularly at energies above the GZK limit.

Neutrino telescopes offer a unique advantage over cosmic ray detectors: events can be completely contained, allowing for a far more detailed accounting of deposited energy and unique topological signatures that distinguish ordinary electroweak interactions from black hole events. This paper represents the first systematic identification of microscopic black hole signatures in next-generation neutrino observatories such as IceCube-Gen2 and PONE.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### Large Extra Dimensions and the Fundamental Planck Scale

In LED models, confining Standard Model interactions to a brane while allowing gravity in the bulk provides a mechanism to lower the effective Planck scale. Current observational constraints limit the scale M* to be above approximately 3-25 TeV, depending on the number of extra dimensions. Solar System-scale modifications of Newtonian gravity prohibit a TeV-scale Planck mass if only one LED exists, but two or more LEDs remain allowed by current observations.

The fundamental principle underlying black hole production is the hoop conjecture: a black hole will form if the impact parameter b between two colliding particles is smaller than twice the horizon radius r_H of a black hole with mass M = E_CM. For a particle collision with center-of-mass energy E_CM >> M*, the cross section for black hole formation becomes significant.

### Neutrino-Nucleus Interactions and Black Hole Cross Sections

When the center-of-mass energy exceeds the fundamental Planck scale, the black hole formation cross section can contribute significantly to the total neutrino-nucleus interaction cross section. This leads to an increase in the rate of interactions with incoming neutrino energy, which could appear as an enhancement in the derived neutrino energy spectrum at the high-energy end.

The cross section for black hole production scales approximately as:

sigma_BH ~ (pi/2) * r_H^2 = (pi/2) * (G_5 * M)^2

where G_5 is the five-dimensional gravitational constant and M is the black hole mass equal to the center-of-mass energy.

### Hawking Evaporation and Detector Signatures

Microscopic black holes produced in neutrino interactions evaporate through Hawking radiation. The Hawking temperature increases as the black hole loses mass, producing a characteristic spectrum of particles. For black holes with mass near the TeV scale, the evaporation occurs rapidly, producing high-multiplicity final states dominated by leptons, photons, and hadronic jets.

The key signature is the production of particles with high transverse momentum relative to the incident neutrino direction. In a contained detector, the Hawking evaporation products produce:
- Multiple high-energy hadrons (significant hadronic energy deposition)
- Multiple leptons (electrons/muons from charged pions and other decay products)
- Photons and neutrinos from neutral meson decay

### Cherenkov Detection and Neutron Recombination Effects

In large water/ice detectors, Cherenkov radiation from charged particles produces characteristic light patterns. A unique signature identified in this work is the delayed Cherenkov signal from neutron recombination. High-energy black hole evaporation produces free neutrons, which subsequently recombine with dissolved hydrogen, emitting photons. This creates a distinctive double-pulse signature: the immediate prompt Cherenkov light from Hawking products, followed by delayed light from neutron recombination.

The ratio of hadronic to electronic energy deposition provides another diagnostic tool. In standard neutrino interactions, this ratio follows predictable patterns depending on the interaction type. Black hole evaporation, however, produces an unusual ratio due to the democratic branching of the Hawking spectrum across all Standard Model degrees of freedom.

### Event Topology Analysis

Standard neutrino-nucleus interactions typically produce one or two high-energy products (from quasi-elastic scattering or deep inelastic scattering). Black hole production, by contrast, creates high-multiplicity events with many particles emerging from a localized region. The opening angle distribution and energy distribution of these particles differs markedly from background processes.

## Key Results

1. **Planck Scale Sensitivity**: Next-generation neutrino telescopes can probe fundamental Planck scales up to 6 TeV through identification of microscopic black hole signatures.

2. **Unique Event Topologies**: Black hole production creates distinctive high-multiplicity event patterns with multiple high-energy hadrons and leptons emerging from a compact vertex, distinguishable from all Standard Model processes.

3. **Energy Deposition Ratios**: The ratio of hadronic to electronic energy deposition in black hole events exceeds the ranges observed in standard neutrino interactions, providing a powerful discriminant.

4. **Delayed Cherenkov Signatures**: Neutron recombination produces characteristic delayed Cherenkov light echoes (0.1-10 microsecond timescales), creating a unique temporal signature absent from standard interactions.

5. **Enhanced Cross Section Rise**: At energies above M*, the total neutrino-nucleus cross section rises steeply due to black hole production, creating a distinguishable feature in the high-energy spectrum.

6. **Detector Reach Enhancement**: The identification of unique topologies could push the Planck scale sensitivity even further beyond 6 TeV.

## Impact and Legacy

This work established the foundation for direct experimental searches for large extra dimensions using next-generation neutrino telescopes. By identifying specific, multi-faceted signatures of microscopic black hole production--ranging from event topology to energy deposition patterns to novel Cherenkov signatures--it provides a blueprint for observational searches in IceCube-Gen2 and similar facilities.

The paper's emphasis on the unique advantages of neutrino telescopes (complete event containment, detailed calorimetry, temporal resolution) over cosmic ray detectors shaped subsequent experimental programs. The identification of the neutron recombination signature in particular represents a novel discovery-mode capability that distinguishes black hole events from all other high-energy processes.
