# Primordial Black Hole Dark Matter in the Context of Extra Dimensions

**Author(s):** Avi Friedlander, Katherine J. Mack, Sarah Schon, Ningqiang Song, Aaron C. Vincent

**Year:** 2022

**Journal/ArXiv:** arXiv:2201.11761

---

## Abstract

Theories of large extra dimensions (LEDs) such as the Arkani-Hamed, Dimopoulos & Dvali scenario predict a "true" Planck scale M* near the TeV scale, while the observed Planck mass M_Pl is due to the geometric effect of compact extra dimensions. These theories allow for the creation of primordial black holes (PBHs) in the early Universe, from the collisional formation and subsequent accretion of black holes in the high-temperature plasma, leading to a novel cold dark matter (sub)component.

Because of their existence in a higher-dimensional space, the usual relationship between mass, radius and temperature is modified, leading to distinct behavior with respect to their four-dimensional counterparts. This work derives the cosmological creation and evolution of such PBH candidates, including the greybody factors describing their evaporation, and obtains limits on LED PBHs from direct observation of evaporation products, effects on big bang nucleosynthesis, and the cosmic microwave background angular power spectrum.

The limits cover scenarios of 2 to 6 extra dimensions, and PBH masses ranging from 10 to 10^21 grams. For two extra dimensions, LED PBHs represent a viable dark matter candidate with a range of possible black hole masses between 10^17 and 10^23 grams depending on the Planck scale and reheating temperature. For M* = 10 TeV, this corresponds to PBH dark matter with mass M ~ 10^21 g, unconstrained by current observations.

## Historical Context

Black holes have long been recognized as ideal dark matter candidates. Cosmological data indicates that 85% of the Universe's matter content must behave as a cold, pressureless fluid present since the early Universe. Stellar remnant black holes are products of the late Universe, so primordial black hole creation mechanisms must be postulated. Typical scenarios invoke large-scale inhomogeneities at small scales created during inflation, leading to black hole creation during subsequent matter or radiation domination.

As black holes evaporate via Hawking radiation, a minimum black hole mass of approximately 10^15 grams (10^-18 solar masses) is required for survival until today. Strong constraints on the fraction of dark matter in primordial black holes exist over masses ranging from this lifetime threshold up to the "incredulity limit" (at least one PBH per dynamical object), derived from gravitational lensing effects, binary disruption, heating, accretion signatures, and cosmic microwave background distortion.

In theories with large extra dimensions, however, a qualitatively different scenario emerges. The lowered Planck scale M* ~ TeV allows high-energy collisions to produce microscopic black holes. These exhibit fundamentally different properties than four-dimensional black holes, with modified mass-radius-temperature relationships and distinct evaporation signatures. This work represents the first comprehensive study of LED PBHs as dark matter candidates across multiple dimensions and observational constraints.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### Black Holes in Extra Dimensions

In models with d extra spatial dimensions, the Planck scale relates to the fundamental gravitational coupling:

M_Pl^2 ~ M_*(d) * L_c^(d-1)

where M_*(d) is the true Planck mass in (4+d) dimensions and L_c is the compactification scale. For d=2 extra dimensions and L_c ~ 1 TeV^-1, the true Planck mass can be as low as M_* ~ 10 TeV.

The Schwarzschild radius of a black hole in (4+d) dimensions differs from the four-dimensional case:

r_H ~ (M / M_*)^(1/(d+1))

This leads to vastly increased horizon radii for black holes smaller than the compactification scale. A black hole with mass M ~ TeV can have a horizon radius comparable to the compactification length, making it effectively higher-dimensional.

The Hawking temperature in (4+d) dimensions is:

T_H ~ M_* * (M / M_*)^(-d/(d+1))

This temperature increases rapidly with decreasing mass, and the evaporation timescale becomes much shorter than in four dimensions for comparable masses.

### Black Hole Formation in the Early Universe

Primordial black holes in LED scenarios can form through:

1. **High-temperature collisions**: In the early Universe at temperatures above M*, particle collisions produce black holes with cross-section:

   sigma_BH ~ r_H^2 ~ (M_*/E_CM)^(d/(d+1))

2. **Accretion and growth**: Formed black holes can accrete surrounding particles, growing in mass during the radiation-dominated era.

3. **Merger events**: Multiple black holes can coalesce, further increasing mass.

The mass spectrum of PBHs produced depends on the primordial density fluctuations, the number of extra dimensions, and the Planck scale. For monochromatic or narrow mass distributions, a characteristic mass emerges determined by formation conditions.

### Greybody Factors and Evaporation

Unlike four-dimensional black holes, higher-dimensional black holes emit particles with modified greybody factors. The evaporation rate is:

dM/dt ~ -sigma_greybody * T_H^d / M^2

The greybody factors account for the probability that a particle produced at the horizon escapes to infinity, which depends on both the angular momentum barrier and the extra-dimensional geometry.

For d=2 dimensions:
- Photon greybody factor increases relative to 4D case
- Particle spectrum becomes more democratic across Standard Model degrees of freedom
- Evaporation products include enhanced high-energy photons and relativistic particles

### Observational Constraints

The analysis derives constraints from four primary channels:

1. **Galactic Constraints**: Microlensing, heating of stars, and dynamical effects limit the abundance of PBHs in galactic halos.

2. **Isotropic Background Light**: Evaporating black holes produce photons, electrons, and positrons contributing to the extragalactic background light. The observed isotropic background constrains the black hole abundance.

3. **Cosmic Microwave Background**: PBH evaporation injects energy into the early Universe plasma, ionizing the medium and distorting the CMB angular power spectrum.

4. **Big Bang Nucleosynthesis**: The radiation from evaporating black holes affects light element abundances through photo- and hadrodissociation of nuclei, and by altering the expansion rate.

## Key Results

1. **Two Extra Dimensions Viability**: For scenarios with two extra spatial dimensions, LED PBHs form a viable dark matter candidate across a wide mass range (10^17 - 10^23 grams), depending on the Planck scale and reheating temperature.

2. **Unconstrained Parameter Space**: At M* = 10 TeV, PBH dark matter with mass M ~ 10^21 grams remains unconstrained by current observations, representing a genuine discovery window.

3. **Dimension-Dependent Constraints**: Constraints become progressively tighter for scenarios with more than two extra dimensions due to the enhanced evaporation rates in higher-dimensional spaces.

4. **Distinctive Signatures**: LED PBHs produce different evaporation spectra than four-dimensional black holes, with enhanced photon production and modified particle ratios.

5. **Relic Abundance Calculation**: The calculation of the surviving PBH relic abundance incorporates accretion, mergers, and evaporation, yielding precise predictions for the dark matter fraction.

6. **Updated 4D Constraints**: The work refines and updates constraints on ordinary four-dimensional primordial black holes, establishing a comprehensive constraint map.

## Impact and Legacy

This work opened a novel dark matter parameter space by systematically studying primordial black holes in extra-dimensional theories. By deriving comprehensive constraints across dimensions, mass ranges, and observational channels, it established LED PBHs as a theoretically viable and observationally interesting dark matter candidate.

The work's analysis of higher-dimensional evaporation signatures has informed subsequent searches in gamma-ray observatories, X-ray missions, and neutrino detectors. The framework of systematic constraint derivation became standard for studying black holes in beyond-Standard-Model scenarios.
