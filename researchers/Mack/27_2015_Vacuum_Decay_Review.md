Vacuum decay via quantum tunneling is a fundamental process in quantum field theory. The Standard Model electroweak vacuum is metastable: due to quantum corrections, the Higgs potential develops a second (deeper) minimum at very high field values (phi ~ 10^{17} GeV). The true vacuum is therefore not the electroweak minimum we observe, but this high-energy state. However, the tunneling probability is so small that the electroweak vacuum lifetime exceeds the age of the universe by many orders of magnitude, making decay observationally irrelevant.

Key considerations include:
1. **Coleman-De Luccia bounce solution**: The nucleation rate of bubbles containing the lower vacuum is Gamma = A e^{-B}, where B is the Euclidean action of the bounce.
2. **Higgs mass constraint**: Precision measurements of the Higgs mass and top quark mass determine the vacuum stability boundary. The Standard Model is near the borderline of metastability.
3. **Supersymmetry and beyond**: Extensions to the SM (SUSY, extra dimensions) can stabilize the vacuum or change the decay rate.

## Historical Context

The observation that the Standard Model vacuum may be unstable was a profound realization. The top quark and Higgs masses are such that quantum loop corrections to the Higgs potential generate a negative lambda (quartic coupling) at high scales. This causes the potential to become negative for phi >> m_H, signaling a second minimum.

Coleman and De Luccia developed the semiclassical tunneling theory in the early 1980s. A "bounce" solution of the Euclidean field equations describes the tunneling event: a spherical bubble of the true (lower) vacuum nucleates and expands classically through the false (electroweak) vacuum.

The decay rate depends critically on the Higgs mass m_H and the top mass m_t. Precision Higgs measurements at the LHC have sharpened the constraint: the Standard Model is metastable but stable over cosmological timescales.

## Key Physics

### Higgs Potential Loop Corrections

The tree-level Higgs potential is:

$$V(\phi) = -\mu^2 \phi^2 + \lambda \phi^4$$

At one-loop order, quantum corrections from top and other heavy particles modify lambda:

$$\lambda(\mu) = \lambda_0 - \frac{3}{8\pi^2} y_t^4 \ln\left( \frac{\mu}{m_t} \right) + ...$$

where y_t is the top Yukawa coupling. At high scales, lambda can become negative, turning the quadratic-plus-quartic potential into a runaway shape.

For Standard Model parameters (m_H ~ 125 GeV, m_t ~ 173 GeV), the potential crosses zero around phi ~ 10^{17} GeV, develops a true minimum deeper than the electroweak vacuum, yet the decay lifetime is ~ 10^{140} seconds.

### Bounce Solution and Tunneling Exponent

The bounce phi(s) satisfies:

$$\frac{d^2 \phi}{ds^2} + \frac{3}{s} \frac{d\phi}{ds} = \frac{dV}{d\phi}$$

in O(4)-symmetric Euclidean space (s = sqrt(x_1^2 + ... + x_4^2)). The action of the bounce

$$B = 2\pi^2 \int_0^\infty ds \, s^3 \left[ \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{d\phi}{ds} \right)^2 + V(\phi) \right]$$

is very large for the Standard Model, making Gamma = A e^{-B} extraordinarily small.

### Precision Constraints

Recent measurements:
- **Higgs mass**: m_H = 125.09 ± 0.24 GeV (LHC)
- **Top mass**: m_t = 173.0 ± 0.4 GeV (Tevatron + LHC)

With these values, vacuum stability analysis shows:
- **Absolutely stable** if m_H > 129.6 GeV
- **Metastable** if m_H < 129.6 GeV
- **Current prediction** (m_H = 125 GeV): metastable, but lifetime >> cosmic age

### Beyond the Standard Model

In supersymmetric extensions, additional Higgs bosons and superpartners modify the RGE running of lambda. Depending on parameters, the MSSM can stabilize the vacuum.

Extra dimensions also affect the analysis: the effective potential shape changes due to Kaluza-Klein contributions.

## Key Results

1. **Standard Model is metastable**: The electroweak vacuum is not the true ground state, but the true minimum is so deep that tunneling is negligible.

2. **Lifetime >> age of universe**: Gamma ~ 10^{-10000} per Hubble volume, making decay unobservable.

3. **Higgs mass near criticality**: The observed Higgs mass is close to the stability boundary, a fine-tuning that suggests new physics.

4. **Top mass dependence**: The tunneling rate depends strongly on m_t; small changes in the top mass would dramatically affect stability.

5. **Vacuum history****: During the very early universe (at extremely high temperatures), the full potential is different. Vacuum transitions (first-order, higher-order) could have occurred, leaving imprints on inflation and particle production.

## Impact and Legacy

Vacuum metastability motivates beyond-SM physics and constrains new models. The near-criticality of the SM Higgs mass is viewed by many as evidence for new physics at high scales (such as SUSY, compositeness, or extra dimensions).

This analysis has influenced collider physics searches, model-building constraints, and early-universe cosmology (vacuum transitions during inflation, reheating).
