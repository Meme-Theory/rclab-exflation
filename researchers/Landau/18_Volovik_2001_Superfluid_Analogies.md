## Abstract

In a modern viewpoint the relativistic quantum field theory is the emergent phenomenon arising in the low energy corner of the physical fermionic vacuum — the medium, whose nature remains unknown. The same phenomenon occurs in the condensed matter systems: In the extreme limit of low energy the condensed matter system of special universality class acquires all the symmetries, which we know today in high energy physics: Lorentz invariance, gauge invariance, general covariance, etc. The chiral fermions as well as gauge bosons and gravity field arise as fermionic and bosonic collective modes of the system. The inhomogeneous states of the condensed matter ground state — vacuum — induce nontrivial effective metrics of the space, where the free quasiparticles move along geodesics. This conceptual similarity between condensed matter and quantum vacuum allows us to simulate many phenomena in high energy physics and cosmology, including axial anomaly, baryoproduction and magnetogenesis, event horizon and Hawking radiation, cosmological constant and rotating vacuum, etc., probing these phenomena in ultra-low-temperature superfluid helium, atomic Bose condensates and superconductors. Some of the experiments have been already conducted.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### I. Physical Vacuum as Condensed Matter (Section I)

Volovik presents two opposing views of symmetry: (1) the Grand Unification view where symmetry increases at higher energy, and (2) the "anti-grand-unification" scenario where symmetries are emergent low-energy phenomena. Superfluid $^3$He-A supports both: below $T_c$ symmetry breaks (standard picture), but as $T \to 0$ new symmetries emerge — analogs of Lorentz invariance, gauge invariance, and general covariance. This happens because $^3$He-A belongs to a special universality class of Fermi systems with topologically protected Fermi points.

### II. Landau-Khalatnikov Two-Fluid Hydrodynamics as Effective Gravity (Section II)

The effective metric experienced by phonons in superfluid $^4$He is the acoustic metric:

$$g^{00} = -\frac{mn}{c}, \quad g^{0i} = -\frac{v_s^i}{mnc}, \quad g^{ij} = \frac{c^2\delta^{ij} - v_s^i v_s^j}{mnc}$$

The quadratic Lagrangian for sound waves (scalar field $\alpha$) necessarily has the Lorentzian form: $\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\alpha\,\partial_\nu\alpha$. This demonstrates how effective Lorentzian metric arises naturally in condensed matter.

### III. Vacuum Energy and Cosmological Constant (Section II.G)

The vacuum energy density $\tilde{\varepsilon}(n) = \varepsilon(n) - \mu n$ of a self-sustaining liquid is identically zero in equilibrium at $T=0$:

$$\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\text{vacuum of self-sustaining system}} \equiv 0$$

This follows from the equilibrium condition $d\tilde{\varepsilon}/dn = 0$ and the absence of external pressure ($P_{vac} = 0$). The effective field theory estimate $\varepsilon_{eff} \sim E_P^4/(c^3)$ gives a result $\sim 120$ orders of magnitude too large — confirming that effective field theory cannot predict vacuum energy.

This suggests two resolutions to the cosmological constant problem:
1. $\Lambda = 0$ in equilibrium at $T=0$; at nonzero $T$, vacuum energy is of order the energy density of matter
2. The cosmological term has the form $\Lambda(g - g_0)^2$, vanishing in equilibrium ($g = g_0$), so only nonequilibrium vacuum gravitates

### IV. Universality Classes of Fermionic Vacua (Section IV)

Volovik classifies fermionic vacua by momentum-space topology:
- **Fermi surface** (topological invariant $N_1$): Landau Fermi liquid, metals
- **Fully gapped systems**: Dirac particles in superconductors, $^3$He-B
- **Fermi points** (topological invariant $N_3$): chiral fermions, $^3$He-A, Standard Model
- **Gapped with nontrivial topology in 2+1D**: quantum Hall systems

The topological invariant for Fermi points is:

$$N_3 = \frac{1}{24\pi^2} e_{\mu\nu\lambda} \text{tr} \oint_\sigma dS^\mu G \partial_{p_\nu} G^{-1} G \partial_{p_\lambda} G^{-1}$$

where $G$ is the Green's function. This invariant is the generalization of chirality and ensures massless fermions are topologically protected.

### V. Standard Model from Fermi Point Topology (Section V.B)

The Standard Model has 16 left-handed fermions per generation with Fermi points at $\mathbf{p} = 0$ protected by discrete symmetry. Each left fermion has $N_3 = +1$. The total topological charge is $N_3^{tot} = 16$, equal to $\text{dim}(\Psi_+) = 16$ (the dimension of the positive chirality spinor of $\text{Spin}(10)$). Above the electroweak transition, the topological invariants nullify, giving massive fermions.

### VI. Chiral Anomaly (Section VII)

The Adler-Bell-Jackiw equation is verified experimentally in rotating $^3$He-A. The anomalous production of "baryonic charge" (momentum of quasiparticles) is observed when vortices pass through the superfluid, confirming the analog of baryogenesis via spectral flow.

### VII. Event Horizons and Hawking Radiation (Section XIII)

Horizons form in superfluid flow when the flow velocity exceeds the Landau critical velocity (speed of "light"). The effective metric in Painleve-Gullstrand form:

$$ds^2 = -c^2 dt^2 + (dr - v_s dt)^2 + r^2 d\Omega^2$$

has a horizon where $|v_s| = c$. The superfluid resists formation of the horizon due to the non-linear dispersion at high momenta (transPlanckian physics), providing a natural cutoff absent in general relativity.

### VIII. Vacuum Pressure in $^3$He (Section X.C)

The interplay between vacuum pressure and matter pressure demonstrates that in equilibrium: (1) vacuum energy is exactly zero, (2) perturbations of the vacuum away from equilibrium produce a cosmological term proportional to $(T/E_P)^4$ — naturally small.

## Key Results

1. Relativistic QFT and gravity are emergent low-energy phenomena of an underlying fermionic vacuum
2. The acoustic metric in superfluids provides a direct analog of curved spacetime
3. Vacuum energy of a self-sustaining system is identically zero — resolution of the cosmological constant problem
4. Fermionic vacua are classified by momentum-space topology into universality classes
5. The Standard Model belongs to the Fermi point universality class; 16 left fermions per generation follows from topological invariant $N_3$
6. The chiral anomaly (Adler-Bell-Jackiw equation) is experimentally verified in $^3$He-A
7. Superfluid horizons provide analogs of black holes with natural transPlanckian cutoff
8. Only non-equilibrium vacuum gravitates — equilibrium vacuum energy vanishes exactly
