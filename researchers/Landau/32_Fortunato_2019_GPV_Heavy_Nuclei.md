# The Giant Pairing Vibration in Heavy Nuclei: Present Status and Future Studies

**Author(s):** M. Assie, C. H. Dasso, R. J. Liotta, A. O. Macchiavelli, A. Vitturi
**Year:** 2019
**Journal:** European Physical Journal A (special issue in memory of Pier Francesco Bortignon)
**arXiv:** 1905.01339
**Relevance:** MEDIUM

---

## Abstract

The Giant Pairing Vibration, a two-nucleon collective mode originating from the second shell above the Fermi surface, has long been predicted and expected to be strongly populated in two-nucleon transfer reactions with cross sections similar to those of the normal Pairing Vibration. Recent experiments have provided evidence for this mode in $^{14,15}$C but, despite sensitive studies, it has not been definitively identified either in Sn or Pb nuclei where pairing correlations are known to play a crucial role near their ground states. In this paper we review the basic theoretical concepts of this "elusive" state and the status of experimental searches in heavy nuclei. We discuss the hindrance effects due to Q-value mismatch and the use of weakly-bound projectiles as a way to overcome the limitations of the (p,t) and (t,p) reactions. We also discuss the role of the continuum and conclude with some possible future developments.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### 1. Theoretical Foundation of the GPV (Section I-II)

The GPV arises from the schematic pairing Hamiltonian:
$$H = \sum_j e_j(a_j^\dagger a_j + a_{\bar{j}}^\dagger a_{\bar{j}}) - G\sum_{j,k} a_j^\dagger a_{\bar{j}}^\dagger a_k a_{\bar{k}}$$

where $e_j$ are single-particle energies measured from the Fermi surface, and $G$ is the pairing force strength. The RPA dispersion relation is:
$$F(E) = \sum_j \frac{2j+1}{E - 2e_j} = \frac{2}{G}$$

The GPV is the collective state relative to the second major shell, analogous to how giant dipole resonances involve coherent particle-hole excitations across one major shell. The unperturbed two-particle energies form two bunches (first and second shell), and the GPV is the collective solution pushed down from the upper bunch by the pairing interaction.

### 2. Formal Analogy: Shape and Pairing Modes

The paper emphasizes the deep analogy between particle-hole (shape) and particle-particle (pairing) excitations, established by Broglia et al.:
- **Shape vibrations** are probed by inelastic scattering; collectivity is measured by $B(E\lambda)$.
- **Pairing vibrations** are probed by two-nucleon transfer reactions; collectivity is measured by the pair-transfer matrix element $\langle GPV|T|A_0\rangle$.

The GPV wave function $|GPV\rangle = \sum_j \alpha_j |j^2\rangle$ yields a cross section:
$$\sigma(GPV) \propto \left(\sum_j \alpha_j\right)^2 \sigma_{sp}$$

In the degenerate limit of $\Omega$ levels: $\sigma(GPV) \sim \Omega \cdot \sigma_{sp}$, scaling as $A^{2/3}$.

### 3. GPV and Alpha Decay (Section II)

An independent rediscovery of GPV physics came from alpha-decay studies. The calculation of $^{212}$Po ground-state half-life required including high-lying pairing configurations. The wave function:
$$|^{212}\text{Po(gs)}\rangle = A|^{210}\text{Pb(gs)} \otimes ^{210}\text{Po(gs)}\rangle + B|^{210}\text{Bi}(0_1^+) \otimes ^{210}\text{Bi}(0_1^+)\rangle$$

where $^{210}$Bi$(0_1^+)$ is the isovector pairing state at $\sim 5$ MeV (neutron-proton clustering in the next major shell), connects to the GPV through isoanalog structure. The GPV state $^{210}$Po$(0^+_{GPV})$ lies at $\sim 10$ MeV.

### 4. Experimental Searches in Heavy Nuclei (Section III)

**Via (p,t) reactions:**
- iThemba LABS: 60 MeV protons on $^{208}$Pb and $^{120}$Sn at 7 degrees. No evidence found.
- K=600 spectrometer at 0 degrees, 50 and 60 MeV protons on $^{118}$Sn. A small bump near 12 MeV but no clear evidence. Upper limit: 0.13-0.19 mb for GPV cross section.
- LNS Catania: 35 MeV protons on $^{120}$Sn with MAGNEX spectrometer. Small bump at $\Gamma = 1.5 \pm 0.4$ MeV, but inconclusive.

**Via ($^6$He,$\alpha$) reactions:**
- GANIL: $^{208}$Pb($^6$He,$\alpha$) at 20 MeV/A. Large breakup background masked any GPV signal.
- TRIUMF: $^{116}$Sn($^6$He,$\alpha$) at 8 MeV/A with IRIS Array. Analysis ongoing; breakup background problematic.

### 5. Q-Value Mismatch Effects (Section IV)

The non-observation in heavy nuclei is largely attributed to Q-value mismatch. The cross section is exponentially suppressed:
$$\sigma \sim \exp\left[-\frac{(Q - Q_{opt})^2}{2\hbar^2 \kappa r_0}\right]$$

where $\kappa$ is the slope of the two-particle transfer form factor and $r_0$ is the distance of closest approach. For (p,t) and (t,p) reactions, the ground-state-to-ground-state transition is Q-matched, but the large excitation energy of the GPV ($\sim 12-14$ MeV in Sn/Pb) creates severe mismatch.

**Weakly-bound projectiles** like $^6$He and $^{11}$Li have Q-values much closer to the optimum for populating high-lying states. A survey map shows that for $^{208}$Pb, the GPV cross section exceeds the ground-state cross section for $^6$He and $^{11}$Li projectiles.

### 6. Continuum Effects (Section V)

Using the Berggren representation (complex-energy shell model including bound states, Gamow resonances, and scattering continuum), the two-neutron GPV in $^{210}$Pb was found to be very wide and not a physically relevant state but part of the continuum background. The proton-neutron GPV in $^{210}$Bi is meaningful only if bound (below 7 MeV excitation); above threshold, collectivity vanishes and the resonance becomes very broad.

The dominance of low-$\ell$ single-particle states in transfer amplitudes means the GPV peak "melts" due to the width acquired from these states. This, combined with Q-value mismatch, accounts for the elusive nature of the GPV in heavy nuclei.

## Key Results

1. The GPV is predicted at $\sim 65/A^{1/3}$ MeV with a cross section $\sim \Omega \cdot \sigma_{sp}$ in the degenerate limit, scaling as $A^{2/3}$.
2. Despite extensive experimental searches in Sn and Pb isotopes using (p,t), (t,p), and ($^6$He,$\alpha$) reactions, the GPV has not been definitively identified in heavy nuclei.
3. Q-value mismatch exponentially suppresses the cross section for conventional reactions; weakly-bound projectiles ($^6$He, $^{11}$Li) can overcome this limitation.
4. Continuum effects (Berggren representation) show the GPV in $^{210}$Pb is very broad and merges with the continuum background.
5. The GPV connection to alpha-decay formation probability was independently rediscovered through neutron-proton clustering calculations.
6. The observation in light nuclei ($^{14,15}$C) while failing in heavy nuclei provides unexpected insight into the relationship between Landau, doorway, and compound damping mechanisms of giant resonances.
7. Future searches should explore charge-exchange reactions and tritium beams.
