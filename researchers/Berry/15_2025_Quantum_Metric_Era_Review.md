# From Berry curvature to quantum metric: a new era of quantum geometry metrology for Bloch electrons in solids

**Author(s):** Bohm-Jung Yang
**Year:** 2025 (published January 2026)
**Journal:** arXiv preprint (cond-mat.mtrl-sci)
**arXiv:** 2512.24553
**Relevance:** HIGH

---

## Abstract

For decades, "geometry" in band theory has largely meant Berry phase and Berry curvature -- quantities that reshape semiclassical dynamics and underpin modern topological matter. Yet the full geometric content of a Bloch band is richer and encoded in the quantum geometric tensor (QGT), whose imaginary part is the Berry curvature and whose real part is the quantum metric. Here, we briefly review the recent progress in direct experimental access to the QGT in real crystalline solids using the polarization- and spin-resolved angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES). The extraction of the QGT in momentum space was successfully addressed by two different approaches: One is by introducing quasi-QGT that faithfully represents the QGT and is directly measurable by ARPES. The other is through pseudospin tomography in a material with simple low energy band structure, which successfully retrieved all matrix components of the quantum metric. We discuss the physical meaning of these two recent progresses, their implication/limitation, and open directions.

---

## Key Arguments and Derivations

The paper reviews two 2025 experimental breakthroughs in directly measuring the quantum geometric tensor (QGT) in real crystalline solids:

**Kang et al. (Nature Physics 2025):** Developed a "quasi-QGT" framework applicable to multiband crystalline solids. The real part of the quasi-QGT corresponds to the band Drude weight (measurable via band curvature analysis in high-resolution ARPES), while the imaginary part corresponds to the orbital angular momentum (measurable via circular-dichroism and spin-resolved ARPES). Demonstrated in kagome metal CoSn near avoided band crossings.

**Kim et al. (Science 2025):** Direct measurement of the full quantum metric tensor in black phosphorus via pseudospin tomography. Near the valence-band maximum, the electronic structure is described by an effective two-band Hamiltonian. Symmetries constrain the pseudospin to a great circle of the Bloch sphere, parameterized by a single rotation angle reconstructed from polarization-dependent ARPES. Key result: the quantum metric anisotropy is far stronger than the energy dispersion anisotropy. In black phosphorus, Berry curvature vanishes identically (space-time inversion symmetry), so the quantum metric fully characterizes the QGT.

**Physical effects requiring quantum metric (not just Berry curvature):** Anomalous flat-band Landau levels, geometric superconductivity (superfluid stiffness in flat bands), nonlinear Hall effects, excitonic Lamb shifts, geometric orbital susceptibility.

---

## Key Results

1. The quantum metric can now be directly measured in real solids via ARPES, not merely inferred from transport.
2. In black phosphorus, the quantum metric anisotropy exceeds the energy dispersion anisotropy -- geometry and dispersion are independent.
3. The quasi-QGT framework provides a general route to QGT measurement applicable to multiband systems.
4. Berry curvature can vanish identically (e.g., with space-time inversion symmetry) while the quantum metric remains large and measurable.
5. Quantum metric controls superfluid stiffness in flat bands, anomalous Landau levels, and nonlinear responses.

## Relevance to Phonon-Exflation

This paper directly validates the physical regime of the phonon-exflation framework. The ERRATUM result -- vanishing Berry curvature ($\mathrm{Im}(T) = 0$) with large quantum metric ($\mathrm{Re}(T) = 982.5$) -- is precisely the situation realized in black phosphorus, where space-time inversion symmetry forces $F_{ij} = 0$ while the quantum metric remains large and anisotropic. The experimental demonstration that quantum metric controls physical observables (superfluid stiffness, Landau levels, nonlinear responses) independently of Berry curvature confirms that the framework's "sensitivity without topological protection" is a physically real and measurable regime, not a theoretical artifact.
