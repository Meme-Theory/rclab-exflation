## Abstract

The Standard Model lacks an organizing principle to describe quark and lepton "flavours". Neutrino oscillation experiments show that leptons mix very differently from quarks, adding a major challenge to the flavour puzzle. We briefly sketch the seesaw and the dark-matter-mediated "scotogenic" neutrino mass generation approaches. We discuss the limitations of popular neutrino mixing patterns and examine the possibility that they arise from symmetry, giving a bottom-up approach to residual flavour and CP symmetries. We show how such family and/or CP symmetries can yield novel, viable and predictive mixing patterns. Model-independent ways to predict lepton mixing and neutrino mass sum rules are reviewed. We also discuss UV-complete flavour theories in four and more space-time dimensions. As benchmark examples we present an A4 scotogenic construction with trimaximal mixing pattern TM2 and another with S4 flavour symmetry and generalized CP symmetry. Higher-dimensional flavour completions are also briefly discussed, such as 5-D warped flavordynamics with a T' symmetry yielding a TM1 mixing pattern, detectable neutrinoless double beta decay rates and a very good global fit of flavour observables. We also mention 6-D orbifolds as a way to fix the structure of the 4-D family symmetry. We give a scotogenic benchmark orbifold model predicting the "golden" quark-lepton mass relation, stringent neutrino oscillation parameter regions, and an excellent global flavour fit, including quark observables. Finally, we discuss promising recent progress in tackling the flavor issue through the use of modular symmetries.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### 1. The Flavour Puzzle (Section 1)

The SM has 22 free parameters in the flavour sector (6 quark masses, 3 charged lepton masses, 3 neutrino masses, 4 CKM parameters, 4 PMNS parameters, 2 Majorana phases). Understanding these hierarchies and mixing patterns is one of the deepest open problems.

### 2. Neutrino Mass Generation (Section 2)

Reviews seesaw mechanisms (Type I, II, III), inverse seesaw, linear seesaw, and scotogenic (radiative) mass generation where dark matter mediates neutrino mass.

### 3. Mixing Patterns (Sections 3-5)

Discusses tri-bimaximal (TBM), golden ratio, bi-maximal, and their generalizations (TM1, TM2, bi-large). Shows how residual symmetries in the charged lepton and neutrino sectors can predict mixing patterns.

### 4. Flavour and CP Symmetries (Section 6)

Bottom-up reconstruction of mixing from residual flavour symmetry G_l in the charged lepton sector and G_nu in the neutrino sector. Combining discrete flavour symmetries with generalized CP symmetries yields highly predictive patterns.

### 5. UV-Complete Models (Sections 8-9)

Benchmark A4 scotogenic model, S4 + CP model, warped T' model in 5D, and 6D orbifold models. The orbifold approach fixes the 4D family symmetry from extra-dimensional geometry.

### 6. Modular Symmetries (Section 10)

Modular flavour symmetries use the modular group Gamma_N (finite quotients of SL(2,Z)) where Yukawa couplings are modular forms. The modulus tau serves as the sole source of flavour symmetry breaking.

## Key Results

1. Comprehensive review of discrete flavour symmetry groups (A4, S4, T', Delta(27), etc.) and their predictions for mixing patterns.
2. Residual symmetry approach provides model-independent predictions for mixing angles and CP phases.
3. Mass sum rules from flavour symmetries provide testable predictions for neutrinoless double beta decay.
4. Warped 5D model with T' yields TM1 mixing and excellent global fit.
5. 6D orbifold model predicts "golden" quark-lepton mass relation and stringent oscillation parameter regions.
6. Modular symmetries offer a promising new approach where Yukawa couplings are modular forms of a single complex modulus.

## Relevance to Phonon-Exflation

This review is directly relevant as a comprehensive survey of the flavour problem that the phonon-exflation framework addresses from a geometric/spectral perspective. The framework derives mass ratios from the Dirac spectrum on SU(3), which is a fundamentally different approach from the discrete flavour symmetry program reviewed here. Key contrasts: (1) the framework's mass ratios come from geometry (tau-dependent eigenvalues) rather than from group-theoretic texture zeros; (2) the modular symmetry approach using a complex modulus tau has a structural parallel to the framework's moduli parameter tau controlling the SU(3) metric.
