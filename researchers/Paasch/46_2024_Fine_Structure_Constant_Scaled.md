# The Fine-Structure Constant as a Scaled Quantity

**Author(s):** Harry Sticker
**Year:** 2024 (2025 preprint)
**Journal:** Submitted to Studies in History and Philosophy of Science
**arXiv:** 2512.07027
**Relevance:** MEDIUM

## Abstract

The fine-structure constant alpha ~ 1/137 is traditionally regarded as a fundamental dimensionless parameter. I argue instead that alpha is a scaled quantity that arises only where the structural scales contributed by classical electromagnetism (e), quantum mechanics (hbar), and special relativity (c) intersect. None of these theories, taken individually, supplies the independent scales required to define alpha. The constant first appears when relativistic corrections are added to the Schrodinger-Bohr description of hydrogen (Sommerfeld), and it becomes the structural coupling in quantum electrodynamics, where quantum and relativistic effects modify the classical electromagnetic interaction. Expressing the governing laws in canonical form reveals this dependence and eliminates representational artifacts that make alpha appear fundamental. The running of alpha in QED further demonstrates its status as a scale-dependent coupling rather than a universal constant. I conclude that alpha is a domain-specific structural ratio reflecting contingent relationships among independent physical scales.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### 1. Alpha as a Scaled Quantity (Section 3)

A scaled quantity is a dimensionless ratio R = A/B where A and B are independent structural parameters of a theory, where the theory renders their comparison physically interpretable, and where outside that theoretical context the ratio lacks meaning. Examples: Reynolds number (fluid mechanics), beta = v/c (relativity), Mach number.

Alpha belongs to this category: it requires the simultaneous presence of electromagnetic coupling (e), quantum action (hbar), and relativistic speed (c). Outside the domain where these three scales coexist, alpha does not arise.

### 2. Classical Electromagnetism Cannot Define Alpha (Section 4.1)

The rescaling symmetry q -> lambda*q, epsilon_0 -> epsilon_0/lambda^2 leaves all predictions invariant. Classical EM has no intrinsic strength scale.

### 3. Non-Relativistic QM Cannot Define Alpha (Section 4.2)

QM introduces hbar but e^2/(4pi epsilon_0 hbar) has dimensions of velocity. Without a distinguished speed (no invariant c in Galilean mechanics), no dimensionless coupling can be formed.

### 4. Emergence at Framework Intersection (Section 4.4)

Alpha crystallizes at the intersection of EM, QM, and SR:
- Sommerfeld (1916): v/c ~ alpha for the Bohr orbit
- QED: interaction amplitudes scale with alpha = e^2/(4pi epsilon_0 hbar c)
- Running: alpha(0) ~ 1/137.036, alpha(m_Z) ~ 1/128.9

### 5. Canonical Forms (Section 5)

Natural units (hbar = c = 1) obscure alpha's structural dependencies by absorbing quantum and relativistic scales. Canonical form alpha = e^2/(4pi epsilon_0 hbar c) makes all three independent scales explicit.

### 6. Against Mathematical Derivability (Section 6.1)

Expecting alpha to emerge from pure mathematics is a category mistake. The Reynolds number cannot be derived mathematically; neither can alpha. Both encode empirical magnitudes of independent physical scales.

## Key Results

1. Alpha is a domain-specific scaled quantity, not a fundamental constant of nature.
2. It requires the joint presence of electromagnetic, quantum, and relativistic structures.
3. The running of alpha in QED confirms its scale-dependent, framework-bound nature.
4. Canonical representation reveals hidden dependencies obscured by natural units.
5. The search for a purely mathematical derivation of alpha is misdirected.
6. Dimensionlessness does not imply fundamentality (contra Duff).
