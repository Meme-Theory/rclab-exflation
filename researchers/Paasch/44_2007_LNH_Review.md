## Abstract

Large dimensionless numbers, arising out of ratios of various physical constants, intrigued many scientists, especially Dirac. Relying on the coincidence of large numbers, Dirac arrived at the revolutionary hypothesis that the gravitational constant G should vary inversely as the cosmic time t. This hypothesis of Dirac, known as Large Number Hypothesis (LNH), sparked off many speculations, arguments and new ideas in terms of applications. Works done by several authors with LNH as their basic platform are reviewed in this work. Relationship between some of those works are pointed out here. Possibility of time-variations of physical constants other than G are also discussed.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### 1. Large Numbers (Section 2)

The history of large dimensionless numbers begins with Weyl (1919), who noted that the ratio of the electron's electromagnetic radius to a hypothetical gravitational energy radius is ~4 x 10^{42}. This equals:
- F_e/F_g (electrostatic to gravitational force ratio for electrons)
- E_0/E_g (electromagnetic to gravitational self-energy ratio)

Eddington connected this to N = total number of particles in the universe: F_e/F_g = sqrt(N) = "Weyl's number", giving N ~ 1.75 x 10^{85} (Eddington's magic number).

### 2. Dirac's LNH (Section 3)

Dirac (1937, 1938) noted that the ratio of electromagnetic to gravitational forces (~10^{40}) is of the same order as the age of the universe in atomic units (T/t_atomic ~ 10^{40}). Rather than treating this as coincidence, Dirac proposed G ~ 1/t, so that the ratio remains ~T at all epochs.

Three categories of work followed:
- I. Modifications of Einstein gravity to accommodate G(t)
- II. Arguments for and against LNH
- III. Testing and applications

### 3. Modifications of Gravitational Theory (Section 4)

- **Brans-Dicke theory**: G replaced by 1/phi where phi is a scalar field
- **Hoyle-Narlikar theory**: Variable G in steady-state cosmology
- **Dirac's two-metrics theory**: Atomic and gravitational metrics evolve differently
- **Canuto et al.**: Scale-covariant theory accommodating varying G

### 4. Arguments For and Against (Section 5)

**For**: Cosmic coincidences, connection to accelerating universe, variable Lambda
**Against**: Constraints from Big Bang nucleosynthesis (BBN), binary pulsar timing, lunar laser ranging (G-dot/G < 10^{-12}/yr)

### 5. Connection to Dark Energy (Sections 1, 7)

The paper notes parallels between LNH (G decreasing with cosmic time) and the cosmological constant Lambda (also decreasing from a high initial value). Variable Lambda models feature particle creation, which is also a feature of LNH. The link between LNH and dark energy cannot be ignored.

### 6. Applications (Section 6)

LNH has been applied to: flat galactic rotation curves, unification of LNH with GR, implications of variable fine-structure constant, and connections to the accelerating universe.

## Key Results

1. Dirac's LNH: G ~ 1/t (gravitational constant decreases with cosmic time).
2. The large number ~10^{40} connects electromagnetic/gravitational force ratio to the age of the universe in atomic units.
3. Eddington's number N ~ 10^{85} connects force ratios to total particle number.
4. Observational constraints: G-dot/G < 10^{-12}/yr from binary pulsars and LLR.
5. LNH parallels the decreasing cosmological constant Lambda(t).
6. Variable G models generally predict particle creation.
