# Progress in Lunar Laser Ranging Tests of Relativistic Gravity

**Author(s):** James G. Williams, Slava G. Turyshev, Dale H. Boggs
**Year:** 2004
**Journal:** Physical Review Letters (submitted)
**arXiv:** gr-qc/0411113
**Relevance:** MEDIUM

---

## Abstract

Analyses of laser ranges to the Moon provide increasingly stringent limits on any violation of the equivalence principle (EP); they also enable several very accurate tests of relativistic gravity. These analyses give an EP test of Delta(M_G/M_I)_EP = (-1.0 +/- 1.4) x 10^{-13}. This result yields a strong equivalence principle (SEP) test of Delta(M_G/M_I)_SEP = (-2.0 +/- 2.0) x 10^{-13}. Also, the corresponding SEP violation parameter eta is (4.4 +/- 4.5) x 10^{-4}, where eta = 4beta - gamma - 3 and both beta and gamma are post-Newtonian parameters. Using the Cassini gamma, the eta result yields beta - 1 = (1.2 +/- 1.1) x 10^{-4}. The geodetic precession test, expressed as a relative deviation from general relativity, is K_gp = -0.0019 +/- 0.0064. The search for a time variation in the gravitational constant results in G-dot/G = (4 +/- 9) x 10^{-13} yr^{-1}; consequently there is no evidence for local (~1 AU) scale expansion of the solar system.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### 1. Lunar Laser Ranging (LLR) Overview

LLR measures the round-trip light time between an observatory on Earth and corner cube retroreflectors placed on the Moon by Apollo 11, 14, 15 and Soviet Luna 17, 21 missions. The dataset spans March 1970 to April 2004 with 15,553 normal points. The weighted rms scatter for the last decade of ranges is ~2 cm, or 0.5 x 10^{-10} relative to the 385,000 km Earth-Moon distance.

### 2. Equivalence Principle Tests

The EP test compares free-fall accelerations of the Earth and Moon toward the Sun. A violation would produce a lunar orbit perturbation with a 29.53-day synodic period (distinct from the 27-day orbital period). The measured EP violation is Delta(M_G/M_I) = (-1.0 +/- 1.4) x 10^{-13}. Combined with University of Washington laboratory EP experiments, the SEP-specific test yields Delta(M_G/M_I)_SEP = (-2.0 +/- 2.0) x 10^{-13}.

### 3. PPN Parameter beta

Using the Cassini spacecraft determination of gamma (gamma - 1 = (2.1 +/- 2.3) x 10^{-5}) combined with the LLR eta measurement, the PPN parameter beta is constrained to beta - 1 = (1.2 +/- 1.1) x 10^{-4}, consistent with GR (beta = 1).

### 4. Time Variation of G

The analysis constrains G-dot/G = (4 +/- 9) x 10^{-13} yr^{-1}. This uncertainty is 83 times smaller than the inverse age of the universe (t_0 = 13.4 Gyr). No evidence for local-scale expansion of the solar system is found. Isotropic orbit expansion satisfies a-dot/a = -G-dot/G.

### 5. Geodetic Precession

The de Sitter (geodetic) precession test yields K_gp = -0.0019 +/- 0.0064, consistent with GR prediction (K_gp = 0). High correlation (0.88) exists with the lunar potential Love number and lunar core oblateness.

## Key Results

1. EP test: Delta(M_G/M_I)_EP = (-1.0 +/- 1.4) x 10^{-13}
2. SEP test: Delta(M_G/M_I)_SEP = (-2.0 +/- 2.0) x 10^{-13}
3. SEP violation parameter: eta = (4.4 +/- 4.5) x 10^{-4}
4. PPN beta: beta - 1 = (1.2 +/- 1.1) x 10^{-4}
5. Time variation of G: G-dot/G = (4 +/- 9) x 10^{-13} yr^{-1}
6. Geodetic precession: K_gp = -0.0019 +/- 0.0064
7. No evidence for local (~1 AU) scale expansion of the solar system

## Relevance to Phonon-Exflation

The LLR constraint G-dot/G = (4 +/- 9) x 10^{-13} yr^{-1} sets a direct observational bound on any time variation of the gravitational constant. In the phonon-exflation framework, the internal compactification modulus tau evolves in time, and if G depends on the internal volume (as in standard Kaluza-Klein theory), then the LLR bound constrains the present-day rate of tau evolution. The clock constraint (Session 22d) predicts dalpha/alpha = -3.08 * tau_dot, making the LLR G-dot bound complementary to the varying-alpha constraints from quasar spectra. Any viable tau(t) trajectory must satisfy both constraints simultaneously.
