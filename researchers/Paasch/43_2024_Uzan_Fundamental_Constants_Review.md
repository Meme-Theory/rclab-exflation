## Abstract

Fundamental constants are a cornerstone of our physical laws. Any constant varying in space and/or time would signal a violation of local position invariance and be associated with a violation of the universality of free fall, and hence of the weak equivalence principle at the heart of the geometrisation of gravity. It will also reflect the existence of new degrees of freedom that couple to standard matter fields. Thus, testing for the stability of fundamental constants is of utmost importance for our understanding of gravity and for characterizing the domain of validity of General Relativity. Besides, it opens an independent window on the dark matter and dark energy components.

This review details the various roles of the fundamental constants in the laws of physics and in the construction of the International System of units, which now depends strongly on them. This requires to distinguish the concepts of fundamental units and fundamental parameters. Then, the relations between constants, the tests of the local position invariance and of the universality of free fall are presented, as well as the construction of field theories that account for "varying constants" and the motivations arising from high-energy physics and string theory.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### 1. Definition and Classification of Constants (Section 2)

Distinguishes between:
- **Fundamental units**: c, hbar, k_B (set scales but carry no physical information beyond the unit system)
- **Fundamental parameters**: alpha, m_e/m_p, etc. (dimensionless ratios that encode physics)

Constants are categorized by their role in the theoretical framework. The number of fundamental constants depends on the theory.

### 2. Varying Constants and General Relativity (Section 2.2)

Any time/space variation of a fundamental constant implies:
- Violation of Local Position Invariance (LPI)
- Associated violation of the Universality of Free Fall (UFF) / Weak Equivalence Principle
- Existence of new dynamical fields coupled to matter

The link between LPI violation and UFF violation is made precise through the formalism of scalar-tensor theories.

### 3. Theoretical Frameworks (Section 3)

- **Scalar-tensor theories**: Varying G coupled to a scalar field
- **Bekenstein-type models**: Varying alpha from a dilaton field
- **Kaluza-Klein theories**: Constants vary with internal space size (Section 3.4.1)
- **String theory**: Dilaton and moduli fields control constants (Section 3.4.2)
- **Chameleon/screening mechanisms**: How light fields can hide in dense environments

### 4. Relations Between Constants (Section 4)

Gauge coupling unification implies relations between alpha, alpha_s, and alpha_W variations. Masses and binding energies expressed in terms of fundamental constants. The (R, S) parameterization relates observable quantities to primary varying parameters.

### 5. Experimental Constraints (Section 5)

Comprehensive review of constraints from:
- Atomic clocks: dalpha/alpha < 10^{-19}/yr
- Oklo natural reactor: dalpha/alpha < 10^{-8} over 2 Gyr
- Meteorite dating: dalpha/alpha constraints over 4.6 Gyr
- Quasar absorption spectra: Webb et al. dipole claim, other null results
- Stellar physics: BBN, CMB constraints
- Lunar laser ranging: dG/G < 10^{-13}/yr

### 6. Anthropic Considerations (Section 12)

Discussion of the fine-tuning of constants and anthropic arguments, including the landscape/multiverse perspective and its limitations.

## Key Results

1. Any varying constant implies new physics: violation of equivalence principle and new dynamical fields.
2. Current best constraints: dalpha/alpha ~ 10^{-19}/yr (atomic clocks), dG/G ~ 10^{-13}/yr (LLR).
3. Kaluza-Klein and string theory naturally predict varying constants through dynamical moduli.
4. The (R, S) parameterization provides a systematic framework for comparing different observational constraints.
5. Spatial variation (Webb dipole): delta_alpha/alpha ~ 10^{-5} across the sky — contested but not ruled out.
6. Over 1200 references cataloguing the state of the field as of 2025.
