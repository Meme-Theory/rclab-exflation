# THE ROTATING DYONIC BLACK HOLES OF KALUZA-KLEIN THEORY

**Author(s):** Dean Rasheed
**Year:** 1995
**Journal:** arXiv preprint (DAMTP Cambridge)
**arXiv:** hep-th/9505038
**Relevance:** MEDIUM

---

## Abstract

The most general electrically and magnetically charged rotating black hole solutions of 5 dimensional Kaluza-Klein theory are given in an explicit form. Various classical quantities associated with the black holes are derived. In particular, one finds the very surprising result that the gyromagnetic and gyroelectric ratios can become arbitrarily large. The thermodynamic quantities of the black holes are calculated and a Smarr-type formula is obtained leading to a generalized first law of black hole thermodynamics. The properties of the extreme solutions are investigated and it is shown how they naturally separate into two classes. The extreme solutions in one class are found to have two unusual properties: (i). Their event horizons have zero angular velocity and yet they have non-zero ADM angular momentum. (ii). In certain circumstances it is possible to add angular momentum to these extreme solutions without changing the mass or charges and yet still maintain an extreme solution. Regarding the extreme black holes as elementary particles, their stability is discussed and it is found that they are stable provided they have sufficient angular momentum.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### Kaluza-Klein reduction and sigma-model structure

Starting from 5D vacuum gravity with a Killing x^5, the metric decomposes as ds^2_{(5)} = e^{4 sigma/sqrt(3)} (dx^5 + 2 A_mu dx^mu)^2 + e^{-2 sigma/sqrt(3)} g_{mu nu} dx^mu dx^nu, which reduces to 4D Einstein-Maxwell-dilaton theory with dilaton coupling b = sqrt(3). For stationary solutions with an additional Killing partial/partial t, a further 3+2 decomposition introduces a unimodular 3x3 matrix chi containing lambda_{ab} (the 2x2 metric in (t, x^5) directions) and twist potentials V_a. The remaining Einstein equations become a 3D sigma-model on the symmetric space SL(3,R)/SO(3): (chi^{-1} chi_{,i})_{;i} = 0 and R_{ij} = (1/4) Tr(chi^{-1} chi_{,i} chi^{-1} chi_{,j}).

### Solution generation via SO(1,2)

The sigma-model equations are manifestly SL(3,R) invariant, but asymptotic flatness (chi -> eta = diag(-1, 1, -1)) restricts to the subgroup SO(1,2). Solutions are generated by applying SO(1,2) to the Kerr solution's chi matrix chi_K. A further restriction (preventing NUT charge from appearing) yields a two-parameter family labeled by boost parameters alpha, beta related by tan(2 gamma) = tanh(alpha) sinh(beta). The restricted set is not a group.

### Static dyonic solutions

For static solutions, chi = eta e^{A f} with A a constant matrix satisfying Tr A = 0 and A^T = eta A eta. The most general such A has parameters M (mass), P (magnetic charge), Q (electric charge), and Sigma (dilaton charge), with the NUT charge N set to zero. Solutions split into two classes: non-singular A (generically singular at the horizon unless Sigma = 0, giving Schwarzschild) and singular A (det A = 0, the cubic constraint Q^2/(Sigma + M sqrt(3)) + P^2/(Sigma - M sqrt(3)) = 2 Sigma/3). The singular class contains the 5D plane wave (Q = 2M, P = 0, Sigma = M sqrt(3)), the Gross-Perry-Sorkin magnetic monopole (P = 2M, Q = 0, Sigma = -M sqrt(3)), and the extreme P = Q = M/sqrt(2) Reissner-Nordstrom embedding (Sigma = 0). Extreme static dyons satisfy the "astroid" (|Q|/M)^{2/3} + (|P|/M)^{2/3} = 2^{2/3}.

### Family of extreme curves vs dilaton coupling

Combining Bogomol'nyi bound |Q/M| = sqrt(1+b^2) (pure electric) and the Reissner-Nordstrom point P = Q = M/sqrt(2), the extreme dyon curves in the (|Q/M|, |P/M|) plane form a one-parameter family indexed by b: (|Q/M|)^n + (|P/M|)^n = (1 + b^2)^{n/2}, n = 2/(1 + log_2(1+b^2)). This recovers the Einstein-Maxwell circle (b = 0, n = 2), the string-theory square-root curve (b = 1, n = 1), and the KK astroid (b = sqrt(3), n = 2/3).

### Rotating dyonic solutions

Applying SO(1,2) to the Kerr chi_K (with parameters M_K, a) produces the rotating dyonic metric in the form ds^2_{(5)} = (B/A) (dx^5 + 2 A_mu dx^mu)^2 + sqrt(A/B) ds^2_{(4)}, with A and B polynomial in r and theta depending on M, P, Q, Sigma, a, and J. The new parameters relate to M_K, a, alpha, beta by explicit formulas. Notably, J^2 = a^2 [(M + Sigma/sqrt(3))^2 - Q^2][(M - Sigma/sqrt(3))^2 - P^2] / (M^2 + Sigma^2 - P^2 - Q^2), showing that J and a are not independent.

### Extreme surface: S and W components

The extreme-solution surface in (|P|/M, |Q|/M, |J|/M^2) space decomposes into two pieces: surface **S** from boosting extreme Kerr (a = M_K) with J >= PQ, and a vertical wall **W** reached as beta -> infinity, on which a = M_K = 0, J <= PQ, and (P/M)^{2/3} + (Q/M)^{2/3} = 2^{2/3}. The two meet at J = PQ. Surfaces S and W behave completely differently:

- **S**: nonzero a, nonzero Omega_H, ergoregion, standard rotating behavior.
- **W**: a = 0, Omega_H = 0, no ergoregion, yet nonzero ADM angular momentum, and not spherically symmetric. Adding angular momentum up to J = PQ keeps the solution extreme with M, P, Q fixed -- an Einstein-Maxwell impossibility.

### Gyromagnetic and gyroelectric ratios

For the general rotating dyon the gyromagnetic and gyroelectric ratios are g_M = 2M(M - Sigma/sqrt(3))/((M + Sigma/sqrt(3))^2 - Q^2) and g_E = 2M(M + Sigma/sqrt(3))/((M - Sigma/sqrt(3))^2 - P^2). Both are bounded below by 1 but unbounded above: e.g., as P -> 0, Q -> 2M, Sigma -> sqrt(3) M, g_E diverges. This contrasts sharply with Kerr-Newman, which always has g_M = g_E = 2. For P = Q (Sigma = 0) they are equal and range from 2 to 4 (extreme limit). The pure electric case has g_M = 2 - v^2, matching prior work.

### Thermodynamics and first law

The outer horizon is at r_+ = M + sqrt(M^2 + Sigma^2 - P^2 - Q^2 - a^2). Surface gravity kappa = (r_+ - M)/sqrt(AB)|_{r=r_+, theta=0} vanishes in the extreme limit on both S and W. Area A can be written in terms of J, a, r_+, M, Sigma, P, Q. The generalized Smarr formula is M = kappa A/(4 pi) + 2 Omega_H J + Phi_H Q + Psi_H P, and Euler's theorem yields the generalized first law dM = (kappa/(8 pi)) dA + Omega_H dJ + Phi_H dQ + Psi_H dP.

### Stability of extreme black holes

Treating extreme BHs as particles, the stability condition is the triangle inequality M(x_1 + x_2) <= M(x_1) + M(x_2) on the conserved-charge vector. For non-rotating dyons this holds iff b <= 1 (Minkowski's inequality); at b = sqrt(3) (KK) it fails, so non-rotating extreme dyons are unstable. Including J, the condition (with J as a homogeneous function of degree 2 in M, Q, P) becomes J(x_1 + x_2) >= J(x_1) + J(x_2) -- the unit ball J = 1 must be concave. Computing the Hessian shows this holds for S (J >= PQ) but fails for W (J < PQ). Thus rotating dyons on S are stable; those on W are unstable. Super-radiance from S solutions depletes angular momentum, but as J -> PQ, a -> 0 and the ergoregion disappears, shutting off super-radiance; solutions asymptotically approach J = PQ but never cross into the unstable W region.

## Key Results

1. Explicit construction of the most general electrically and magnetically charged rotating black hole solutions of 5D Kaluza-Klein theory, parametrized by (M, P, Q, J).
2. Extreme-solution surface decomposes into two pieces S (J >= PQ, standard rotation) and W (J <= PQ, zero Omega_H but nonzero J).
3. Non-rotating extreme dyons lie on the astroid (|Q|/M)^{2/3} + (|P|/M)^{2/3} = 2^{2/3}.
4. Dilaton coupling determines a family of extreme curves (|Q/M|)^n + (|P/M|)^n = (1+b^2)^{n/2} with n = 2/(1 + log_2(1+b^2)).
5. Gyromagnetic and gyroelectric ratios can become arbitrarily large in the dyonic case, in contrast to Kerr-Newman (g = 2).
6. The dilaton charge Sigma satisfies the cubic constraint Q^2/(Sigma + M sqrt(3)) + P^2/(Sigma - M sqrt(3)) = 2 Sigma/3.
7. Generalized Smarr formula: M = kappa A/(4 pi) + 2 Omega_H J + Phi_H Q + Psi_H P.
8. Generalized first law: dM = (kappa/(8 pi)) dA + Omega_H dJ + Phi_H dQ + Psi_H dP.
9. Extreme dyons on S with J >= PQ are stable against splitting; those on W with J < PQ are unstable; super-radiance drives solutions to the J = PQ line but not across it.
10. On W, extreme solutions have zero event-horizon area (entropy S = 0), zero angular velocity, no ergoregion, yet nonzero ADM J -- a non-rotating-in-the-sense-of-Omega_H = 0 solution that is nonetheless neither static nor spherically symmetric.
