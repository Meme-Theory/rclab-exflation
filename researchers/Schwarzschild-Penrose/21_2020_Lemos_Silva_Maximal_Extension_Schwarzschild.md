## Abstract

We find a specific coordinate system that goes from the Painlevé-Gullstrand partial extension to the Kruskal-Szekeres maximal extension and thus exhibit the maximal extension of the Schwarzschild metric in a unified picture. We do this by adopting two time coordinates, one being the proper time of a congruence of outgoing timelike geodesics, the other being the proper time of a congruence of ingoing timelike geodesics, both parameterized by the same energy per unit mass E. E is in the range 1 ≤ E < ∞ with the limit E = ∞ yielding the Kruskal-Szekeres maximal extension. So, through such an integrated description one sees that the Kruskal-Szekeres solution belongs to this family of extensions parameterized by E. Our family of extensions is different from the Novikov-Lemaître family parameterized also by the energy E of timelike geodesics, with the Novikov extension holding for 0 < E < 1 and being maximal, and the Lemaître extension holding for 1 ≤ E < ∞ and being partial, not maximal, and moreover its E = ∞ limit evanescing in a Minkowski spacetime rather than ending in the Kruskal-Szekeres spacetime.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

*Substitute reference for Kruskal 1960 maximal extension paper (Phys. Rev. 119:1743, pre-arXiv).*

**Section I — Introduction.** The history runs from Schwarzschild (1916), Droste (1917), and Hilbert (1917) to Birkhoff's uniqueness theorem (1923). The problem of extending the solution across r = 2M was tackled in stages: Einstein-Rosen bridges (1935), Painlevé-Gullstrand coordinates using proper time of infalling test particles with E = 1 (Painlevé 1921, Gullstrand 1922), Lemaître (1933) with a comoving radial coordinate, Novikov (1963) for 0 < E < 1 (maximal), and Eddington-Finkelstein null coordinates. Kruskal (1960) and Szekeres (1960) produced the maximal extension in a single coordinate patch using both outgoing and ingoing null geodesics, exhibiting the nontraversable Einstein-Rosen bridge connecting two asymptotically flat regions through white-hole and black-hole interiors.

**Section II — Schwarzschild in double Painlevé-Gullstrand form.** Starting from standard Schwarzschild (1), the authors introduce two time coordinates t and τ parameterized by a single energy E ≥ 1: one associated with outgoing timelike geodesics (eq. 2), one with ingoing (eq. 3). The combined transformation yields the double-Painlevé-Gullstrand line element (eq. 6) that couples dt and dτ through a cross-term involving (2E² − 1 + 2M/r). For E = 1 and E > 1 the solution to r(t, τ) differs; E = ∞ is the null limit.

**Section III — Maximal extension for E > 1.** The inverse transformation is solved explicitly (eqs. 7-8). A further analytical extension sets t'/M = −exp(−t/4ME) and τ'/M = exp(τ/4ME) and yields the line element (13) and implicit defining relation (14) for r(t', τ'). The r = 2M sphere becomes the null surfaces t' = 0 and τ' = 0; the true curvature singularity r = 0 becomes two spacelike hyperbolae t'τ'/M² = 1. Four regions I-IV appear: two asymptotically flat exterior sheets connected by a dynamic nontraversable Einstein-Rosen bridge whose throat expands to r = 2M from a white hole singularity and recollapses into a black hole. The coordinates t' and τ' remain timelike everywhere except on the horizons.

**Section IV — E = 1 limit.** Taking E → 1 directly from the generic case (limiting the logarithms) produces (eqs. 15-18, 21, 22) the same four-region structure. The global topology and causal diagram are essentially identical to the E > 1 case.

**Section V — E = ∞ limit: Kruskal-Szekeres.** Redefining u ≡ lim_{E→∞} t/E and v ≡ lim_{E→∞} τ/E yields the null form u = t − r − 2M ln|r/2M − 1| and v = t + r + 2M ln|r/2M − 1| (eqs. 23-24). The line element becomes ds² = −(1 − 2M/r) du dv + r²dΩ² (eq. 25). Further rescaling u'/M = −exp(−u/4M) and v'/M = exp(v/4M) produces the Kruskal-Szekeres line element ds² = −(32M/r) exp(−r/2M) du' dv' + r²dΩ² (eq. 29) with r(u', v') defined implicitly by (r/2M − 1) exp(r/2M) = −u'v'/M² (eq. 30). In this limit u' and v' are null coordinates; the timelike character of t' and τ' in the E > 1 generic case collapses onto null lines. This shows that Kruskal-Szekeres is the E = ∞ member of a one-parameter family of maximal extensions.

**Section VI — Causal diagrams.** Figures 4-7 trace the radial null geodesics for E = 1, 1.1, 1.5, and ∞. In the finite-E cases the lines of constant t' and τ' are spacelike hypersurfaces (tachyonic) except at the horizons t' = 0 and τ' = 0 where they are null. In the E = ∞ limit these spacelike lines become the null lines u' = const and v' = const of Kruskal-Szekeres. The contravariant normals n^(t'a) and n^(τ'a) are always inside the local light cone for finite E and become null in the Kruskal-Szekeres limit.

**Section VII — Conclusions.** The Painlevé-Gullstrand branch splits into two bifurcation branches: Novikov-Lemaître (maximal only for 0 < E < 1, ending in Minkowski as E → ∞) and the new double-Painlevé-Gullstrand family (maximal for 1 ≤ E < ∞, ending in Kruskal-Szekeres as E → ∞). The Kruskal-Szekeres maximal extension is the Eddington-Finkelstein null endpoint of the second branch.

## Key Results

1. A one-parameter family of maximal analytic extensions of Schwarzschild parameterized by the energy per unit mass E ∈ [1, ∞) of ingoing and outgoing timelike geodesics is constructed.
2. The Kruskal-Szekeres maximal extension is the E = ∞ limiting member of this family.
3. The family contains the Painlevé-Gullstrand-type limit at E = 1 and connects smoothly to Kruskal-Szekeres by letting E grow.
4. In the E > 1 cases the analytically extended coordinates t' and τ' are timelike (normals timelike); at E = ∞ they collapse onto the null coordinates u' and v'.
5. Every member of the family exhibits the four-region structure: two asymptotically flat exterior sheets connected via a nontraversable Einstein-Rosen wormhole through a white-hole region (past singularity) and a black-hole region (future singularity).

## Relevance to Phonon-Exflation

The Kruskal-Szekeres maximal extension is the paradigmatic example of how a metric written in coordinates that appear singular on a hypersurface (here r = 2M) can be globally extended through analytical continuation. In the phonon-exflation framework, the analog is "wall circumvention" — the Jensen deformation trajectory through the dump point at τ ≈ 0.19 exits a restricted patch of modulus space and continues on the full manifold. The framework's dump-point analog of an extremal horizon (κ = 0) sits in the same geometric role as r = 2M in Schwarzschild, and the double-time-coordinate construction of Lemos-Silva (outgoing and ingoing timelike congruences joined through a single cross-term) offers a direct template for analytical extension across fold hypersurfaces in the spectral-action landscape. This paper substitutes historically for Kruskal (1960), whose single-patch maximal extension is the classical reference the framework invokes for all analytical-continuation arguments.
