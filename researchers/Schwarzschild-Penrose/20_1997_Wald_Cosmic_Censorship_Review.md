## Abstract

We review the status of the weak cosmic censorship conjecture, which asserts, in essence, that all singularities of gravitational collapse are hidden within black holes. Although little progress has been made toward a general proof (or disproof) of this conjecture, there has been some notable recent progress in the study of some examples and special cases related to the conjecture. These results support the view that naked singularities cannot arise generically.

## Key Arguments and Derivations

*Substitute reference for Penrose 1969 cosmic censorship paper (Riv. Nuovo Cim. 1:252, pre-arXiv).*

**Section 1 — Introduction.** Solutions to Einstein's equation with physically reasonable matter must develop singularities under a wide variety of circumstances. If sufficient mass is concentrated in a sufficiently small region, trapped surfaces must form (Schoen and Yau) or future light cone reconvergence occurs (Penrose), and gravitational collapse to a singularity results. The weak cosmic censorship conjecture states that generically the singularities of gravitational collapse are contained in black holes. This is distinct from strong cosmic censorship, which asserts timelike singularities never occur generically.

**Section 2 — Formulation of weak cosmic censorship.** The conjecture is made precise by restricting to spacetimes with asymptotically flat initial data on a surface Σ (topologically the connected sum of R³ with a compact manifold). The black hole region is defined via conformal completion as B = M − I⁻(I⁺), with event horizon H = ∂B. The formal statement (Wald's rendering of Penrose 1969): "Let (h_ab, K_ab, ψ) be nonsingular, asymptotically flat initial data on Σ for a solution to Einstein's equation with suitable matter. Then, generically, the maximal Cauchy evolution of this data is a spacetime (M, g_ab) which is asymptotically flat at future null infinity, with complete I⁺." Wald discusses why "suitable matter" should exclude fluids (shell crossings) but should include Maxwell and Klein-Gordon fields, and why "generic" must be left partly open pending a natural measure on initial data.

**Section 3 — Evidence in favor.** Black hole stability: Vishveshwara (1970) established axial stability of Schwarzschild, Price (1972) demonstrated full stability with decay, Kay-Wald (1987) gave a rigorous boundedness proof, and Whiting (1989) proved mode stability for Kerr. Test-particle arguments for extremal Kerr-Newman black holes show that a black hole cannot swallow enough charge/angular momentum to be "destroyed" (Wald 1974, Semiz 1990), though Brill-Horowitz-Kastor-Traschen (1994) show overcharging is possible for asymptotically de Sitter extremal black holes.

**Section 3.2 — Failed counterexamples.** Penrose (1973) and Gibbons (1972) proposed collapsing null-dust shell counterexamples. If censorship holds, every trapped surface must lie inside a black hole, and the null-energy-condition area theorem applies, giving the string of inequalities A(S) ≤ A₀ ≤ 16π M²_bh ≤ 16π M² (eq. 4). The inequality A(S) ≤ 16π M² can be computed without solving for the exterior; its failure would nearly disprove censorship. Trudinger's (1994) strengthened isoperimetric inequalities enable a general proof that A(S) ≤ 16π M² holds. A parallel argument for time-symmetric data gives A(S_out) ≤ A₀ ≤ 16π M² (eq. 5) — the Penrose inequality, later proved by Huisken-Ilmanen for time-symmetric data.

**Section 4 — The hoop conjecture.** Thorne's hoop conjecture states black holes form when and only when mass M is compacted into a region of circumference C ≤ 4π M in every direction. Wald argues the "only when" portion faces formidable problems (no local notion of gravitational energy, no unambiguous circumference of a world tube). Cylindrical fluid collapse produces singularities without trapped surfaces, but vacuum and Einstein-Maxwell cylindrical collapse produces no singularities at all (Berger-Chruściel-Moncrief 1995). Wald argues the Shapiro-Teukolsky (1991) numerical spindle results do not provide strong evidence that the singularities they found are actually naked.

**Section 5 — The Einstein-Klein-Gordon system.** Under spherical symmetry with a massless Klein-Gordon scalar field φ, the full system is ∇ᵃ∇ₐφ = 0 and G_ab = 8π[∇ₐφ∇_bφ − (1/2)g_ab∇_cφ∇ᶜφ]. Christodoulou's sequence of papers culminated in a true cosmic censorship theorem: classify maximal Cauchy evolutions of initial data (characterized by α = d(rφ)/dr of bounded variation on a future null cone) into (i) no singularities at any finite advanced time, I⁺ complete; (ii) singularity entirely within a black hole; (iii) neither. Christodoulou proved: for any α₀ evolving to a spacetime in case (iii), there exists a continuous function f such that α = α₀ + cf (c ≠ 0) evolves to a spacetime in case (ii). This shows naked singularities are non-generic within initial data of bounded variation — the first cosmic censorship theorem for a nontrivial system.

## Key Results

1. The black hole stability tests (Schwarzschild and Kerr linear stability; Vishveshwara, Price, Kay-Wald, Whiting) have been passed.
2. Extremal Kerr-Newman black holes cannot be "destroyed" by test-particle accretion in asymptotically flat space.
3. The Penrose inequality A(S) ≤ 16π M² for null-dust shell data has been proven in general via Trudinger's isoperimetric results.
4. The Penrose inequality A(S_out) ≤ 16π M² for time-symmetric data (Huisken-Ilmanen) has been proven.
5. Christodoulou's theorem: in the spherically symmetric Einstein-Klein-Gordon system, naked singularities are non-generic in bounded-variation initial data. This is the first rigorous cosmic censorship theorem for a nontrivial matter model.
