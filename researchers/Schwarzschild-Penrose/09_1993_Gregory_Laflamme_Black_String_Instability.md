# BLACK STRINGS AND p-BRANES ARE UNSTABLE

**Author(s):** Ruth Gregory, Raymond Laflamme
**Year:** 1993
**Journal:** arXiv preprint (EFI-93-02); later published in Phys. Rev. Lett. 70 (1993) 2837
**arXiv:** hep-th/9301052
**Relevance:** HIGH

---

## Abstract

We investigate the evolution of small perturbations around black strings and branes which are low energy solutions of string theory. For simplicity we focus attention on the zero charge case and show that there are unstable modes for a range of time frequency and wavelength in the extra 10 - D dimensions. These perturbations can be stabilized if the extra dimensions are compactified to a scale smaller than the minimum wavelength for which instability occurs and thus will not affect large astrophysical black holes in four dimensions. We comment on the implications of this result for the Cosmic Censorship Hypothesis.

---

## Key Arguments and Derivations

### Motivation: four-dimensional stability does not extend to higher-D

The paper begins by reviewing why black holes matter to string theory: in 4D they are classically stable, hiding singularities behind horizons (Cosmic Censorship Hypothesis, Penrose 1969), and quantum-mechanically they radiate thermally (Hawking 1975) and are analogous to thermal systems with entropy proportional to horizon area. String theory is formulated in 10 dimensions, so the appropriate arena for black holes is higher-D; prior work by Myers-Perry (higher-D black holes) and Horowitz-Strominger (non-spherical horizon topologies like S^2 x R^6 or S^3 x R^5) established the existence of black strings and p-branes as low-energy string-theory solutions.

### Heuristic entropy argument

Consider a 5D black string Sch x R. A segment of length L has mass M = ML and entropy proportional to M^2 / L. A 5D black hole has entropy proportional to M^{3/2}. For large L, the string's entropy per unit mass is much lower than that of the hypersphere, so a long string is thermodynamically disfavored compared to a chain of localized black holes. This suggests that long-wavelength perturbations along the string direction should drive a classical instability.

### Perturbation setup

The authors perturb the uncharged Horowitz-Strominger black brane metric (equation 1). Because the uncharged case decouples dilaton and gauge perturbations, they only need gravitational perturbations g_ab -> g_ab + h_ab, imposed in transverse trace-free (de Donder) gauge (equation 3). This yields the Lichnerowicz equation (equation 4) for h_ab.

### Gauge analysis and mode decomposition

Of the (N-2)(N+1)/2 solutions to the N-dim Lichnerowicz equation, (N-1) are pure gauge (generated by divergence-free harmonic vectors), leaving N(N-3)/2 physical degrees of freedom. The perturbation is split in Kaluza-Klein spirit into transverse (scalar), mixed (vector), and parallel-to-brane (tensor) pieces with respect to the D-dim Schwarzschild directions.

### Boundary condition strategy (Whitt's trick)

Prior analytic work (Gregory-Laflamme 1988) could not settle stability because no single non-singular unstable mode existed on the t=0 Schwarzschild slice. Following Whitt's PhD thesis (1988), the authors choose an initial data surface ending on the future event horizon rather than passing through the wormhole neck, avoiding the fixed point of the isometry used for mode decomposition and eliminating the mode-superposition issue. Regularity at the horizon is imposed in Kruskal coordinates; modes are constructed in Schwarzschild coordinates and transformed to check regularity.

### Existence of an unstable s-wave

For an s-wave ansatz (equation 8) with time dependence exp(Omega t) and transverse dependence exp(i mu_i x^i), the D-dim Lichnerowicz equation reduces to a scalar wave equation with an effective mass term (sum mu_i^2) (equation 9). Pure gauge modes satisfy the D-dim Lichnerowicz equation with zero mass, so any solution with mu^2 != 0 is a physical (non-gauge) perturbation. Using the gauge conditions to eliminate all components but H^{tr}, the authors derive a second-order ODE in r (equation 10) with asymptotic behavior exp(-sqrt(Omega^2 + mu^2) r) at infinity and (r - r_+)^{-1 +/- r_+ Omega/(D-3)} at the horizon. Regularity demands the positive root and Omega > 0.

### Numerical confirmation

The authors integrate the ODE from r_1 = 200 toward r_+ (stopping at r_2 = 2.0000002), using adaptive Runge-Kutta with tolerance 10^{-10}, and compute the ratio R = A_-/A_+ of the two horizon asymptotic coefficients. A zero of R signals an unstable mode. Such zeros are found for a range of (Omega, mu) for all 4 <= D <= 9. The equation has a scaling symmetry r_+ -> alpha r_+, Omega -> Omega/alpha, mu -> mu/alpha, so one value of r_+ suffices. An analytic argument rules out instability for Omega > (D-3)/r_+; instability lives at Omega ~ 1/r_+.

### Physical interpretation

The perturbation is not pure gauge. The authors conjecture that the endpoint is fragmentation of the brane into periodic black holes (known solutions exist), which would produce naked singularities and potentially violate Cosmic Censorship. A more conservative reading is that black p-branes simply cannot form from collapse because of the instability. Compactifying transverse dimensions on a scale smaller than 1/M quantizes mu_i, and if the smallest mu exceeds the instability threshold the "black doughnut" is stable -- ensuring ordinary 4D astrophysical black holes are safe in any realistic compactification.

## Key Results

1. Uncharged black strings and p-branes in D <= 9 are classically linearly unstable against long-wavelength s-wave gravitational perturbations, for 4 <= D <= 9.
2. No unstable scalar or vector mode exists; the instability is in the tensor (purely D-dim Schwarzschild) sector.
3. Unstable modes exist for Omega of order 1/r_+ (the horizon scale); for Omega > (D-3)/r_+ no instability exists (analytic bound).
4. The ODE for H^{tr} has a scaling symmetry r_+ -> alpha r_+, Omega -> Omega/alpha, mu -> mu/alpha.
5. Compactifying transverse dimensions below the critical wavelength quantizes mu_i and stabilizes "black doughnuts"; hence the instability does not affect astrophysical 4D black holes.
6. The entropy heuristic (M^2/L vs M^{3/2}) is consistent with the calculation: long strings are disfavored relative to chains of black holes.
7. Instability implies either fragmentation (potentially violating Cosmic Censorship) or that black branes cannot form from collapse.
