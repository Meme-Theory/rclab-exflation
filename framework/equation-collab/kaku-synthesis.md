**Three over-claims to flag (all minor, all about emphasis not substance):**

1. **§6.3 presents the `a(t)` gap monolithically.** It IS the load-bearing gap, but the genre lens (II.2) shows it is (a) shared with SFT and every background-independent theory, and (b) a *named 2-parameter residual* ({Z_norm, V0}), not an absence. **Recommendation:** add one sentence to §6.3 — "this gap is the generic background-independence problem of any one-functional master action (SFT shares it); within the computable matrix-model genre `H²(τ,τ̇)` closes in closed form (S95 W3-2), blocked by the 2-set {Z_norm, V0}." This *strengthens* the section without weakening the caveat.

2. **§1.3a's "structurally stronger than a string field theory" is true but the cited reason is incomplete.** The document says SFT "must *select* its interaction vertex from inequivalent options." Correct — but the deeper genre statement is the **absence of the duality web** (no T-duality, no S-duality, no Hagedorn — my breaks #19/#20/#21). A string theory's interaction *choice* is partly tamed by dualities relating the choices; the substrate has no such web, so its rigidity (`HH² = 0`) is doing *all* the work the string's duality web + vertex choice do together. The document's claim is right; the supporting argument should cite the duality-web absence, not just the vertex choice, or it under-states the rigidity. (Minor.)

3. **§9 frontier #8 promotion language is correctly genericity-qualified, but the "immune to LIV/foam-dispersion" claim (`α_LIV = 0` exactly) deserves a regime tag.** The argument — substrate discreteness is *internal* (`SU(3)` fiber at `M_KK`), emergent `g_M` is the `a₂` moment of a *continuous* heat-kernel trace, so Hossenfelder's no-go does not bite — is genre-correct and I endorse the conclusion. At higher order, the internal `M_KK` tower *could* in principle imprint a suppressed dispersion (the same NNLO Casimir re-entry that gives the genuine EP prediction at NNLO, CF-S96). **Recommendation:** tag `α_LIV = 0` as "exact at LO; higher-order internal-tower imprint is the open NNLO question" — parallel to the EP language already in the document. (Minor; the conclusion stands.)

**Two unstated assumptions:**

1. **The boundary-domination reading (§1.3a) assumes the genesis boundary `τ = 0` is the dominant saddle of `Z = Σ e^{−S}`.** The document argues "an action with no interior stationary point is dominated by its boundary configuration (genesis `τ=0`)" — the Gibbons–Hawking–York boundary-dominated path-integral analog. But a monotone `e^{−S(τ)}` with `dS/dτ > 0` is *largest* at *small* `S`, i.e. small `τ` — so `Z` is dominated by `τ = 0` only if the *measure* `Dτ` does not overwhelm the weight at the *other* boundary (`τ → ∞`, where `S → ∞` so `e^{−S} → 0`, fine) AND the integral converges. This is almost certainly fine, but the document asserts boundary-domination without writing the measure. It is an unstated assumption that the τ-measure is benign. (Harvested as V.7.)

2. **§7.1's joint-improbability argument assumes the spectral-moment layers are statistically independent *as priors*, not just algebraically independent.** The Wronskian (§4.2) proves *algebraic* independence (the functions `a₀, a₂, a₄` are not functionally related). The §7.3 probability-multiplication step requires *statistical* independence of the *observational matches* across layers — a stronger statement. Algebraic independence is necessary but not obviously sufficient: two algebraically independent functions of the *same* `τ` could still produce correlated observational residuals if the data-comparison shares a systematic (e.g., both `a₀` and `a₄` observables borrow the same external `H(t)` via C10). The document is careful to forbid intra-layer multiplication; it should also state that cross-layer multiplication assumes the *external inputs* (notably the C10 `H(t)`) do not induce cross-layer correlation. (Harvested as V.8.)
