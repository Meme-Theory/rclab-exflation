## 2. Where paasch's argument has a precise, provable error (the reason, not the conclusion)

Paasch's §2 Step 1 (the foundation of his entire wall) reads (verbatim, his lines 51–55):

> *"By Peter–Weyl, `H_K = ⊕ V_{(p,q)} ⊗ ℂ^{m(p,q)} ⊗ ℂ^{16}`, where the **left-regular** factor `V_{(p,q)}` carries the `R_X`-action and the **multiplicity** factor `ℂ^{m(p,q)}` is `R_X`-INERT. Therefore `D_K` acts as a SCALAR on `ℂ^{m(p,q)}`."*

And his §3a (his "first and decisive blow," lines 85–89):

> *"any operator acting non-trivially on `ℂ^{m(p,q)}` does NOT commute with `D_K` … UNLESS it is itself `R_X`-covariant — and `ℂ^{m(p,q)}` is `R_X`-inert."*

**This has the two Peter–Weyl legs exactly transposed.** The Peter–Weyl theorem for `L²(G)` is `L²(G) = ⊕_{(p,q)} V_{(p,q)} ⊗ V_{(p,q)}^*`, where the **left-regular** action `L_g` acts on the **first** factor `V_{(p,q)}` and the **right-regular** action `R_g` acts on the **second** factor `V_{(p,q)}^* ≅ ℂ^{m(p,q)}` (the multiplicity leg, `m(p,q) = dim V_{(p,q)}`). So:

- The multiplicity leg `ℂ^{m(p,q)}` is the **right-regular** leg — it carries the **full SU(3)_R action**. It is `R_X`-**ACTIVE**, not `R_X`-inert.
- The commutant of the **left** action `{L_g}'` is generated by the **right** translations `{R_X}`. A **left-invariant** operator therefore *can* act as **any element of `1_V ⊗ M_{m(p,q)}(ℂ)`** — i.e. **non-scalar on the multiplicity (generation) leg** — and remain left-invariant.

**Consequence**: homogeneity (left-invariance) **alone** does NOT force multiplicity-scalar. A left-invariant operator built from right translations `R_X` is generation-non-scalar AND G-invariant (left-invariant). Paasch's §2 conclusion `(R-i) ∧ (R-ii) ⇒ ¬(R-iii)` does **not** follow from homogeneity per se. The Schur argument he invokes ("the only `SU(3)_R`-invariant operators on `V_{(p,q)}` are multiples of the identity") is the commutant of the **right** action — but a generation-resolving operator does **not** need to be right-invariant; it needs to be **left**-invariant, and the left-commutant is the entire right-regular algebra, which is richly non-scalar on the multiplicity leg.

**Why is `D_K` nonetheless multiplicity-scalar?** Not because the leg is inert (it isn't), but because `D_K` is engineered to commute with `R_X` on the multiplicity structure — the homogeneous Dirac operator on `G` is built from the **bi-invariant** (or `H`-invariant, for `G/H`) data, and the inner-fluctuation algebra `A_K` acts via the **left-regular pullback**, so `[D_K, a]` for `a ∈ A_K` lands in the **left**-action algebra `⊕ B(V_{(p,q)}) ⊗ 1` (registry line 21170, verbatim: *"`Ω¹_{D_K}(A_K)` maps into the multiplicity-scalar subalgebra `⊕ B(V_{(p,q)}) ⊗ 1_{m(p,q)}`"*). The multiplicity-scalar-ness is a property of **`A_K`'s left-action**, NOT of the multiplicity leg's intrinsic structure.

**So the correct statement of the wall is**:

> The generation degeneracy is unliftable by any **`A_K`-built** form because **`A_K` acts left-only** and `Ω¹_{D_K}(A_K)` never reaches the right-active multiplicity leg — NOT because the multiplicity leg is `R_X`-inert (it is `R_X`-active) and NOT because "homogeneity forbids any G-invariant non-scalar" (left-invariant `R_X`-operators are G-invariant and non-scalar).

This is exactly §VII.BL's **own** cohomological form (its "NCG home (E2/D1)", registry line 21170): *"`ε_LX` lives in the COMPLEMENT — the multiplicity-acting summand `⊕ 1_{V_{(p,q)}} ⊗ M_{m(p,q)}(ℂ)` that the cochain CANNOT reach."* That complement **is** the right-regular algebra. §VII.BL is precise; paasch's R1 §2/§3a re-derivation of it via "`R_X`-inert" is the imprecise version, and the imprecision matters because it mis-identifies *what* is forbidden (any G-invariant non-scalar) versus what is actually forbidden (any `A_K`-built non-scalar).
