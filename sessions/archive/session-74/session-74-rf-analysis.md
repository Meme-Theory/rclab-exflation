## I. Thesis

The framework's Jensen-resonance substrate pair-production prediction (Tesla-Mack workshop `session-74-tesla-mack-bells-workshop.md`) implies that coherent phased-array emitters operating at the right frequency should produce anti-matter pairs at rates enhanced above the Schwinger baseline by mode-selective substrate catalysis. Direct laboratory tests require either a dedicated physics-test build ($10-100M for academic-scale pilot, $1-3B for full Level-1 physics test, $10-100B for industrial pilot) or retrospective analysis of existing coherent-emitter operational archives.

**What we are looking for** (stated per user direction, the scope of this document):

> "Existing published data or papers reporting anomalies that — in hindsight, through the framework's lens — could be substrate pair production from accidental RF-harmonic Jensen-resonance coupling. Not 'how to access a database.' Signal hidden in existing data because nobody had the right prediction to search against."

The epistemic rule (from earlier this session, now permanent doctrine): **"search not performed ≠ null result"**. A published anomaly is more interesting than an unsearched archive because someone has already noted it; an unsearched archive is more interesting than a thoroughly-searched-with-null-result archive because the prior work didn't know what to look for. When a paper explicitly says "mechanism unexplained," the framework gets first right-of-refusal on whether its prediction applies.

## III. Search Categories (Six, per user specification)

### Category 1: Ionospheric Heaters Reporting Airglow or Emission Anomalies Inconsistent With Electron-Heating Models

**Why this matters**: Ionospheric heaters are the canonical public example of high-power coherent-array RF facilities. HAARP, EISCAT Heating, Arecibo, Sura, and Tromsø have been driving megawatts-to-GW of coherent HF/VHF radiation at the upper atmosphere for 40+ years combined. The standard mechanism for observed airglow is N₂/O₂ excitation by RF-accelerated electrons — any emission inconsistent with that model is an anomaly worth framework-specific scrutiny.

**Lead 1a: HAARP "bright spots" at magnetic zenith — mechanism contested but reproducible**

Published repeatedly since Pedersen's February 2002 experiments. The bright spot is 630 nm (O¹D) and 557.7 nm (O¹S) emission in a small region (≈ 6 inches diameter equivalent) just equatorward of the magnetic zenith, hundreds of Rayleigh intensity, **reproducible on every beam-up-the-magnetic-field shot**. Mechanism proposed: Gurevich self-focusing by small-scale striations. Not all versions of the effect are fully explained. Emission lines are consistent with standard O-excitation, so pair annihilation is NOT a candidate explanation for these specific features — they are noise from the framework's perspective.

**Sources**:

- [HF-induced airglow at magnetic zenith: Thermal and parametric instabilities near electron gyroharmonics — Mishin et al. 2005](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2005GL023864)
- [Artificial Aurora Produced by HAARP — Tulegenov et al. 2019](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1029/2019JA026607)

**Framework diagnostic**: N/A for current observations. The HAARP bright-spot literature does not report 511 keV lines, MeV-scale gamma photons, or annihilation signatures in the emission spectrum. The literature does report specific O and N emission lines consistent with standard plasma physics. Retrospective search of raw photometric data (not the reduced papers) for unexplained broad-spectrum continuum components or unexpected high-energy signatures is the only potentially interesting direction.

**Lead 1b: EISCAT X-mode optical emissions, 19 October 2012 — mechanism explicitly unexplained**

This is the strongest Category 1 lead. Published by Blagoveshchenskaya and colleagues, 2012-2015 series. On 19 October 2012 during an EISCAT Heating experiment, strong artificial optical emissions at 630 nm and 557.7 nm were observed during **X-mode** HF pumping at 7.1 or 6.2 MHz with 430-600 MW ERP. Under X-mode polarization, the standard electron-heating mechanism that explains O-mode airglow does NOT apply — the polarization couples differently to the plasma and does not efficiently transfer energy to electrons at the gyrofrequency.

The published result: **"The mechanism responsible for the emission enhancements during X-mode heating is not known."** The X-mode optical emissions coexisted with HF-enhanced ion and plasma lines and strong artificial field-aligned irregularities throughout the heater pulse. Parametric decay and oscillating two-stream instabilities were NOT quenched by the field-aligned irregularities — meaning the expected damping mechanism for the X-mode pump wave was not operating.

(ResearchGate PDF)](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/N-Blagoveshchenskaya/publication/268989066_Optical_and_Ionospheric_Phenomena_at_EISCAT_under_Continuous_X-mode_HF_Pumping/links/59e8c745a6fdccfe7f95b242/Optical-and-Ionospheric-Phenomena-at-EISCAT-under-Continuous-X-mode-HF-Pumping.pdf)

- [Plasma modifications induced by an X-mode HF heater wave in the high latitude F region of the ionosphere — Blagoveshchenskaya et al. (Academia.edu)](https://www.academia.edu/21497433/Plasma_modifications_induced_by_an_X-mode_HF_heater_wave_in_the_high_latitude_F_region_of_the_ionosphere)
- [Results of Russian experiments dealing with the impact of powerful HF radiowaves on the high-latitude ionosphere using the EISCAT facilities — Blagoveshchenskaya et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1134/S0016793211080160)
- [New capabilities of the upgraded EISCAT high-power HF facility — Rietveld et al. 2016](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/2016RS006093)

**Framework diagnostic**: The X-mode anomaly is a high-priority retrospective-analysis target. Specific questions for framework-informed re-analysis:

1. Does the X-mode emission spectrum contain ANY high-energy (>1 keV) photon component beyond the reported 557.7 / 630 nm optical lines?
2. Were any gamma-ray or X-ray coincidence detectors operating at Tromsø during the X-mode campaigns?
3. Is the time-resolved structure of the X-mode emission consistent with sudden-quench pair production (sharp onset correlated with pulse edges) or with adiabatic plasma heating (smooth buildup)?
4. The X-mode heating campaigns span ~2009-present per the Blagoveshchenskaya series. Integrated exposure time at 430-600 MW ERP is on the order of 10⁴-10⁵ seconds. If framework predictions give any pair production at these parameters, the cumulative count would be substantial.

**Caveat per Mack R2-B sudden-quench analysis**: EISCAT Heating pulses are typically 10s-of-ms to seconds in duration. This is deeply adiabatic relative to the Leggett timescale. The framework's sudden-quench-only prediction would say X-mode heating should NOT produce detectable pair signatures regardless of whether the mechanism is contested. If the X-mode anomaly DOES contain framework-specific signatures, Mack's sudden-quench constraint is wrong or incomplete. This makes the spectrum-sweep search a double-value exercise: it either constrains the adiabatic enhancement factor (if signal appears) or confirms the sudden-quench-only regime (if it does not).

**Lead 1c: Arecibo — anomalous plasma cavity formation, June 2019**

Published 2020 by Levine et al.: "The formation of an anomalous, high-altitude plasma cavity was observed during the 11–15 June 2019 ionospheric heating campaign at Arecibo Observatory. The cavity was induced by interactions of the transmitted 5.125-MHz heater wave with the ionospheric plasma." The language "anomalous" here is the researchers' own, not a framework imposition.

Arecibo's 430 MHz radar has been used for both plasma probing and parametric instability excitation for decades; the 430 MHz scattering is triggered by heater-induced parametric decay instability. **Lead 1d: Sura (Russia) — 5 September 2021 sporadic E-layer 557.7 nm emission, first recording**

Published by the Sura group (Frolov et al.) in 2022. During an ionospheric heating experiment on 5 September 2021, a 557.7 nm emission enhancement at sporadic E-layer heights was recorded at Sura **for the first time**. The mechanism description — "powerful high-frequency radio waves produce energetic electrons in the region where the waves reflect in the F region. These hot or suprathermal electrons collide with atomic oxygen atoms to produce localized regions of metastable O(¹D) and O(¹S)" — is the standard O-mode airglow picture, and the emission spectrum is consistent with it. The novelty is the sporadic E height, not the mechanism.

Sura has been operational since 1981, operates at 4.3-9.5 MHz, and produces 80-260 MW ERP. It has a 44-year operational archive with a rich parameter space.

### Category 4: Strong-Field Laser Experiments Reporting Excess Signal or Unexplained Positron Counts

**Why this matters**: This is the single most directly comparable category to the framework's prediction. Strong-field laser-based pair production IS the mainstream Schwinger-effect experimental program. If there are any excess positron counts above Schwinger-prediction rates in published strong-field laser experiments, they are prima facie framework-relevant.

**Lead 4a: SLAC E-144 (1997) — nonlinear Breit-Wheeler pair production**

First and currently only direct observation of photon-photon pair production via multiphoton process. 46.6 GeV electron beam collided with 1.3 × 10¹⁸ W/cm² optical laser. Detected **106 ± 14 positrons** for the 46.6 GeV case and **22 ± 10 positrons** for the 49.1 GeV case above background. Earlier preliminary result: 15 positrons above background of 14 in 6,000 laser shots.

The published results are consistent with nonlinear Breit-Wheeler pair production via multiphoton absorption (N photons sum to pair threshold energy). No reported anomalous excess beyond this mechanism.

**Sources**:
- [SLAC Experiment E144 Home Page](https://www.slac.stanford.edu/exp/e144/)
- [Studies of nonlinear QED in collisions of 46.6 GeV electrons (PRD main paper)](https://www.slac.stanford.edu/exp/e144/ps/prdmainkk.pdf)
- [SLAC-R-626: Positron Production in Multiphoton Light-by-Light Scattering](https://www.slac.stanford.edu/pubs/slacreports/reports04/slac-r-626.pdf)
- [Review Article: Electron-positron pair production in ultrastrong laser fields](https://www.researching.cn/ArticlePdf/m00084/2017/2/5/MRE2017-05-0225.pdf)
- [Nonlinear Breit-Wheeler pair production using polarized photons from inverse Compton scattering — arXiv 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.08559v1)

**Framework diagnostic**: E-144 positron counts are consistent with Schwinger/Breit-Wheeler predictions. The 106 ± 14 and 22 ± 10 raw counts are small numbers with meaningful uncertainty. Framework-specific re-analysis would ask: (a) is the angular distribution consistent with pure multiphoton Breit-Wheeler, or is there an unexplained isotropic component consistent with substrate-level production, (b) is the energy spectrum of produced positrons narrower or broader than predicted, (c) was there any unexpected time structure (pulse-to-pulse positron count variance inconsistent with Poisson). The original papers did not report such anomalies but the raw data exists at SLAC.

### Category 5: High-Power Acoustic / Ultrasound Experiments With Anomalous Energy Release

**Why this matters**: Sonoluminescence shows that acoustic cavitation in liquids can produce extreme local conditions — temperatures of 20,000 K and plasma cores have been directly measured. Acoustic waves are coherent emitters, and the Taleyarkhan controversy specifically involved claims of neutron emission from ultrasound-driven bubble collapse. If any reproducible acoustic-driven particle emission exists, it is directly framework-relevant as an acoustic analog of the Jensen-resonance mechanism.

**Lead 5a: Flannigan & Suslick — plasma core in single-bubble sonoluminescence, 20,000 K**

Published Nature 2005, JACS 2007, and review 2008. Flannigan and Suslick used concentrated sulfuric acid as the sonoluminescence medium and achieved the most intense SBSL signal on record. The emission spectrum showed atomic Ar emission, molecular SO progressions, and O₂⁺ ionic progressions. Population of O₂⁺ excited states with 18 eV ionization energy + excitation energy indicated a **plasma core temperature of at least 20,000 K**. This was the first direct experimental confirmation of the predicted plasma in collapsing bubbles.

**Sources**:
- [Plasma formation and temperature measurement during single-bubble cavitation — Flannigan & Suslick, Nature 2005](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature03361)
- [Evidence for a Plasma Core during Multibubble Sonoluminescence in Sulfuric Acid — JACS 2007](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/ja070192z)
- [Inside a collapsing bubble: sonoluminescence and the conditions during cavitation — Suslick & Flannigan review 2008](https://suslick.scs.illinois.edu/documents/arpc.08.659.pdf)
- [Plasma Quenching by Air during Single-Bubble Sonoluminescence — Flannigan et al. 2006](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/jp063023u)

**Framework diagnostic**: 20,000 K is well below the threshold for pair production (pair threshold is ~10¹⁰ K for thermal production). **Lead 5b: Taleyarkhan bubble fusion / sonofusion — retracted, scientific misconduct**

Rusi Taleyarkhan and colleagues at Oak Ridge (2002) reported tritium and neutron emission from acoustic cavitation in deuterated acetone, claimed to indicate fusion. The result was not independently reproducible. Oak Ridge's independent replication attempt with better neutron detectors found coincidences consistent with random background. Purdue University's investigation found Taleyarkhan engaged in falsification of independent verification; he was stripped of his professorship.

This null result constrains the framework's prediction at those parameters: any mechanism that would produce detectable particle emission at sonofusion-class conditions is already ruled out.

### Category 6: Lightning / Upper-Atmosphere Energetic Phenomena With Unexplained Gamma or Positron Signatures

**Why this matters per the user's framing**: "The last category is the most interesting and I think most overlooked. TGFs are documented, real, and their mechanism is genuinely contested."

**Lead 6a: Terrestrial Gamma-Ray Flashes (TGFs) — established antimatter production, contested mechanism**

TGFs are extraordinarily bright gamma-ray bursts produced in thunderstorms with durations 0.2-3.5 ms and photon energies up to 20 MeV. They routinely produce electron-positron pairs via atmospheric pair production from the gamma rays, and the positrons have been directly detected by Fermi GBM and RHESSI. NASA's Fermi observed positron signatures from TGFs in 2011 and subsequent years. "Antimatter particles were formed in a terrestrial gamma-ray flash — a brief burst produced inside thunderstorms."

**The mechanism is contested**: The standard explanation is Relativistic Runaway Electron Avalanche (RREA), where large-scale thunderstorm electric fields accelerate seed electrons to relativistic energies and an avalanche cascade produces bremsstrahlung gamma rays. The problem: **"It is not yet understood how lightning produces an enormous number of relativistic electrons in dense atmospheres. The RREA scheme is not enough to explain the number of relativistic electrons involved in a TGF, typically 10¹⁶ to 10¹⁹."** The RREA mechanism falls short by orders of magnitude on the required seed electron population.

Competing mechanisms proposed:
- **Relativistic feedback** (Dwyer): backward-traveling X-rays from bremsstrahlung create positrons via pair production, which seed new avalanches in a self-sustained feedback loop
- **Thermal runaway** (Gurevich-Milikh): thermal electrons heated by cold-runaway mechanism near lightning leaders
- **Photoelectric initiation** (Pasko 2025): photoelectric effect in air explains lightning initiation and TGFs

"In spite of much study over the past 10 years, the underlying mechanism by which lightning is associated with TGFs is still a mystery."

3. Lightning is NOT a coherent phased-array emitter in the conventional sense — but **lightning leaders carry rapidly-oscillating currents at radio frequencies** (LF-HF broadband emission). A leader channel with 10¹⁹-10²¹ A/m² current density oscillating at MHz frequencies IS coherent-emitter-like physics at substrate scale.
4. Thunderstorm electric fields are ~ 10⁵-10⁶ V/m peak — far below Schwinger critical but comparable to the fields needed to drive Bogoliubov coherent oscillations in He-3-like substrate modes if the framework's Jensen resonance accepts low-amplitude long-duration drives.

The "missing seed electrons" problem could potentially be resolved by framework-specific substrate pair production: if the Jensen resonance fires at lightning-leader RF frequencies, it would create electron-positron pairs at rates that RREA cannot explain, and the pairs would seed the subsequent avalanche.

**This is a load-bearing framework-test pathway**. **Lead 6b: Transient Luminous Events (Sprites, Elves, Blue Jets)**

Sprites occur ≈ 50-90 km altitude from positive cloud-to-ground lightning discharges. Blue jets extend upward from cloud tops to ≈ 40 km. Elves are EM pulse-driven emission rings at ≈ 90 km altitude that expand radially outward from the lightning source.
