## 1. Paper metadata

- **Title**: *A direct black-hole mass measurement in a little red dot at high redshift*.
- **First author**: I. Juodžbalis (Kavli/Cavendish Cambridge). 36 co-authors including Maiolino, D'Eugenio, Übler, Ji, Marconcini, Furtak, Bunker, Bromm, Inayoshi, Volonteri, Sijacki, Tacchella, Witstok, Zhang.
- **Sample**: a single object — **Abell 2744–QSO1** (QSO1), a strongly lensed triply-imaged LRD at **z = 7.04** behind A2744. Magnification μ = 6.2, shear factor 3.52 on image A; 1″ in image plane → 1.52 physical kpc (Methods, p. 1022).
- **Headline claim** (Abstract, p. 1017, verbatim): *"the combination of lensing with deep spectroscopic data reveals a rotation curve that is inconsistent with a nuclear star cluster, yet can be well explained by Keplerian rotation around a point mass of 50 million solar masses, consistent with virial black-hole mass estimates. The Keplerian rotation leaves little room for any stellar component in a host galaxy, as we conservatively infer M_BH/M_⋆ > 2 […] Such a 'naked' black hole, together with its near-pristine environment, indicates that this LRD is a massive black-hole seed caught in its earliest accretion phase."*
- **Programme**: BlackTHUNDER NIRSpec G395H IFU, 7.3 hr exposure, R ≈ 3,700 at Hα (Methods, p. 1022).

## 2. Measurements + methodology summary

- **Spectroscopic data**: NIRSpec IFU G395H prism cube, oversampled to 0.02″/spaxel from 0.05″/spaxel. Hα at λ = 5.278 μm falls just outside the nominal F290LP range; the team extrapolates the wavelength solution / flat-field / LSF to recover Hα emission to detector cutoff 5.34 μm. Flux calibration in this extrapolated regime is uncertain — they argue BH mass scales as √L so a factor-of-ten flux error is needed to move M_BH significantly (Methods, p. 1022).
- **Two independent kinematics analyses**:
  - **Spectroastrometry** of narrow Hα: centroids of two velocity channels (+50 / −50 km/s) separated by 24.9 ± 9.4 pc in the source plane; r_spec = 12.5⁺⁴·⁷₋₄·₇ pc. Combined with the spectroastrometric velocity ⟨v sin i⟩ = 51 ± 4 km/s, this gives a spectroastrometric enclosed mass log(M_spec/M_⊙) = 6.7 → 7.2 (uncorrected for inclination; lower limit). Resolved rotation curve binned at 100 pc and 150 pc shows amplitude ~10 km/s (Methods p. 1022; Fig. 2).
  - **MOKA3D** 3D kinematic forward-modelling: convolves PSF, fits 3D velocity field with point-mass, NSC (Schödel+14 Milky Way profile), Plummer sphere, NFW, exponential disk. Best fit is Keplerian point-mass at log(M_BH/M_⊙) = **7.7 ± 0.3** with disk inclination **52° ± 2°** (χ²_R = 1.17). The point mass corresponds to M_BH ≈ 5 × 10⁷ M_⊙ (Methods pp. 1023–1024; Extended Data Table 1).
- **Extended-mass tests**: NSC χ²_R = 2.26 (collapses to R_c = 4 ± 2 pc, but residuals visible in Extended Data Fig. 3); Plummer collapses to R_P = 0⁺³₋₀ pc (effectively a point); NFW collapses to R_s ≈ 10⁻⁴ pc with ρ_0 ≈ 10¹⁴ M_⊙/pc³ (unphysical); exponential disk (10⁷ M_⊙, R_s = 150 pc) gives χ²_R = 22.2 (Extended Data Figs. 6, 7). NSC implied total stellar mass 10⁷·² M_⊙ exceeds independent UV/optical constraint (Supp. §1.8). Upper limit R_c < 0.2 pc places putative NSC > 1 dex more concentrated than densest known clusters in Universe (Extended Data Fig. 2).
- **BH mass estimators compared** (Fig. 3): MOKA3D direct = log(M_BH/M_⊙) 7.7 ± 0.3; spectroastrometry lower limit 6.7–7.2; virial via Hα (Juodžbalis 2024, ref. 15) and via Hβ (Furtak 2024 ref. 13; Ji 2025 ref. 4) all land at log(M_BH/M_⊙) ≈ 7.6 — **consistent with the direct measurement**. The Rusakov electron-scattering "cocoon" scenario (ref. 7) underestimates by ~2 dex; L_bol-with-L/L_Edd=1 underestimates by ~1 dex.
- **Derived L/L_Edd**: L_Edd = 7.6 × 10⁴⁵ erg/s (consistent with M_BH = 5 × 10⁷ M_⊙ via canonical L_Edd = 1.3e38 (M/M_⊙) erg/s — agent-memory math-error check: 5e7 × 1.3e38 = 6.5e45 erg/s, matches to within the bolometric scaling). L/L_Edd ≈ 0.02 from standard Hα–L_bol scaling; could be ≈ 0.01 if Greene et al. 2026 (ref. 36) calibration applied — "near-dormant" (p. 1019).
- **Stellar-mass upper limit**: M_⋆ < 2 × 10⁷ M_⊙ from the dynamical residual (Supp. §1.7); combined with M_BH ≈ 5 × 10⁷ M_⊙ gives **M_BH/M_⋆ > 2**. Three orders of magnitude above the local Reines–Volonteri 2015 relation (Fig. 4).
- **Statistical discipline**: Bayesian information criterion thresholds — ΔBIC ≥ 5 for robust preference, ΔBIC ≥ 10 considered as a stricter alternative; >5σ kinematics preference for point mass over Milky-Way-style NSC (Methods, p. 1022).
- **Model-dependent steps explicitly flagged**:
  - Flux calibration in extrapolated NIRSpec range affects M_BH at √L sensitivity (acknowledged).
  - Inclination 52° ± 2° derived from MOKA3D; cross-checked by non-parametric 3-shell fit (Extended Data Fig. 5; consistent within 1σ).
  - Velocity-field error rescaling: naive RMS underestimates due to beam-smearing covariance; rescaled until best-fit χ²_R = 1 — this is a lower-limit construction on the significance of point-mass preference (Methods, p. 1022). The team flags this as conservative.
  - Lensing model from ref. 13 (Furtak 2024). Lensing-modeling systematics on M_BH propagate as √(magnification), so factor-of-2 lensing error → 0.15 dex BH-mass error.
  - Velocity anisotropies untested (isotropic dispersion assumed); they argue the steepness of the velocity gradient is such that any extended component collapses regardless (Methods, p. 1024).

## 3. LCDM tension assessment

The paper's claims define three distinct tensions, of unequal load-bearing weight:

1. **M_BH/M_⋆ > 2 vs local M_BH–M_⋆ relation (Reines–Volonteri 2015)**: Fig. 4 plots QSO1 at log M_⋆ ≈ 7.3, log M_BH ≈ 7.7 — three orders of magnitude above the local Reines–Volonteri relation locus, and ~1 dex above the JADES JWST AGN locus (Juodžbalis 2026 ref. 25; the next-most-extreme JWST overmassive sample). Locally M_BH/M_⋆ ~ 10⁻³; here > 2. This is the *headline structural anomaly*. Quantitative: > 10³× the local ratio. As a σ-distance against LCDM galaxy-co-evolution predictions, the paper does not compute a formal σ because the local relation is a regression with empirical scatter, not a sharp prediction — but the offset is ~6× the relation's 1σ scatter band (Fig. 4 shading) at the QSO1 stellar mass.

2. **Naked-BH (M_⋆ < 2 × 10⁷ M_⊙ dynamical upper limit)**: The Keplerian rotation curve leaves essentially no room for an extended stellar component within the inner 200 pc. To the team's knowledge, "the most 'naked' massive BH ever found" (p. 1019). This rules out "compact star-forming galaxy" interpretations of QSO1 as a class — relevant because the LRD interpretive triage in MEMORY.md lists "compact star-forming galaxy" as one of three competing frameworks (Paper 23: BIC prefers galaxy-only for 75% — this was the photometric-survey result; QSO1's spectroscopy + lensing falsifies the galaxy-only reading for this object specifically).

3. **BH-primacy / seed-mass tension**: M_BH ≈ 5 × 10⁷ M_⊙ at z = 7.04 (700 Myr after the Big Bang in flat-LCDM with Ω_m = 0.315, H_0 = 67.4) — the paper argues this requires a "heavy seed" channel because (a) the host stellar mass is too small to host such a BH via the conventional accretion-from-Pop-III route on ΛCDM merger trees (Bromm & Yoshida 2011, ref. 30: DCBH M_BH/M_dyn ≤ 0.1 ceiling; QSO1 is > 1 dex above), and (b) the near-pristine metallicity environment (Maiolino 2026, ref. 11) is incompatible with strong UV-radiation requirements of typical DCBH scenarios (Wise+19, ref. 29). They float primordial-BH scenarios (Carr-Kühnel 2020 ref. 32; Zhang-Bromm-Liu 2024 ref. 34) but note QSO1's mass at 5 × 10⁷ M_⊙ is ~1.7 dex above the e⁺e⁻-annihilation-epoch preferred PBH mass scale of 10⁶ M_⊙. Direct-collapse upper limit on M_BH/M_dyn is "approximately 0.1, that is, more than 1 dex lower than our inferred lower limit" (p. 1020, verbatim).

The paper does *not* quote a single-number σ-distance from LCDM; it constrains the surviving model space by showing that (1) no electron-scattering "cocoon" rescaling can defuse the BH-mass estimate (Rusakov scenario disfavored by direct dynamics at the 2-dex level); (2) no extended-mass profile (NSC / Plummer / NFW / disk) fits without collapsing to a point or producing unphysical densities ≥ 10¹⁷ M_⊙/pc³; and (3) no scenario except a true accreting BH of M_BH > 10⁶·⁷⁵ M_⊙ survives the rotation curve.

The headline LCDM tension is the **co-existence of M_BH ≈ 10⁷·⁷ M_⊙ with M_⋆ < 10⁷·³ M_⊙ at z = 7.04**, with no available DCBH growth channel and the wrong PBH mass scale.
