## Round 1 — vdd: Opening Analysis (Kasparov-submersion-faithfulness lens)

### V-a: Sub-question (a) — is χ the shriek map π_! of the submersion?

**Key finding: χ is NOT the shriek map π_!. They act on structurally different objects, at different layers, and there is no commuting square that identifies them. The factorization [D_K] = [D_F] ⊗_{C(M)} [D_M] is a topological K-homology identity that is BLIND to the algebra-block surgery χ performs.** I read sub-question (a) as: χ acts at a layer the Kasparov factorization does not reach. Below I make the distinction precise.

**1. What π_! actually is (Paper 01, 1811.07824, Thm 3.4 / "Factorization of the Fundamental Class").** For the Riemannian submersion π : E = M⁴×SU(3) → M⁴, my Paper 01 result is

  [D_E] = π_! ⊗_{C(M⁴)} [D_{M⁴}]   in KK^d(C(E), ℂ)   (Paper 01, Thm 3.4; file lines 70–76)

Here π_! is the **shriek (Gysin) map in K-homology**: the class of the *vertically elliptic* operator on the total space — concretely, the family of fiber-Dirac operators {D_{SU(3)}} pushed forward along the submersion (Paper 01 §"Tensor Sum on Submersions", lines 47–58; §"The Vertical Ellipticity Condition", lines 39–45). Three facts about π_! are load-bearing here:

  (i) π_! is a class in KK^{dim F}(C(E), C(M⁴)) — it lives in the relative KK-group of the **algebras of functions on the manifolds** C(E) and C(M⁴), NOT in the finite matrix algebra A_F. Its inputs are C₀(M⁴)-module structure and the vertical principal symbol σ_V(D_K) (Paper 01, lines 41–44).

  (ii) On our product geometry the factorization is exactly the regime my paper handles. The registry already records the O'Neill tensors vanish — A = T = 0 EXACT for the Jensen product metric (atlas-07 A7 |A_coset|² decomposition; my own S61 memory: "O'Neill A=T=0 EXACT"). With A = T = 0 the tensor sum D_E ⊗ 1 + 1 ⊗ D_M is regular and the bounded transforms compose to the Kasparov product with **no curvature obstruction and no cross-terms** (Paper 01 lines 52–58, the "regular vertically elliptic" hypothesis is automatically met). This is the *clean* case — which is exactly why the factorization is robust and why it CANNOT be doing the work χ does.

  (iii) The fiber content π_! carries is selected by **Peter-Weyl + the vertical symbol**, and what couples to the base is the color-SINGLET, trivial-(0,0) content. My S61/A7 record: of 136,480 modes, exactly 16 (the trivial (0,0) irrep) couple to 4D — π_! integrates over the SU(3) fiber and the surviving base-class is the SU(3)-invariant part. Critically, **π_! retains the full SU(3) fiber as the thing it integrates over**; it does not delete any irrep block from the algebra. The fiber's color structure is *integrated*, not *amputated*.

**2. What χ actually is.** The registry's own statement (§VII.W-3.SUBSTRATE, registry line 17060; §VII.W-3.ALGEBRAIC, line 16987) is:

  χ : A_F = ℂ ⊕ ℍ ⊕ M_3(ℂ) → M_2(ℂ),  unital *-homomorphism, χ(M_3(ℂ)) = 0.

Three facts about χ:

  (a) χ is a map of **finite matrix *-algebras** — its domain is A_F (the internal algebra), its codomain is M_2(ℂ) (the BdG/Nambu sector). It is NOT a map between C(E) and C(M⁴). It does not live in any KK-group of manifold function algebras; it lives at the finite-spectral-triple algebra level. This is a different category from π_!.

  (b) The registry explicitly identifies χ as the **inheritance morphism** ι used in the 3He-B falsifier protocol: §VII.W-3.LAB element 3 calls it "inheritance morphism χ : ℂ ⊕ ℍ ⊕ M_3(ℂ) → M_2(ℂ) (M_3(ℂ) → 0; BDI → BdG sector child)" (registry line 17107); §VII.AZ.OP-PROJ names it "Cross-Morphism M₃(ℂ)-Kernel Universality" with `ker(ι_*) = M_3(ℂ)` (registry line 287). It is a *parent→child algebra restriction* onto a Bogoliubov–de Gennes sub-sector, accompanied by a chirality-grading reversal BDI → DIII. That is a finite-algebra surgery, not a fiber integration.

  (c) χ kills the M_3(ℂ) **block of A_F**. π_! integrates over the SU(3) **group manifold**. These are not the same M₃/SU(3): A_F's M_3(ℂ) is the color matrix algebra acting on the C¹⁶ internal spinor (the finite part D_F); SU(3) the fiber is the *base manifold of the submersion* whose function algebra is C(SU(3)). χ deletes one Wedderburn summand of the finite algebra; π_! pushes the Dirac family forward along the whole group. The objects are categorically distinct, so "χ = π_!" is a type error.

**3. The decisive test: does the factorization COMMUTE with χ-killing M₃(ℂ)?** No — and there is no square in which it could. To even ask whether [D_K] = [D_F] ⊗_{C(M)} [D_M] "commutes with χ" one would need χ to be an endomorphism of one of the objects appearing in the product. It is not: the product lives in KK^d(C(E), ℂ) built from C(M⁴) and the vertical symbol; χ acts on the *coefficient* algebra A_F that rides inside the finite part D_F. The orientability axiom (axiom 5) that χ is invoked to rescue is a **Hochschild-cycle condition on (A_F, D_F)** — the registry pins it: "orientability via Hochschild cycle of degree d" (registry line 6389), and the C-6 gate reports axiom 5 fails at 4.000 for D_total (atlas-04 N3; registry line 2302). The Hochschild orientation cycle is a property of the *finite spectral triple's algebra*, evaluated INSIDE the fiber, at a single point of M⁴. The Kasparov factorization is a statement about the *total K-homology class over the base*. **The shriek map π_! never sees the Hochschild orientability cocycle of A_F**: π_! is built from the vertical symbol and the C₀(M)-module structure (Paper 01 §"Vertical Ellipticity"), both of which are insensitive to which Wedderburn summands of A_F are present, because they depend on D_F only through its *symbol/index data on the fiber*, not through its algebra's Hochschild orientation class.

**Structural implication for N3/N7.** χ acts strictly *below* the layer the Kasparov factorization reaches. The factorization is an identity of the topological (Layer-1, K-homology) content of the product geometry — and per my canonical boundary (memory: "Kasparov product gives TOPOLOGY ... NOT ANALYSIS"), it certifies the index-theoretic decomposition, not the satisfaction of the orientability *axiom* by the finite algebra. χ is an extrinsic operation on A_F that is not in the image of, nor licensed by, the submersion factorization. **On reading (a) I conclude: χ is an extrinsic finite-algebra projection, not the intrinsic shriek map of the M⁴×SU(3) submersion.** This is the strong prior I bring into (c) — but I flag for connes (V-Q) that there is a way to rescue faithfulness if χ can be exhibited as the shriek map of a *second, internal* submersion (SU(3) → SU(3)/SU(2)-type coset) rather than the M⁴×SU(3) → M⁴ one; if so, the "amputation" reading weakens to a "fiber-of-a-fiber integration" reading.

### V-b: Sub-question (b) — does pre-orientability amputation of M₃(ℂ) invalidate D_total?

**Key finding: the cited NCG precedent (Connes Papers 23/24) resolves an order-one failure at 4.000 by KEEPING the failing algebra and ADDING quadratic inner fluctuations — the exact OPPOSITE of χ-killing the failing block. The full D_total is, on the bare-axiom register, BROKEN — and the framework's own register says so (N3 bare = BROKEN, registry line 101, atlas-04).** Here is the case.

**1. The number 4.000 is the SAME obstruction Connes-Chamseddine-van Suijlekom hit — and their fix is not amputation.** Paper 24 (1304.8050) states the SM algebra is exactly ours, A_SM = ℂ ⊕ ℍ ⊕ M_3(ℂ) (Paper 24 lines 30–37), and Paper 23 (1304.7583) records that the order-one violation "by 4.000 (as measured by [[D,a],b]/||D||)" is what triggers the extension (Paper 23 line 86). Our C-6 gate's axiom-5 failure at 4.000 = 2² on the (ℍ,ℍ)/M₃(ℂ) sector (atlas-04 N3; registry C-6 line 2302; order-one norm 3.117 at C-3 line 2298) is the SAME structural obstruction. But the canonical resolution in the literature my project cites is **inner fluctuations WITHOUT the first-order condition**: D_A = D + A_lin + A_quad, where the quadratic term A_quad = Σ c_ij [D,a_i][D,a_j] is precisely the content that the order-one failure forces into existence (Paper 23 lines 56–64; Paper 24 lines 54–64). In that treatment the M₃(ℂ)/color sector is RETAINED in full — it is the *quark color* that the whole construction is built to carry (Paper 24 line 37: "M_3(ℂ) is the strong interaction SU(3)_C"). Connes et al. relax an *axiom*; the framework deletes a *sector*. These are not the same operation, and only the former preserves the physical content.

**2. χ-killing removes color from precisely the axiom that tests the full operator.** The orientability axiom (axiom 5) is the Hochschild-cycle condition on (A_F, D_F) (registry line 6389). The sector that fails it is the M₃(ℂ) block (atlas-04 N3 line 101: "The S28 axiom-5 fail at value=4.000 is the M_3(ℂ) sector specifically"). χ then sets χ(M₃(ℂ)) = 0 BEFORE the axiom is checked on the image. So the axiom "passes" on the M₂(ℂ) image not because the geometry satisfies orientability but because **the only sector that violated it has been projected out of the test**. This is structurally a tautology: deleting the failing summand and then re-checking the remaining summands is guaranteed to pass — the §VII.W-3.ALGEBRAIC theorem itself says exactly this (registry Step 8, line 17003: "A satisfies A0 ∧ M2 iff every block is (i) n=1 division OR (ii) n≥2 **χ-killed**"). Clause (ii) is "the block is χ-killed," i.e. the rescue is *definitionally* "make the bad block invisible." The theorem is a correct algebraic statement about χ-kernels; it is not a demonstration that D_total satisfies orientability.

**3. Inner-fluctuation relaxation does NOT close it for D_total either — and shifts KO-dim.** The spawn-context numerics (and the framework register) are explicit: inner fluctuations without order-one reduce the defect from 4.000 to 2.100 but do NOT close it, AND KO-dimension shifts 6 → 2. A relaxation route that drops KO-dim to 2 breaks G4 — so the framework cannot take the Connes-23 relaxation route without sacrificing the very KO-dimension that gives it the SM spectrum and the Lorentzian Type-D structure. The framework is thus pinned between two horns: (H1) keep all sectors + relax order-one → KO-dim collapses 6→2 (G4 broken); (H2) χ-kill M₃(ℂ) → KO-dim=6 preserved but color deleted from orientability (N3 bare broken, rescued only "WHEN M₃ χ-killing is admitted"). The framework takes H2. The phrase "WHEN M₃ χ-killing is admitted" (atlas-04 N7, registry §VII.W-3.SUBSTRATE) is doing exactly the load-bearing work the spawn flags as LBA-5.

**4. So is the spectral-triple claim invalidated?** I separate two claims that the register itself separates:

  - **CLAIM-FULL: "D_total is a spectral triple (all 7 axioms)."** This is FALSE at the bare-axiom level and the register says so: N3 bare = "BROKEN at bare-axiom level" (registry line 101); C-6 = "6/7 NCG axioms (axiom 5 fails at 4.000)" (line 2302). The Kasparov factorization (V-a) does not repair this, because it is a topological identity that never touches the Hochschild orientation cocycle of A_F. **So CLAIM-FULL is not rescued by anything in my domain.**

  - **CLAIM-χ: "The χ-image triple (A_F/ker χ = ℂ⊕ℍ → M₂(ℂ)) is a spectral triple."** This is TRUE and is what §VII.W-3 actually proves. But it is a triple on a *color-free* algebra. It is a legitimate object — it is, in fact, exactly the kind of object my Paper 01 + Paper 02 machinery would call the *fiber-restricted child* — but it is NOT D_total, and the substrate's physical content (SU(3)_color, the entire reason G2 = SU(3) was chosen) lives in the kernel that was deleted.

**Structural implication for N3/N7.** The honest reading is that N7 ("A_F = ℂ⊕ℍ⊕M₃(ℂ) is the unique algebra realizing the Wedderburn-Frobenius rescue") is a theorem about *which algebra survives a chosen surgery*, conditional on admitting that surgery. Whether that amputation is *intrinsic* (a genuine fiber-over-base operation, faithful-rescue) or *extrinsic* (a convenient deletion of the failing sector, amputation) is decided by (a) — and on (a) I find it extrinsic.
